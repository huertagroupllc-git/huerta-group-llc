## Purpose and scope

Defines how educational assets become authoritative while preserving
peer-room authority and configurable future governance — approval,
revision, supersession, methodology fidelity, IP authority, brand
authority, development boundaries, and Corporate Headquarters
boundaries. The Foundation source (§12) is authoritative; this record
summarizes it and is corrected by it wherever they differ.

## Approved content

**Educational authority (§12.1).** An educational asset becomes
authoritative only when all eight conditions hold: scope defined;
authority basis identified; required source dependencies established;
required reviews completed; approval by the appropriate configurable
approving authority; version and status recorded; disclosure level
established; and material limitations and unresolved matters recorded.

**Program approval (§12.2).** A program is approved only through
explicit approval of its educational substance for a defined scope —
and program approval does not automatically authorize delivery,
enrollment, public disclosure, commercial sale, data collection,
credential issuance, or technical deployment.

**Configurable authority (§12.3).** The repository supports
configurable authority roles and approval relationships; it never
hard-codes named individuals, permanent officeholders, or irreversible
assumptions about future constitutional authority
(validator-enforced).

**Peer-room preservation (§§12.4–12.6).** Educational adaptations of
methodology must reference the governing methodology record, identify
the applicable version, preserve substantive fidelity, record
adaptation scope, identify approved simplification or
contextualization, and be reviewed when methodology changes materially
— Education never independently amends methodology (§12.4).
Educational assets support relationships to IP records governing
ownership status, classification, disclosure, permitted use,
adaptation authority, licensing constraints, lifecycle, and
preservation — Education never determines ownership or disclosure
rights independently (§12.5). Public educational names, claims,
descriptions, visual identity, messaging, and representations comply
with Branding authority; educational approval is never brand approval
(§12.6).

**Platform & Systems Development boundary (§12.7).** Development may
implement approved record structures, define schemas, establish
validation, build traceability and versioning, create reversible
foundations, recommend refinements, and identify constraints. It may
not invent educational doctrine, create unapproved competency
standards, define assessment validity, approve programs, authorize
credentials, determine educational quality, convert public copy into
internal requirements, or infer offerings from technical capability.

**Corporate Headquarters boundary (§12.8).** Corporate Headquarters
governs legally, financially, contractually, administratively, and
commercially operative matters (budgets, contracts, instructor
relationships, enrollment operations, scheduling, payments, customer
support, compliance, operational records, resource allocation,
commercial delivery); it may provide evidence and recommend changes
but never independently redefines educational doctrine, competency
architecture, curriculum standards, assessment architecture,
certification philosophy, or quality standards.

**Provisional work and revision (§§12.9–12.10).** Provisional work
stays clearly distinguishable from approved authority and is never
treated as authoritative requirements or public offerings. Revisions
preserve prior version, decision history, effective scope, approval
record, supersession relationship, material change summary, dependency
effects, and historical accessibility — approved records are never
silently overwritten.

## Limitations and open questions

- Summary record: Foundation §12 is authoritative and corrects this
  record wherever they differ.
- Final constitutional allocation and delegation of educational
  approval authority remain unresolved; the configurable interim
  representation stands until that architecture is approved (§20.1).
