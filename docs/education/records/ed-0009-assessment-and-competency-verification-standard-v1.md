# ed-0009 — Assessment and Competency Verification Standard v1

**Classification: Approved Methodology, Governance, and Policy**

- **Record:** `ed-0009` · v1.0 · `assessment_governance_standard`
- **Authority basis:** Education & Workforce Development Foundation v1,
  §9 (per §18.9)
- **Source:** [`education-workforce-development-foundation-v1.md`](../education-workforce-development-foundation-v1.md)
- **Disclosure:** internal
- **Status:** approved internal standard · governance record

## Purpose and scope

Defines assessment distinctions, evidence concepts, human-judgment
requirements, and AI boundaries — knowledge checks, practice,
formative and summative assessment, competency assessment, projects,
capstones, organizational outcomes, and credential evaluation. The
Foundation source (§9) is authoritative; this record summarizes it and
is corrected by it wherever they differ.

## Approved content

**Assessment categories (§§9.1–9.9):** `knowledge_check`
(low-consequence examination of understanding — not, by default, a
competency assessment) · `practice_activity` (development and
reinforcement, not final evaluation) · `formative_assessment`
(feedback during learning; informs but does not necessarily determine
final competency) · `summative_assessment` (end-of-unit evaluation of
specified outcomes; never automatically competency verification) ·
`competency_assessment` (whether evidence supports demonstration of a
defined competency under relevant conditions) ·
`practical_project_review` (applied work evaluated against approved
criteria) · `capstone_evaluation` (culminating integrated work) ·
`organizational_outcome_verification` (organizational change evidence;
must account for factors beyond learner activity and never attribute
causality without sufficient evidence) · `credential_evaluation`
(whether approved credential requirements are satisfied — **no
credential-evaluation process is authorized for operation**).

**Evidence (§9.10–9.11).** Seventeen permitted future evidence
categories, from written explanations and structured analysis through
portfolios, organizational artifacts, multi-source evidence, and
verified prior evidence. Attendance, content access, time spent, video
viewing, or submission alone never establishes competency (§9.11).

**Human judgment (§9.12).** Human review is preserved where evidence
is ambiguous, context materially affects interpretation, judgment is
itself part of the competency, the decision affects credentialing or
has meaningful professional or organizational consequences, automated
evaluation lacks reliability or explainability, or the governing
standard requires it.

**Technology and AI assistance (§9.13).** Technology or AI may assist
with delivering assessments, organizing evidence, low-consequence
practice feedback, gap identification, rubric support, inconsistency
detection, evaluator-note drafting, review routing, and accessibility
— and may never independently make high-consequence competency,
credential, certification, disciplinary, or eligibility decisions
without separately approved authority, evidence standards, controls,
review, and appeal processes.

**Intentionally unapproved (§9.14):** scoring systems, passing
thresholds, grade scales, examination rules, attempt limits, time
limits, proctoring, automated grading authority, appeals, reassessment
policy, evidence-retention periods, accommodation procedures,
academic-integrity procedures, and credential-evaluation standards.

## Limitations and open questions

- Summary record: Foundation §9 is authoritative and corrects this
  record wherever they differ.
- Assessment categories are governance vocabulary only: no assessment
  instrument, question, rubric, scoring system, or assessment
  operation exists or is authorized.

## Technical implementation

Approved as governance metadata only; the schema's assessment-category
vocabulary creates no operational assessment instrument, and no
automated high-consequence decision is authorized.
