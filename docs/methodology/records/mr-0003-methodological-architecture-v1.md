## Purpose and scope

Establishes the approved layers and high-level domains through which
Huerta Group methodology is organized, institution-wide — deliberately
without asserting a complete proprietary consulting framework.

## Approved architecture (§2.1) — the five authoritative layers

1. **Layer 1 — Methodological Doctrine.** The enduring beliefs and
   governing propositions shaping Huerta Group's approach to
   organizational improvement (nine approved doctrine propositions,
   from "organizational performance is the objective; technology is an
   enabling instrument" through "learning from engagements should
   strengthen institutional methodology without automatically
   converting client-specific material into institutional doctrine" —
   full approved list in the source).
2. **Layer 2 — Methodological Domains.** The major bodies of
   methodological work: organizational understanding, assessment,
   analysis, improvement design, implementation, verification,
   sustainment, knowledge transfer, continuous improvement, and
   client-engagement application — approved as the present high-level
   architecture, **not** as a proprietary numbered consulting system.
3. **Layer 3 — Methodological Asset Classes.** The governed forms in
   which methodology may be expressed: principles, methods, frameworks,
   models, standards, assessments, diagnostic instruments, decision
   systems, verification procedures, implementation guidance, and
   supporting tools, templates, and workflows.
4. **Layer 4 — Application Adaptations.** Approved methodology may be
   adapted for client delivery, internal organizational improvement,
   education and workforce development, publishing, software
   implementation, managed services, and other approved institutional
   applications — an adaptation never acquires authority to redefine
   the underlying methodology.
5. **Layer 5 — Evidence and Evolution.** The methodology must preserve
   authority basis, development history, evidence from use, validation
   status, known limitations, open questions, changes, supersession
   history, and related institutional dependencies.

## Incomplete areas (§2.2) — must not be inferred from this foundation

A complete proprietary consulting lifecycle · detailed discovery
procedures · standardized organizational assessment instruments ·
formal maturity models · detailed process-mapping methodology ·
detailed diagnostic decision trees · standard implementation playbooks ·
formal verification thresholds · formal sustainment assessment
instruments · consulting-quality scoring models · industry-specific
methodological adaptations · consultant certification requirements ·
formal methodology-validation protocols · complete metadata standards
beyond the minimum approved in the foundation.

## Limitations and open questions

- Incomplete by design: the areas above are declared unresolved by the
  approved source; filling any of them requires new approved
  methodology substance.
- No completeness claim: this framework must never be presented as a
  finished proprietary consulting framework.
