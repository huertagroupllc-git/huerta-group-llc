## Purpose and scope

Establishes the approved principle-level progression for high-level
organizational-improvement work. **This progression is authoritative as
a governing structure. It is not approved as a branded, numbered,
proprietary phase model**, and it must not be converted into automation
or a mandatory software workflow.

## Approved progression (§3, with its approved substance)

- **Understand the organization (§3.1).** Establish sufficient
  understanding of the organization, its purpose, stakeholders,
  environment, systems, operating structure, constraints, priorities,
  and relevant history. The organization should not be reduced to the
  presenting issue alone.
- **Define the problem or intended outcome (§3.2).** Clarify what
  condition requires attention, why it matters, what evidence supports
  its existence, who or what is affected, what outcome is sought, what
  boundaries apply, and what assumptions remain unresolved. A requested
  solution should not be accepted automatically as an accurate
  definition of the underlying problem.
- **Examine systems and evidence (§3.3).** Study the relevant
  processes, roles, decisions, information flows, technologies,
  controls, measures, incentives, dependencies, risks, constraints,
  historical evidence, and stakeholder experience. Analysis should
  distinguish causes, contributing conditions, symptoms, and
  consequences.
- **Design improvement (§3.4).** Develop an improvement design
  connecting the defined problem, evidence, organizational context,
  proposed changes, intended outcomes, risks, dependencies, and
  measures of success — across organizational, operational,
  technological, informational, managerial, or capability-related
  changes.
- **Support implementation (§3.5).** Translate approved improvement
  design into executable action with appropriate sequencing, ownership,
  resources, controls, documentation, communication, training, and
  adoption support. Methodology does not presume that recommendation
  delivery constitutes implementation.
- **Verify results (§3.6).** Examine whether approved changes were
  implemented, the implemented system operates as intended, required
  participants understand and use it, controls and measures function,
  evidence supports the intended improvement, and whether unintended
  consequences or unresolved deficiencies remain. Verification
  requirements should be proportionate to the importance, risk, and
  reversibility of the change.
- **Build sustainable capability (§3.7).** Ensure the organization has
  sufficient ownership, knowledge, documentation, measurement,
  governance, operational capability, and review mechanisms to maintain
  and improve the system. Huerta Group should avoid creating
  unnecessary dependence where sustainable client capability is an
  approved objective.
- **Preserve learning (§3.8).** Capture what was learned, what was
  validated, what failed or remained uncertain, what was
  client-specific, what may improve institutional methodology, what
  requires further testing, what should remain confidential, and what
  may be proposed for institutional approval.

## Limitations and open questions

- Not a finalized proprietary phase model: no phases, gates,
  deliverables, thresholds, or scoring exist or are implied; whether
  the progression becomes a formal consulting lifecycle is a reserved
  later decision (§10.2).
- Related future methods (assessment, implementation, verification,
  sustainment, continuous improvement) remain undeveloped and blocked
  (§10.1, §10.8).

## Technical implementation

Approved only as a non-numbered, non-automated reference framework.
