- **Status:** approved internal standard · governance record · subject
  to future constitutional refinement

## Purpose and scope

Defines admission requirements and the boundaries among substantive
authority, IP authority, Institutional Knowledge Architecture,
Education, Branding, Platform & Systems Development, and Corporate
Headquarters — governing all institutional IP governance and repository
decisions.

## Approved content

**Admission.** A substantive asset enters institutional IP governance
only through: an identifiable source · approved substantive authority ·
provenance · an institutional-value rationale · admission review · a
recorded admission decision (authority, basis, date) · a governance
status · conservative known ownership and rights status · disclosure ·
stewardship. **Repository presence does not create admission, and
admission does not create legal ownership.**

**Authority boundaries.**
- *Substantive authority* stays with the source room (e.g., Methodology
  for methodological substance); IP admission never transfers or
  overrides it.
- *IP authority* governs admission, classification, stewardship,
  disclosure-governance principles, versioning, preservation, and
  relationships.
- *Institutional Knowledge Architecture* retains authority over
  enterprise-wide metadata, taxonomy, identifiers, and versioning
  conventions, which this repository inherits.
- *Education* and *Branding* retain authority over instructional and
  brand substance respectively.
- *Platform & Systems Development* holds technical implementation and
  custody — which is not institutional stewardship and confers no
  authority.
- *Corporate Headquarters* governs all legally, financially,
  administratively, and commercially operative matters (registrations,
  agreements, chain-of-title records, legally operative ownership
  determinations, licensing, commercialization, enforcement,
  third-party and joint-venture arrangements, budgets). The repository
  records evidence references to those matters; it never performs or
  concludes them.

**Configurable authority.** Approval authority is stored as data and
must remain configurable: no immutable constitutional office, no named
individual embedded as permanent authority, no technical role treated
as institutional authority. All authority representations are
subordinate to the Constitution and later-approved decision-rights
architecture.
