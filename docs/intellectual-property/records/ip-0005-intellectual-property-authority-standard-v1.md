# ip-0005 — Intellectual Property Authority Standard v1

**Classification: Approved Methodology, Governance, and Policy**

- **Record:** `ip-0005` · v1.0 · `standard`
- **Authority basis:** Intellectual Property Foundation v1, §5
- **Source:** [`intellectual-property-foundation-v1.md`](../intellectual-property-foundation-v1.md)
- **Disclosure:** internal
- **Status:** approved internal standard · governance record · subject
  to future constitutional refinement

## Purpose and scope

Defines admission requirements and the boundaries among substantive
authority, IP authority, Institutional Knowledge Architecture,
Education, Branding, Platform & Systems Development, and Corporate
Headquarters — governing all institutional IP governance and repository
decisions. The Foundation source (§5) is authoritative; this record
summarizes it and is corrected by it wherever they differ.

## Approved content

**Admission (§5.1).** An asset may become institutionally governed IP
only through an authorized admission decision documenting all fourteen
required elements: asset identity · asset class · source authority ·
provenance · institutional value rationale · current lifecycle state ·
maturity · disclosure classification · known ownership or rights
status · material third-party or client dependencies · stewardship
assignment · admission decision and basis · decision authority ·
effective version and date. **Repository presence does not create
admission, and admission does not create legal ownership.**

**Configurable approval authority (§5.2).** Foundation v1 hard-codes no
permanent constitutional office and no named individual. Authority
assignments must be configurable by institutional domain, asset class,
lifecycle action, governance action, disclosure action, readiness
decision, version approval, supersession, and archival action — and the
authority configuration must remain traceable over time, so each
decision reflects the authority structure effective when it occurred.

**Substantive authority (§5.3).** The room responsible for the
underlying subject matter retains substantive authority: Methodology
for methodological substance, Education for educational architecture,
Branding for brand substance, Platform & Systems Development for
technical implementation, Publishing for publication strategy and
execution where established, Operating Systems & Process Architecture
for institutional operating-system and process substance, and other
authoritative rooms for their domains. Intellectual Property may not
substantively redefine an asset merely by admitting it.

**IP authority (§5.4)** governs admission criteria, institutional IP
status, asset classification for IP purposes, IP lifecycle standards,
stewardship doctrine, disclosure-governance principles, versioning and
preservation requirements, relationships among proprietary assets and
adaptations, and institutional treatment of qualifying assets.

**Institutional Knowledge Architecture (§5.5)** governs the
authoritative-source hierarchy, knowledge metadata, cross-room
relationships, decision traceability, knowledge propagation,
supersession architecture, and institutional-memory standards; the IP
repository must remain compatible with it rather than creating a
competing authority system.

**Education (§5.6)** may request educational adaptations, develop
instruction from approved assets, define curriculum and learning
requirements, and establish educational validation and assessment
standards — never redefine underlying IP ownership, provenance, or
governance status. **Branding (§5.7)** may create and govern brand
assets, brand-use standards, approved identity expression, and public
brand consistency — never independently determine legal trademark
ownership or broader IP doctrine.

**Platform & Systems Development (§5.8)** may implement approved
repository capabilities, identify technical candidate assets, record
technical provenance, maintain technical versions and dependencies,
implement authorized IP assets in software, and identify technical
constraints and risks. It may not independently determine institutional
IP admission, legal ownership, licensing rights, disclosure authority,
commercial readiness, legal protectability, or valuation. Technical
custody is not institutional stewardship and confers no authority.

**Corporate Headquarters (§5.9)** governs the legally, financially,
administratively, and commercially operative treatment of IP: it may
maintain registrations, execute agreements, maintain legal
chain-of-title records, determine legally operative ownership with
authorized legal guidance, administer licensing, conduct
commercialization, protect and enforce rights, manage third-party and
joint-venture arrangements, and allocate budgets and resources.
Institutional IP governance and legally operative ownership must remain
separately identifiable; where they differ, the discrepancy must be
documented and referred to the appropriate institutional and legal
authorities.

## Limitations and open questions

- Final constitutional allocation and delegation of IP approval
  authority remain unresolved; this standard must be revised when that
  architecture is approved, and the configurable interim authority
  representation stands until then.

## Technical implementation

Approved provided authority remains configurable — validator-enforced
(`approvingAuthority.configurable` must be `true`).
