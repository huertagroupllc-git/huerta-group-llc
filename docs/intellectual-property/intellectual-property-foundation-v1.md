## Status

Approved as the minimum authoritative institutional foundation for the
initial technical implementation of the Institutional Intellectual
Property Repository.

This foundation establishes only the governance substance required for
repository architecture, metadata, classification, lifecycle
management, authority, disclosure, relationships, traceability,

## 1. Definition and Scope

### 1.1 Institutional Intellectual Property

Huerta Group institutional intellectual property is:

A validated knowledge-based, creative, technical, operational, or
expressive asset that Huerta Group has formally admitted into
institutional intellectual-property governance for enduring
stewardship, controlled development, preservation, reuse, and
institutional value creation.

Institutional intellectual property is not created merely because an
idea, document, method, or technical artifact exists.

An asset becomes institutionally governed intellectual property only
when:

1. The asset is sufficiently identifiable.
2. Its origin and authoritative source are documented.
3. It possesses or is reasonably expected to possess durable
   institutional value.
4. It is appropriate for preservation, controlled reuse, refinement, or
   future adaptation.
5. It has passed the applicable institutional review.
6. It has been formally admitted into intellectual-property governance.
7. Its ownership status, disclosure status, and maturity are recorded
   without unsupported legal conclusions.

Institutional intellectual-property status is an internal governance
designation. It does not, by itself, establish copyright ownership,
patentability, trademark rights, trade-secret status, contractual
ownership, or any other legally operative conclusion.

### 1.2 Relationship to Institutional Knowledge

Institutional knowledge is the broader body of authoritative knowledge
used by Huerta Group.

Institutional intellectual property is a governed subset of that
broader body.

Institutional Knowledge Architecture governs:

- How knowledge is classified.
- Where authority resides.
- How knowledge is related.
- How versions and decisions are traced.
- How dependencies and supersession are recorded.
- How institutional memory is preserved.

Intellectual Property governs:

- Which qualifying knowledge assets receive proprietary institutional
  status.
- How those assets are admitted, classified, stewarded, refined,
  preserved, and governed throughout their IP lifecycle.

Not all institutional knowledge is intellectual property.

Institutional knowledge may remain authoritative without being
proprietary, reusable, protectable, commercializable, or appropriate
for admission into the IP repository.

### 1.3 Relationship to Methodology

Methodology defines how Huerta Group understands, assesses, analyzes,
improves, implements, verifies, sustains, and transfers organizational
capability.

A methodology, method, framework, assessment, model, or tool may become
institutional intellectual property when it satisfies the admission
criteria established by this foundation.

Methodology remains authoritative for the substantive doctrine and
practice of the method.

Intellectual Property remains authoritative for its status and
stewardship as a proprietary institutional asset.

Admission into the IP repository does not transfer substantive
methodological authority to the Intellectual Property room.

### 1.4 Relationship to Software

Software may embody, implement, operationalize, or itself constitute
institutional intellectual property.

The following may qualify for admission:

- Approved software concepts.
- Original software components.
- System designs.
- Technical architectures.
- Algorithms or structured logic.
- Data models.
- Interfaces.
- Automation patterns.
- AI configurations.
- Technical documentation.
- Software implementations of approved frameworks or methods.

Platform & Systems Development governs technical implementation and
software engineering.

Intellectual Property governs whether a qualifying software-related
asset receives institutional IP status and how that asset is
classified, preserved, versioned, and stewarded.

Software being developed internally does not automatically make every
source file, configuration, dependency, or implementation decision an
institutional IP asset.

### 1.5 Relationship to Publications

Books, articles, research papers, white papers, reports, presentation
systems, and other reusable publications may become institutional
intellectual property.

Publishing or public release does not itself determine institutional
ownership, proprietary status, or repository admission.

Publication strategy, editorial governance, distribution, and audience
strategy remain outside this foundation.

### 1.6 Relationship to Education

Courses, learning materials, exercises, case studies, assessment
instruments, instructional resources, and credential-related content
may qualify as educational intellectual property.

Education governs:

- Curriculum architecture.
- Instructional design.
- Learning pathways.
- Learner assessment.
- Educational delivery.
- Capability development.

Intellectual Property governs the proprietary status, classification,
provenance, preservation, and stewardship of qualifying underlying
assets.

An educational adaptation must remain traceable to its originating
asset where applicable.

### 1.7 Relationship to Branding

Brand assets may qualify as institutional intellectual property when
formally admitted.

Examples may include:

- Approved marks.
- Identity systems.
- Original visual assets.
- Messaging systems.
- Naming systems.
- Brand standards.
- Proprietary presentation systems.

Branding remains authoritative for brand meaning, expression, identity,
and usage standards.

Intellectual Property governs the admitted asset's institutional IP
status and stewardship.

Legal trademark status and registration remain Corporate Headquarters
responsibilities with appropriate legal guidance.

### 1.8 Relationship to Technology

Technology is a means of implementing, storing, controlling,
delivering, or extending institutional intellectual property.

Technology does not independently determine:

- Whether an asset qualifies as institutional IP.
- Institutional ownership doctrine.
- Disclosure status.
- Legal protectability.
- Commercial rights.

Technology may identify candidate assets and provide evidence
concerning origin, development, dependencies, version history, or
technical distinctiveness.

### 1.9 Relationship to Services

Service delivery may generate:

- New institutional knowledge.
- Refinements to existing assets.
- Candidate frameworks or tools.
- Client-derived insights.
- Reusable implementation patterns.
- Validation evidence.

A service, engagement, or deliverable is not automatically
institutional intellectual property.

Client obligations, contractual restrictions, confidentiality,
contribution sources, and preexisting materials must be respected and
recorded.

### 1.10 Relationship to Operational Knowledge

Internal operating knowledge may qualify as institutional intellectual
property when it is:

- Sufficiently defined.
- Reusable beyond a single event.
- Institutionally valuable.
- Appropriate for controlled preservation or replication.
- Formally admitted.

Routine instructions, transient decisions, ordinary records,
administrative information, and standard business practices do not
automatically qualify.

### 1.11 Relationship to Research

Research may support, validate, challenge, or generate institutional
intellectual property.

Research records may themselves qualify as intellectual property when
they contain original institutional analysis, models, datasets,
findings, or structured knowledge that meet admission criteria.

External facts, public information, third-party research, and common
knowledge do not become Huerta Group intellectual property merely
because they are collected or cited.

### 1.12 Future Institutional Capabilities

Future capabilities may produce new asset classes or require expanded
governance.

This foundation permits controlled extension but does not preapprove
future asset classes, ownership doctrines, commercialization models, or
legal strategies.

### 1.13 What Institutional Intellectual Property Is Not

Institutional intellectual property is not automatically:

- Every idea discussed within Huerta Group.
- Every institutional decision.
- Every internal document.
- Every methodological concept.
- Every software file or configuration.
- Every client deliverable.
- Every public statement.
- Every educational resource.
- Every brand expression.
- Every operational procedure.
- Every piece of research.
- Every dataset.
- Every employee, contractor, contributor, partner, or client
  contribution.
- Every asset legally owned by Huerta Group LLC.
- Every asset Huerta Group would like to own.
- Every asset that may have commercial value.
- Every confidential record.
- Every copyrighted work.
- Every trade secret.
- Every potentially patentable invention.

Institutional IP status must be affirmatively established through
governance.

## 2. Intellectual-Property Architecture

The approved Intellectual Property Foundation v1 architecture consists
of the following governance dimensions.

### 2.1 Governing Doctrine

Defines:

- Institutional meaning of intellectual property.
- Admission principles.
- Stewardship principles.
- Institutional boundaries.
- Relationship to legal and commercial administration.

### 2.2 Asset Classification

Defines:

- The asset's approved class.
- Its substantive source authority.
- Its functional role.
- Whether the classification is approved or provisional.

### 2.3 Lifecycle

Defines:

- Development state.
- Review state.
- Approval state.
- Validation state.
- Supersession.
- Archival treatment.

### 2.4 Institutional Governance Status

Defines whether an asset:

- Has not been evaluated.
- Is a candidate for admission.
- Is under IP review.
- Has been admitted into institutional IP governance.
- Has been rejected for admission.
- Has been removed from active governance without erasing its history.

Institutional governance status is distinct from asset maturity.

### 2.5 Ownership and Rights Status

Records known ownership and rights information without allowing the
repository to make unsupported legal determinations.

The repository may record:

- Claimed institutional interest.
- Known creator or contributor.
- Known agreement references.
- Known third-party interests.
- Ownership status pending review.
- Legally confirmed ownership where authoritative documentation exists.

### 2.6 Stewardship

Identifies:

- The authoritative substantive source.
- The institutional IP steward.
- Responsible reviewing authority.
- Technical custodian where applicable.
- Corporate or legal administrator where applicable.

### 2.7 Disclosure

Defines authorized visibility and handling without treating disclosure
as equivalent to ownership.

### 2.8 Relationships

Records relationships among:

- Source knowledge.
- Methodological records.
- Parent and derivative assets.
- Adaptations.
- Implementations.
- Publications.
- Educational uses.
- Software implementations.
- Brand implementations.
- Client-derived inputs.
- Superseding and superseded versions.

### 2.9 Evidence and Provenance

Records:

- Origin.
- Authorship.
- Contributors.
- Development history.
- Source materials.
- Review decisions.
- Approval evidence.
- Validation evidence.
- Agreement or registration references where available.

### 2.10 Preservation

Requires:

- Durable record retention.
- Version history.
- Supersession traceability.
- Historical-status retention.
- Preservation of rejected, provisional, superseded, transferred, and
  retired records where institutionally significant.

### 2.11 Intentionally Incomplete Architectural Areas

The following remain incomplete:

- Legal ownership doctrine.
- Chain-of-title procedures.
- Licensing architecture.
- Commercialization architecture.
- Portfolio valuation.
- Royalty administration.

## 3. Approved Asset Classes

Asset class identifies the primary form of the governed asset. It does
not determine ownership, disclosure, maturity, legal protection, or
commercial value.

### 3.1 Framework

A structured system of concepts, principles, stages, dimensions, or
relationships used to organize understanding or guide action.

### 3.2 Model

A defined representation of a system, condition, relationship, maturity
state, decision structure, or expected behavior.

### 3.3 Assessment

A structured instrument or system used to evaluate, diagnose, compare,
score, or interpret a defined subject.

This class includes qualifying diagnostics, maturity assessments,
readiness assessments, scorecards, and evaluation instruments.

### 3.4 Methodology Asset

An admitted proprietary asset derived from or used within Huerta Group
methodology.

This may include a complete methodology, bounded method, method
component, protocol, analytical sequence, or implementation approach.

Its substantive authority remains with Methodology.

### 3.5 Standard

An approved requirement, rule, specification, convention, or minimum
condition intended to produce consistency or quality.

### 3.6 Template

A reusable structure intended to guide the creation, documentation,
analysis, communication, or execution of recurring work.

### 3.7 Workflow

A reusable sequence of activities, decisions, handoffs, controls, or
system actions.

A routine operational workflow is not automatically institutional IP.
Admission requires identifiable institutional value and formal
approval.

### 3.8 Tool

A reusable instrument that supports analysis, decision-making,
implementation, verification, documentation, or execution.

A tool may be manual, digital, analytical, procedural, or hybrid.

### 3.9 Software Concept

An approved original concept for a software capability, system,
product, technical mechanism, or platform that is sufficiently defined
for institutional preservation but may not yet have an implementation.

### 3.10 Software Component

An identifiable technical asset implemented in software, including
qualifying code, modules, schemas, algorithms, interfaces, automations,
configurations, or system components.

Open-source, third-party, commodity, and dependency components must
remain distinguishable from original Huerta Group components.

### 3.11 Publication

A reusable written, visual, audio, or multimedia work developed for
internal or external publication.

This includes qualifying books, articles, white papers, reports,
research publications, and presentation works.

### 3.12 Educational Asset

A reusable proprietary asset created or adapted for learning,
instruction, practice, evaluation, credentialing, or knowledge
transfer.

### 3.13 Dataset

A structured collection of data that has institutional value and has
been approved for IP governance.

Dataset admission does not establish rights in underlying third-party,
personal, client, licensed, regulated, or public data.

### 3.14 Research Asset

Original institutional research materials, analyses, findings,
experimental records, or structured research outputs admitted for
long-term stewardship.

### 3.15 Brand Asset

An approved proprietary identity, visual, verbal, naming, messaging, or
presentation asset whose substantive authority remains with Branding.

### 3.16 Institutional Know-How

A defined body of practical institutional knowledge, judgment,
technique, or capability that is not adequately represented by another
approved class and has been formally admitted as a proprietary asset.

This class must not be used as a catch-all for undocumented experience.

### 3.17 Composite Asset

A governed asset composed of multiple approved asset classes that must
be managed as a coherent institutional unit.

Composite classification should be used only when one primary class
cannot adequately represent the asset.

### 3.18 Provisional Asset Classes

The following are not approved as independent authoritative classes
under Foundation v1:

- Patentable invention.
- Trade secret.
- Trademark.
- Certification mark.
- Data product.
- AI agent.
- Algorithm as an independent class.
- Licensing package.
- Commercial product.
- Product family.
- Intellectual-property portfolio.
- Royalty-bearing asset.
- Joint-venture asset.
- Acquired intellectual property.

These terms may be recorded as provisional descriptors or future
governance candidates, but they must not be treated as approved classes
until separately reviewed.

## 4. Lifecycle Standard

### 4.1 Development Lifecycle States

**Working Concept**

An identifiable early concept that may have future institutional value
but is not sufficiently developed for formal review.

A working concept is not approved institutional intellectual property.

**Draft**

An asset under active development with sufficient definition to be
recorded and versioned.

A draft is not approved for institutional reliance unless separately
authorized for limited use.

**Under Review**

An asset undergoing substantive, governance, authority, disclosure,
provenance, or admission review.

Review may identify required revisions without rejecting the asset.

**Approved**

An asset accepted by the appropriate substantive authority for its
stated purpose and approved version.

Approval does not necessarily mean that the asset:

- Has been validated in practice.
- Has been admitted into proprietary governance.
- Is legally owned by Huerta Group.
- Is publicly releasable.
- Is commercially ready.

**Validated**

An approved asset supported by documented evidence of effective use,
testing, operational performance, client application, technical
verification, research support, or another authorized validation
method.

Validation must identify its scope. Validation in one context does not
establish universal validity.

**Superseded**

An asset replaced by a later approved asset or version for active
institutional use.

Superseded assets remain preserved and traceable.

**Archived**

An asset removed from active development or use but retained for
historical, evidentiary, legal, research, or institutional-memory
purposes.

Archival status does not erase prior approval, validation, authorship,
ownership evidence, or relationships.

### 4.2 Institutional IP Governance Status

The repository must track institutional IP governance separately from
lifecycle state.

Approved governance statuses are:

- Not evaluated.
- Candidate.
- Under IP review.
- Admitted.
- Admission declined.
- Governance withdrawn.
- Status pending external determination.

Admitted means that the asset is formally governed as Huerta Group
institutional intellectual property.

Admission is not a legal ownership determination.

### 4.3 Readiness Approvals

Readiness identifies what an asset is authorized to support.

Approved readiness dimensions may include:

- Internal reference ready.
- Internal operational use ready.
- Method use ready.
- Educational adaptation ready.
- Publication review ready.
- Public release ready.
- Software implementation ready.
- Client-use ready.
- Licensing review ready.

A readiness designation must not be inferred from maturity, approval,
validation, disclosure, or IP admission.

### 4.4 Maturity

Maturity describes the degree of development, evidence, stability, and
institutional adoption.

Foundation v1 approves the following maturity levels:

- Emerging.
- Defined.
- Tested.
- Established.
- Mature.

Maturity is separate from lifecycle state, institutional IP governance
status, disclosure, and readiness.

For example, an asset may be:

- Approved.
- Admitted as institutional IP.
- Internally confidential.
- Software-implementation ready.
- Tested in maturity.

These are distinct attributes.

## 5. Authority Standard

### 5.1 Admission into Institutional IP Governance

An asset may become institutionally governed intellectual property only
through an authorized admission decision.

The decision must document:

- Asset identity.
- Asset class.
- Source authority.
- Provenance.
- Institutional value rationale.
- Current lifecycle state.
- Maturity.
- Disclosure classification.
- Known ownership or rights status.
- Material third-party or client dependencies.
- Stewardship assignment.
- Admission decision and basis.
- Decision authority.
- Effective version and date.

### 5.2 Approval Authority

Foundation v1 does not hard-code permanent constitutional offices or
named individuals.

The repository must support configurable authority assignments based
upon:

- Institutional domain.
- Asset class.
- Lifecycle action.
- Governance action.
- Disclosure action.
- Readiness decision.
- Version approval.
- Supersession.
- Archival action.

The authority configuration must remain traceable over time so that
each decision reflects the authority structure effective when the
decision occurred.

### 5.3 Substantive Authority

The room or institutional authority responsible for the underlying
subject matter retains substantive authority.

Examples:

- Methodology governs methodological substance.
- Education governs educational architecture.
- Branding governs brand substance.
- Platform & Systems Development governs technical implementation.
- Publishing governs publication strategy and execution where
  established.
- Operating Systems & Process Architecture governs institutional
  operating-system and process substance.
- Other authoritative rooms govern their respective domains.

Intellectual Property may not substantively redefine an asset merely by
admitting it into IP governance.

### 5.4 Intellectual-Property Authority

The Intellectual Property room governs:

- Admission criteria.
- Institutional IP status.
- Asset classification for IP purposes.
- IP lifecycle standards.
- Stewardship doctrine.
- Disclosure-governance principles.
- Versioning and preservation requirements.
- Relationships among proprietary assets and adaptations.
- Institutional treatment of qualifying assets.

### 5.5 Institutional Knowledge Architecture Authority

Institutional Knowledge Architecture governs:

- Authoritative-source hierarchy.
- Knowledge metadata.
- Cross-room relationships.
- Decision traceability.
- Knowledge propagation.
- Supersession architecture.
- Institutional-memory standards.

The IP repository must remain compatible with institutional knowledge
architecture rather than creating a competing authority system.

### 5.6 Education Authority

Education may:

- Request educational adaptations.
- Develop instruction from approved assets.
- Define curriculum and learning requirements.
- Establish educational validation and assessment standards.

Education may not redefine underlying IP ownership, provenance, or
institutional governance status.

### 5.7 Branding Authority

Branding may:

- Create and govern brand assets.
- Establish brand-use standards.
- Determine approved identity expression.
- Govern public brand consistency.

Branding may not independently determine legal trademark ownership or
the broader institutional IP doctrine.

### 5.8 Platform & Systems Development Authority

Platform & Systems Development may:

- Implement approved repository capabilities.
- Identify technical candidate assets.
- Record technical provenance.
- Maintain technical versions and dependencies.
- Implement authorized IP assets in software.
- Identify technical constraints and risks.

It may not independently determine:

- Institutional IP admission.
- Legal ownership.
- Licensing rights.
- Disclosure authority.
- Commercial readiness.
- Legal protectability.
- Valuation.

### 5.9 Corporate Headquarters Authority

Corporate Headquarters governs the legally, financially,
administratively, and commercially operative treatment of intellectual
property.

It may:

- Maintain registrations.
- Execute agreements.
- Maintain legal chain-of-title records.
- Determine legally operative ownership with authorized legal guidance.
- Administer licensing.
- Conduct commercialization.
- Protect and enforce rights.
- Manage third-party and joint-venture arrangements.
- Allocate budgets and resources.

Institutional IP governance and legally operative ownership must remain
separately identifiable.

Where they differ, the discrepancy must be documented and referred to
the appropriate institutional and legal authorities.

## 6. Disclosure Standard

### 6.1 Disclosure Is Separate from Ownership

Disclosure classification defines who may access, use, receive, or
publish an asset.

It does not determine:

- Who created the asset.
- Who owns the asset.
- Whether the asset is legally protectable.
- Whether rights have been assigned.
- Whether the asset may be licensed.
- Whether the asset has commercial value.

A public asset may remain institutionally governed intellectual
property.

A confidential asset may be owned by a third party.

A proprietary asset may have unresolved legal ownership.

### 6.2 Approved Disclosure Classifications

**Public**

Approved for unrestricted public access or distribution in the
authorized form.

Public status does not authorize alteration, relicensing, commercial
exploitation, or use beyond applicable rights.

**Internal**

Restricted to authorized Huerta Group institutional or corporate use.

Internal material is not necessarily confidential, proprietary, or
legally protected.

**Confidential**

Restricted because unauthorized disclosure may create institutional,
contractual, legal, commercial, technical, client, security, or
reputational harm.

The reason and handling authority should be recorded.

**Proprietary**

Identified as containing Huerta Group proprietary institutional
substance or an institutional proprietary interest.

Proprietary classification is an institutional governance status and
does not itself establish a legally enforceable right.

**Restricted**

Access limited to explicitly authorized persons, roles, systems,
engagements, or purposes.

Restricted may be used where internal or confidential classifications
are insufficiently precise.

### 6.3 Origin and Adaptation Designations

The following are not disclosure levels. They are relationship or
use-context designations that must be recorded separately.

**Client-Derived**

The asset contains knowledge, evidence, structure, data, experience, or
adaptation derived from client work.

Client-derived assets require documented review of:

- Contractual restrictions.
- Confidentiality.
- Personal or regulated data.
- Client ownership.
- Preexisting materials.
- Generalization or de-identification.
- Authorized reuse.

Client-derived status does not establish Huerta Group ownership.

**Educational Adaptation**

An asset adapted for instruction, learning, practice, or assessment.

It must remain traceable to the underlying source asset where
applicable.

**Publishing Adaptation**

An asset adapted for publication or external communication.

Public release authority must be separately recorded.

**Software Implementation**

A technical implementation of another institutional asset.

The implementation and the underlying asset must remain distinguishable
and related.

### 6.4 Multiple Classifications

An asset may carry:

- One primary disclosure classification.
- Additional handling restrictions.
- Origin designations.
- Adaptation designations.
- Jurisdictional or contractual restrictions where documented.

The repository must not collapse these into a single field or
inference.
