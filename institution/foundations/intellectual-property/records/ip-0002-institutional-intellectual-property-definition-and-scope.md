## Purpose and scope

Defines institutional intellectual property and establishes its
admission criteria, scope, relationships, and exclusions — governing
all Huerta Group institutional intellectual-property identification and
governance. The Foundation source (§§1.1–1.13) is authoritative; this
record summarizes it and is corrected by it wherever they differ.

## Approved content

**Definition (§1.1).** Huerta Group institutional intellectual property
is a validated knowledge-based, creative, technical, operational, or
expressive asset that Huerta Group has formally admitted into
institutional intellectual-property governance for enduring
stewardship, controlled development, preservation, reuse, and
institutional value creation. It is not created merely because an idea,
document, method, or technical artifact exists. An asset becomes
institutionally governed IP only when all seven §1.1 conditions hold:
it is sufficiently identifiable; its origin and authoritative source
are documented; it possesses or is reasonably expected to possess
durable institutional value; it is appropriate for preservation,
controlled reuse, refinement, or future adaptation; it has passed the
applicable institutional review; it has been formally admitted into
intellectual-property governance; and its ownership status, disclosure
status, and maturity are recorded without unsupported legal
conclusions. Institutional IP status is an internal governance
designation — it does not, by itself, establish copyright ownership,
patentability, trademark rights, trade-secret status, contractual
ownership, or any other legally operative conclusion.

**Relationships (§§1.2–1.12).** Institutional IP is a governed subset
of institutional knowledge; not all institutional knowledge is IP, and
knowledge may remain authoritative without being proprietary (§1.2).
For every neighboring domain, substantive authority stays home and IP
governs status and stewardship of admitted assets: methodology assets
may qualify while Methodology retains substantive doctrine, and
admission never transfers methodological authority (§1.3); software
concepts, components, designs, architectures, algorithms, data models,
interfaces, automation patterns, AI configurations, documentation, and
implementations may qualify, but internal development does not make
every source file or configuration an IP asset (§1.4); publications may
qualify, and publishing or public release does not itself determine
ownership, proprietary status, or admission (§1.5); educational assets
may qualify, with adaptations traceable to their originating asset
where applicable (§1.6); brand assets may qualify, with legal trademark
status and registration remaining Corporate Headquarters
responsibilities (§1.7); technology is a means of implementing and
evidencing IP and never independently determines qualification,
ownership doctrine, disclosure, protectability, or commercial rights
(§1.8); service delivery may generate candidate assets but no
engagement or deliverable is automatically IP, and client obligations,
contractual restrictions, confidentiality, contribution sources, and
preexisting materials must be respected and recorded (§1.9);
operational knowledge may qualify when defined, reusable,
institutionally valuable, appropriate for controlled preservation, and
formally admitted — routine instructions and ordinary records do not
(§1.10); research records may qualify when they contain original
institutional analysis, models, datasets, findings, or structured
knowledge, while external facts, public information, third-party
research, and common knowledge never become Huerta Group IP merely by
collection or citation (§1.11); and future capabilities permit
controlled extension without preapproving future asset classes,
ownership doctrines, commercialization models, or legal strategies
(§1.12).

**Exclusions (§1.13).** Institutional IP is not automatically any of
the twenty listed categories — from every idea, decision, document,
or client deliverable through every asset legally owned by Huerta Group
LLC, every confidential record, every copyrighted work, every trade
secret, and every potentially patentable invention. Institutional IP
status must be affirmatively established through governance.

Repository presence and IP admission remain separate, and admission,
when granted, is an institutional governance act that creates no legal
ownership or protection.
