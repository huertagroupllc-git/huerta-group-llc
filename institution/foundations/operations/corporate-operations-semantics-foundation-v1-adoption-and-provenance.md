# Corporate Operations Semantics Foundation v1 — Adoption, Scope, and Provenance

**Classification: Approved Methodology, Governance, and Policy.**

- **Governing authority:** Founder Office.
- **Source authority:** Corporate Operations & Command Management.
- **Adoption instrument:** Founder Office adoption recorded at `fd-0012`;
  preservation of the complete source recorded at `fd-0013`.
- **Adopted:** 2026-08-18. **Preserved:** 2026-08-18.
- **Disclosure:** repository-internal.

This record accompanies the adopted Foundation. It states what was
adopted, what the Foundation is and is not, and how the preserved text is
verifiable. **It is not the Foundation and does not speak for it.** Where
this record and the Foundation could be read differently, the Foundation
governs.

## 1. What was adopted

The Founder Office adopted the **Corporate Operations Semantics Foundation
— Version 1** as **new prospective Huerta Group LLC operational
doctrine**.

| Artifact | Canonical path | Sections |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Corporate Operations Semantics Foundation — Version 1 | `CORP-OPS-SEM-FND-V1.md` | §1–§34, complete |

**Authority begins prospectively upon institutional adoption** (Foundation
§3; `fd-0012`). The Foundation is authority going forward. It is not
retroactive authority over prior operational representations.

## 2. Explicit non-OSPA status — permanent

The Foundation is **not**:

- recovered OSPA;
- reconstructed OSPA;
- implied missing OSPA;
- restoration of unavailable OSPA sections;
- evidence of what unavailable OSPA sections previously contained.

Foundation §3 requires that "the institutional record should preserve that
distinction permanently." This record is where that distinction is kept.

**The prohibition runs in both directions.** Unavailable OSPA
(S05–S08, S11–S15) must never be inferred *from* the Foundation, and the
Foundation must never be represented *as* OSPA. The Foundation's digest is
deliberately **not** recorded in this directory's `SHA256SUMS`, which is
the integrity manifest for the six admitted OSPA sections only — mixing
them would blur exactly the boundary the Foundation requires be kept.

## 3. Relationship to surviving OSPA

Surviving OSPA S01–S04, S09 and S10 remain recovered canonical authority,
limited strictly to their surviving text. The Foundation **supplements**
that authority prospectively, and only where Generation One requires
operational concepts surviving OSPA does not itself establish
(Foundation §2).

Foundation §2 fixes the precedence: surviving OSPA "controls wherever it
already governs"; the Foundation "should be interpreted consistently with
surviving OSPA"; it "does not supersede existing corporate governance";
and concepts already governed elsewhere "should retain their established
meanings."

The Foundation expressly preserves two surviving OSPA principles it names:
**conceptual integrity** and **multidimensional state**.

## 5. What the Foundation establishes

Operational **semantics only**. Foundation §25: "The Foundation
establishes operational semantics only. It does not alter authority."

It defines Operational Instance and Operational Record (§5), Operational
State (§6), Material Change (§7), the State/Change/Attention relationship
(§8), Founder Attention and its entry and exit (§9–§10), Dependency (§11),
Blocker (§12–§13), Handoff and its completion (§14–§15), Deadline (§16),
Renewal (§17), Follow-Up (§18) and their distinction (§19), Current-State
Ownership (§20), Operational Representation Responsibility (§21–§23),
lifecycle and transition principles (§24), authority boundaries (§25),
Generation One applicability (§26), the relationship and venture
boundaries (§27–§28), non-goals (§29), worked examples (§30), the ten
semantic tests (§31), the questions it left to the Founder Office (§32),
its maturity assessment (§33), and its recommendation (§34).

## 6. What the Foundation does not authorize

Foundation §29 and §34 are explicit. It is not technical architecture,
database design, schema design, API design, UI design, event architecture,
notification architecture, workflow-engine design, automation doctrine,
AI-agent doctrine, a prioritization algorithm, an enterprise ontology, or
a complete future Command Center semantic model. It is not project,
task, CRM, venture-lifecycle, accounting, compliance, governance,
document-management, or records-retention doctrine.

"No technical architecture, repository modification, schema, API,
interface, integration, automation, or implementation work is authorized
by this proposal itself" (§34). Adoption made the doctrine authoritative;
it did not make it an implementation authorization.

## 7. Founder Office resolution of §32

The Foundation left five policy questions to the Founder Office (§32).
All five were determined and are recorded verbatim at `fd-0012`:
attention-entry authority (§32.1), attention exit after Founder action
(§32.2), materiality disputes (§32.3), representation freshness (§32.4),
and attention priority assignment (§32.5).

Those determinations are **Founder Office policy**, recorded in the
determinations register. They are not amendments to the Foundation, and
the Foundation's text was not altered to absorb them.

Stages 1–3 are historical and are not rewritten to imply the file was
available sooner.
