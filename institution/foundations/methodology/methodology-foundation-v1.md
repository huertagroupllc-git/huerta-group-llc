## 1. Definition and Scope

### 1.1 Definition

Institutional methodology is Huerta Group's governed body of doctrine,
principles, methods, standards, frameworks, models, assessment
approaches, implementation guidance, verification practices, sustainment
practices, and related methodological assets used to understand, assess,
improve, implement, verify, and sustain organizational systems.

Institutional methodology governs how Huerta Group approaches
organizational improvement.

It provides a consistent basis for reasoning, analysis, decision-making,
service delivery, implementation support, knowledge transfer, and
continuous improvement across industries, technologies, consultants,
engagements, and future institutional applications.

Methodology is an enduring institutional authority. It remains
authoritative independently of any particular client engagement, service
line, employee, technology platform, educational program, publication,
or software product.

### 1.2 Approved Scope

Huerta Group methodology currently includes the following approved scope
domains:

**Organizational understanding**

Establishing a disciplined understanding of an organization's purpose,
environment, structure, stakeholders, operations, capabilities,
constraints, dependencies, and current condition.

**Assessment**

Evaluating organizational systems, processes, capabilities, controls,
technology, information, performance, and maturity against defined
criteria or intended outcomes.

**Analysis**

Examining evidence, relationships, causes, dependencies, risks,
patterns, gaps, and operating conditions to distinguish symptoms from
underlying organizational problems.

**Improvement**

Designing changes intended to improve organizational performance,
coherence, reliability, efficiency, scalability, control, adaptability,
or sustainability.

**Implementation**

Translating approved improvements into coordinated organizational
action, including system changes, process changes, role changes,
technology changes, documentation, training, and adoption support.

**Verification**

Determining whether an implemented change was completed as intended,
operates as designed, and produces sufficient evidence of the expected
result.

**Sustainment**

Establishing the ownership, capabilities, controls, documentation,
measurement, maintenance, and review practices required to preserve
improvements over time.

**Knowledge transfer**

Transferring the understanding, operating knowledge, decision logic,
standards, and capabilities necessary for relevant participants to apply
and sustain an approved organizational system.

**Continuous improvement**

Using operational evidence, implementation experience, measured
performance, institutional learning, and changing conditions to refine
organizational systems and Huerta Group's methodology.

**Client engagements**

Applying approved methodology within engagements while adapting its use
to the client's context without compromising methodological doctrine,
quality standards, evidence requirements, or institutional integrity.

### 1.3 What Methodology Is Not

Methodology is not:

- Huerta Group's institutional Vision.
- Huerta Group's Constitution.
- A current strategic plan.
- Corporate Headquarters operations.
- A specific client's operating model.
- A catalogue of consulting services.
- A collection of software features.
- A technology architecture.
- A training curriculum.
- A publication.
- A marketing description.
- A set of templates without governing reasoning.
- A collection of consultant preferences.
- An undocumented customary practice.
- A client-specific recommendation automatically generalized into
  institutional doctrine.
- Intellectual property merely because it was created internally.
- Public methodology merely because a general principle has been
  disclosed publicly.

Technology, AI, automation, software, documentation, dashboards,
assessments, and analytical tools may support the methodology. They do
not independently constitute the methodology and do not determine its
objectives.

## 2. Methodological Architecture

### 2.1 Approved Architecture

The institutional methodology is presently organized into five
authoritative layers.

**Layer 1 — Methodological Doctrine**

The enduring beliefs and governing propositions that shape Huerta
Group's approach to organizational improvement.

Approved doctrine presently includes:

- Organizational performance is the objective; technology is an enabling
  instrument.
- Organizational problems should be understood systemically rather than
  treated only as isolated symptoms.
- Recommendations should be grounded in evidence, organizational
  context, and intended outcomes.
- Improvement should be implementable, measurable, supportable, and
  sustainable.
- Methodology should remain systematic, repeatable, adaptable, scalable,
  and subject to continuous improvement.
- Deliverables are not equivalent to outcomes.
- Implementation without verification is incomplete.
- Improvement without sustainment creates avoidable institutional
  regression.
- Learning from engagements should strengthen institutional methodology
  without automatically converting client-specific material into
  institutional doctrine.

**Layer 2 — Methodological Domains**

The major bodies of methodological work:

- Organizational understanding.
- Assessment.
- Analysis.
- Improvement design.
- Implementation.
- Verification.
- Sustainment.
- Knowledge transfer.
- Continuous improvement.
- Client-engagement application.

These domains are approved as the present high-level architecture.

They are not approved as a proprietary numbered consulting system.

**Layer 3 — Methodological Asset Classes**

The governed forms in which methodology may be expressed:

- Principles.
- Methods.
- Frameworks.
- Models.
- Standards.
- Assessments.
- Diagnostic instruments.
- Decision systems.
- Verification procedures.
- Implementation guidance.
- Supporting tools, templates, and workflows.

**Layer 4 — Application Adaptations**

Approved methodology may be adapted for:

- Client delivery.
- Internal organizational improvement.
- Education and workforce development.
- Publishing.
- Software implementation.
- Managed services.
- Other approved institutional applications.

An adaptation does not acquire authority to redefine the underlying
methodology.

**Layer 5 — Evidence and Evolution**

The methodology must preserve:

- Authority basis.
- Development history.
- Evidence from use.
- Validation status.
- Known limitations.
- Open questions.
- Changes.
- Supersession history.
- Related institutional dependencies.

### 2.2 Incomplete Areas

The following architecture remains incomplete and must not be inferred
from this foundation:

- A complete proprietary consulting lifecycle.
- Detailed discovery procedures.
- Standardized organizational assessment instruments.
- Formal maturity models.
- Detailed process-mapping methodology.
- Detailed diagnostic decision trees.
- Standard implementation playbooks.
- Formal verification thresholds.
- Formal sustainment assessment instruments.
- Consulting-quality scoring models.
- Industry-specific methodological adaptations.
- Consultant certification requirements.
- Formal methodology-validation protocols.
- Complete metadata standards beyond the minimum approved in this
  foundation.

## 3. Approved Work Progression

Huerta Group's approved work progression is principle-level rather than
a finalized proprietary phase system.

### 3.1 Understand the organization

Establish sufficient understanding of the organization, its purpose,
stakeholders, environment, systems, operating structure, constraints,
priorities, and relevant history.

The organization should not be reduced to the presenting issue alone.

### 3.2 Define the problem or intended outcome

Clarify:

- What condition requires attention.
- Why it matters.
- What evidence supports its existence.
- Who or what is affected.
- What outcome is sought.
- What boundaries apply.
- What assumptions remain unresolved.

A requested solution should not be accepted automatically as an accurate
definition of the underlying problem.

### 3.3 Examine systems and evidence

Study the relevant:

- Processes.
- Roles.
- Decisions.
- Information flows.
- Technologies.
- Controls.
- Measures.
- Incentives.
- Dependencies.
- Risks.
- Constraints.
- Historical evidence.
- Stakeholder experience.

Analysis should distinguish causes, contributing conditions, symptoms,
and consequences.

### 3.4 Design improvement

Develop an improvement design that connects the defined problem,
evidence, organizational context, proposed changes, intended outcomes,
risks, dependencies, and measures of success.

Improvement design may involve organizational, operational,
technological, informational, managerial, or capability-related changes.

### 3.5 Support implementation

Translate approved improvement design into executable action with
appropriate sequencing, ownership, resources, controls, documentation,
communication, training, and adoption support.

Methodology does not presume that recommendation delivery constitutes
implementation.

### 3.6 Verify results

Examine whether:

- Approved changes were implemented.
- The implemented system operates as intended.
- Required participants understand and use it.
- Controls and measures function.
- Evidence supports the intended improvement.
- Unintended consequences or unresolved deficiencies remain.

Verification requirements should be proportionate to the importance,
risk, and reversibility of the change.

### 3.7 Build sustainable capability

Ensure that the organization has sufficient ownership, knowledge,
documentation, measurement, governance, operational capability, and
review mechanisms to maintain and improve the system.

Huerta Group should avoid creating unnecessary dependence where
sustainable client capability is an approved objective.

### 3.8 Preserve learning

Capture:

- What was learned.
- What was validated.
- What failed or remained uncertain.
- What was client-specific.
- What may improve institutional methodology.
- What requires further testing.
- What should remain confidential.
- What may be proposed for institutional approval.

This progression is authoritative as a governing structure.

It is not approved as a branded, numbered, proprietary phase model.

## 4. Methodological Asset Distinctions

### 4.1 Authoritative Distinctions

**Methodological doctrine**

Enduring governing beliefs and propositions that constrain how Huerta
Group develops and applies methodology.

Doctrine is broader and more durable than an individual method.

**Principle**

A governing rule or proposition that directs methodological reasoning or
application.

A principle may apply across multiple methods or domains.

**Method**

A defined approach for performing a particular type of methodological
work.

A method should specify its purpose, scope, required inputs, governing
logic, expected outputs, limitations, and appropriate use.

**Framework**

An organized structure that relates concepts, domains, questions,
elements, or activities to support consistent analysis or action.

A framework may contain or coordinate multiple methods.

**Model**

A structured representation of an organization, system, condition,
relationship, maturity state, or decision environment.

A model simplifies reality for a defined analytical or operational
purpose.

**Standard**

An approved requirement governing how methodological work must be
performed, documented, evaluated, or controlled.

Standards are normative. Guidance is not automatically a standard.

**Assessment**

A structured evaluation of an organization, system, capability, process,
condition, or level of maturity against defined criteria.

**Diagnostic instrument**

A governed instrument used to collect, organize, test, or interpret
evidence for diagnostic purposes.

An instrument does not independently establish a diagnosis without an
approved interpretive method.

**Tool**

A supporting resource used to perform methodological work.

Tools may include worksheets, calculators, software utilities, interview
aids, or analytical aids.

A tool is not automatically a method.

**Template**

A reusable structure for capturing or presenting information
consistently.

A template does not independently establish methodological doctrine or
analytical validity.

**Workflow**

A defined sequence of actions, decisions, handoffs, or states used to
execute a method or related operational activity.

**Decision system**

A governed structure of criteria, evidence, decision rights, rules, and
escalation paths supporting consistent decisions.

**Verification procedure**

A defined procedure used to determine whether an implementation,
control, capability, deliverable, or result meets approved criteria.

**Implementation guidance**

Approved guidance supporting the practical application, sequencing,
adaptation, adoption, or sustainment of an improvement.

Guidance may permit context-sensitive judgment and is not automatically
mandatory.

**Educational adaptation**

A learning-oriented representation of approved methodology designed
under Education & Workforce Development authority.

It may simplify, sequence, explain, practice, or assess methodology but
may not redefine it.

**Software implementation**

A technical representation or enablement of approved methodology within
a software system.

Software may enforce, guide, record, automate, or analyze approved
methodological activity but may not establish methodology through code
alone.

### 4.2 Provisional Distinctions

The following terms may be used descriptively but require further
governance before they become formal asset classes:

- Playbook.
- Protocol.
- Maturity model.
- Operating model.
- Reference architecture.
- Pattern.
- Practice.
- Technique.
- Method family.
- Method module.
- Control framework.
- Certification standard.

Platform & Systems Development may retain these as non-authoritative
candidate values but must not assign them as approved classifications
until Methodology or another competent authority approves them.

## 5. Lifecycle and Maturity

Methodological authority, maturity, disclosure, and implementation
readiness are separate dimensions. They must not be collapsed into one
status field.

### 5.1 Development Status

**Working concept**

An early methodological idea under exploration.

It has no institutional authority and must not be represented as
approved methodology.

**Draft**

A documented proposal sufficiently developed for structured review.

A draft remains non-authoritative unless explicitly approved.

**Under review**

A documented asset undergoing methodological, operational, technical,
constitutional, intellectual-property, educational, or other required
review.

Review does not imply approval.

**Validated through use**

An asset has produced meaningful evidence through controlled or
documented application.

Validation through use strengthens the evidence basis but does not
independently create institutional authority.

**Approved internal method**

A methodological asset explicitly approved for internal institutional
use within a defined scope.

Its limitations, dependencies, version, and authority basis must be
recorded.

**Superseded**

An asset has been replaced as the current authoritative version but
remains preserved for traceability and historical understanding.

**Archived**

An inactive asset retained for institutional history, evidence, legal
preservation, client recordkeeping, or knowledge continuity.

Archived material is not current methodology.

### 5.2 Separate Readiness Designations

The following are not maturity stages. They are separate approvals:

**Approved for educational adaptation**

Education & Workforce Development may develop learning experiences from
the approved source methodology.

**Approved for software implementation**

Platform & Systems Development may encode, enable, or operationalize the
approved methodological requirements within defined technical
boundaries.

**Approved for public disclosure**

The asset, or a specified representation of it, may be disclosed
publicly.

Public approval does not authorize disclosure of all internal detail.

**Approved for client application**

The asset may be used within client engagements under its approved scope
and controls.

**Proposed for proprietary institutional-asset governance**

The asset has been referred to Intellectual Property for evaluation,
classification, protection, packaging, licensing, or stewardship.

Referral does not itself determine ownership, protection, licensing
status, or proprietary classification.

### 5.3 Required Lifecycle Rule

No methodological asset may be represented as an approved internal
method solely because it:

- Appears on the public website.
- Exists in software.
- Has been taught.
- Was used by a consultant.
- Was delivered to a client.
- Was published.
- Was created by Huerta Group personnel.
- Has been classified as intellectual property.
- Has been validated through one or more uses.
- Exists in the Institutional Method Library.

## 6. Authority and Approval

### 6.1 Source of Methodological Authority

A methodological asset becomes authoritative when:

1. It falls within the legitimate authority of the Methodology room.
2. Its content and scope are sufficiently defined.
3. Its status, version, authority basis, and limitations are recorded.
4. It receives explicit approval through the then-valid institutional
   approval process.
5. Any required constitutional, intellectual-property, educational,
   technical, operational, legal, or client-specific dependencies are
   resolved or expressly bounded.

Presence in the Method Library records authority. It does not create
authority.

### 6.2 Present Approval Basis

Until a more specific constitutional approval mechanism is established,
methodological assets may be approved through an explicit decision
recorded in the Methodology room under the Founder's existing
institutional authority.

This is an interim governance rule and remains subordinate to:

- The Constitution.
- Later-approved institutional decision-rights architecture.
- Any valid superseding governance decision.

Platform & Systems Development must therefore store the authority basis
rather than hard-code an immutable assumption that one office or role
will always approve methodology.

### 6.3 Provisional Material

Provisional material must:

- Be clearly identified as non-authoritative.
- Record its author or originating source where appropriate.
- Record its purpose.
- Record its current status.
- Record unresolved questions.
- Avoid language implying institutional approval.
- Remain segregable from current approved methodology.

### 6.4 Corrections and Refinements

A non-substantive correction may update:

- Spelling.
- Formatting.
- Broken references.
- Metadata errors.
- Clerical inconsistencies.

A substantive refinement changes:

- Meaning.
- Scope.
- Requirements.
- Methodological logic.
- Decision criteria.
- Required evidence.
- Approved application.
- Limitations.
- Disclosure boundaries.

Substantive refinements require a new version and approval appropriate
to the change.

### 6.5 Supersession

A superseding record must identify:

- The asset superseded.
- The effective version.
- The reason for supersession.
- Whether prior applications remain valid.
- Any transition requirements.
- Any continuing historical or limited use.

Superseded assets must not be deleted from institutional history.

### 6.6 Disagreement and Uncertainty

Methodological disagreement or uncertainty should be recorded through:

- Open issue.
- Competing interpretation.
- Evidence limitation.
- Known exception.
- Unresolved dependency.
- Validation concern.
- Minority or alternative position, where institutionally relevant.
- Decision rationale when the matter is resolved.

Uncertainty must not be concealed through premature approval or false
precision.

## 7. Disclosure and Protection

Disclosure level and methodological authority are separate.

### 7.1 Public methodology principles

High-level approved principles suitable for public communication.

These may describe Huerta Group's philosophy and general approach
without disclosing internal methods, decision logic, instruments, or
proprietary implementation detail.

### 7.2 Internal methodology

Approved methodology intended for authorized internal use.

Internal methodology may include detailed methods, standards, operating
guidance, assessment logic, and verification requirements.

Internal does not automatically mean legally confidential or
proprietary.

### 7.3 Confidential methodological material

Material subject to restricted access because of institutional
sensitivity, contractual obligations, security considerations, client
obligations, personnel sensitivity, or another approved reason.

The basis for confidentiality should be recorded.

### 7.4 Proprietary methodology

Methodological material formally governed as a proprietary institutional
asset through coordination with Intellectual Property.

The Methodology room may determine methodological authority and
substance.

Intellectual Property determines applicable asset classification,
protection, packaging, licensing, and stewardship within its authority.

This foundation makes no legal determination that any particular method
qualifies for trade-secret, copyright, patent, trademark, contractual,
or other protection.

### 7.5 Client-specific material

Material created from or for a specific client, including client
evidence, configurations, recommendations, processes, systems,
deliverables, and implementation records.

Client-specific material must not be converted into general
institutional methodology without appropriate review, abstraction,
confidentiality protection, contractual compliance, and approval.

### 7.6 Educational adaptation

A representation of approved methodology designed for learning.

It must preserve the accuracy and authority boundaries of the source
methodology while remaining governed by Education & Workforce
Development for instructional design.

### 7.7 Publishing adaptation

A representation prepared for publication.

Publication approval must distinguish the approved public representation
from the underlying internal methodology.

### 7.8 Software-ready methodology

Approved methodological substance sufficiently precise and bounded for
technical implementation.

Software-ready status requires:

- An approved source.
- Defined scope.
- Defined logic or requirements.
- Known limitations.
- Version identification.
- Technical implementation approval.
- Traceability to the source methodology.

Software-ready does not necessarily mean publicly disclosable,
educationally approved, or proprietary.

### 10.7 Operations and Client-Delivery Dependencies

- Sustainment evidence.

Operational use may inform methodology but does not independently

### 10.8 Blocking Determination

The absence of detailed proprietary methodology does not block creation
of the governed Method Library foundation.

The approved work is limited to:

- Technical structure.
- Governance.
- Metadata.
- Lifecycle controls.
- Authority controls.
- Disclosure controls.
- Version preservation.
- The eight initial approved records.

Development of substantive assessment, diagnostic, implementation,
verification, sustainment, or client-delivery modules remains blocked
until those methods are separately developed and approved by the
Methodology room.
