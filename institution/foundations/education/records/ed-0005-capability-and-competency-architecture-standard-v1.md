## Purpose and scope

Defines core competency concepts and the minimum record architecture
needed to represent them — capability, competency, knowledge, skill,
judgment, practice, performance, objectives, outcomes, evidence, and
relationships. The Foundation source (§6) is authoritative; this
record summarizes it and is corrected by it wherever they differ.

## Approved content

**Core concepts (§§6.1–6.10).** A *capability* is the demonstrated
ability of an individual, team, or organization to produce or support
defined outcomes under relevant conditions; it may depend on multiple
competencies and supporting systems. A *competency* is a defined and
assessable combination of knowledge, skill, judgment, practice, and
performance associated with effective action in a particular context —
not every competency requires every element in equal measure.
*Knowledge* alone does not establish competency (§6.3). A *skill* is a
learned ability to perform a task or class of tasks with appropriate
consistency and quality. *Judgment* interprets conditions, weighs
evidence, recognizes uncertainty and limits, selects action, and
accepts responsibility. *Practice* is purposeful developmental
activity. *Performance* is observable execution — a single instance
may not establish durable competency (§6.7). A *learning objective* is
prospective and does not establish that learning occurred (§6.8); a
*learning outcome* requires evidence before it may be treated as
achieved (§6.9); an *organizational outcome* is distinguished from
individual learning outcomes (§6.10).

**Minimum competency-record architecture (§6.11).** A competency
record may carry the twenty-six enumerated fields — identifier, title,
definition, purpose, scope, type, context, audience relationship,
constituent elements, evidence expectations, assessment relationships,
methodology / IP / knowledge relationships, related programs and
units, projects and capstones, later-approved credentials,
capabilities, lifecycle status, authority status, version, effective
date, supersession, disclosure, limitations, and dependencies. Not all
fields are required for every competency, and **no detailed competency
library is authorized**.

**Permitted relationships (§6.12).** Competencies may conceptually
relate, many-to-many, to methodology records, IP assets, knowledge
sources, programs, pathways, courses, learning units, objectives,
assessments, exercises, projects, capstones, credentials, roles,
organizational capabilities, and continuing-development activities —
and no relationship implies approval of the related record.

## Limitations and open questions

- Summary record: Foundation §6 is authoritative and corrects this
  record wherever they differ.
- No competency library, competency level, role-based standard,
  scoring criterion, evidence threshold, performance standard, or
  competency mapping exists or is authorized (§4.13, §21); detailed
  competency taxonomy awaits Education authority.

## Technical implementation

Approved as conceptual model and relationship architecture only. The
schema supports competency relationships; no competency record exists,
and no automated competency decision is authorized (§16.8).
