## Purpose and scope

Defines the approved high-level layers and relationships of Huerta
Group's educational system — doctrine, capabilities, programs,
experiences, assessment, application, verification, credentialing,
continuing education, organizational training, quality, and
technology. The Foundation source (§4) is authoritative; this record
summarizes it and is corrected by it wherever they differ.

## Approved content

**The twelve architecture layers (§§4.1–4.12):** educational doctrine
(enduring philosophy, principles, boundaries, purpose); capability and
competency architecture (capabilities to be developed, contributing
competencies, and the relationships among knowledge, skill, judgment,
practice, and performance); learning-program architecture (organizing
approved learning experiences around defined purposes, audiences,
capabilities, objectives, requirements, and outcomes — no specific
program is approved); learning experiences (instructional, practical,
collaborative, reflective, or applied); assessment architecture (how
evidence of learning, progress, competency, and application may be
collected and evaluated); practical application (connecting learning
to realistic tasks, projects, decisions, systems, cases, simulations,
or organizational contexts); verification (whether evidence supports a
defined conclusion such as completion, competency, or readiness);
credentialing (a governed architecture for future recognition — no
public credential or certification authorized); continuing education
(maintenance, renewal, extension, adaptation, or continued development
— no requirement authorized); organizational training (team
capability, shared practices, leadership, governance, knowledge
transfer — no product approved); educational quality (accuracy,
fidelity, coherence, accessibility, relevance, assessment validity,
evidence, feedback, limitations, versioning, improvement); and
educational technology (supporting the system without independently
determining educational substance or consequential learner outcomes).

**Intentionally incomplete areas (§4.13):** detailed competency
taxonomy, program categories, universal curriculum patterns,
credential levels, certification architecture, instructor
qualification standards, facilitator standards, learner-support
standards, specific quality metrics, program-evaluation methodology,
accreditation strategy, commercial and delivery models, and production
educational technology architecture all remain intentionally
incomplete.

## Limitations and open questions

- Summary record: Foundation §4 is authoritative and corrects this
  record wherever they differ.
- Layers are architecture only: no layer's presence approves a
  program, experience, assessment, credential, or offering.

## Technical implementation

Approved as governed content and metadata only. No runtime behavior.
