# Huerta Group LLC Constitution

## Article III — Principles and Character

### Section 1. Constitutional Function

This Article establishes the enduring constitutional principles and institutional character through which the Company shall fulfill the responsibilities established in Article II and exercise its authority in service of the purpose and identity established in Article I.

The principles established by this Article are binding constitutional standards. They are not aspirational values, branding statements, operational procedures, or temporary cultural preferences.

The Company’s strategies, structures, technologies, methodologies, policies, and practices may evolve. The principles governing how the Company conducts itself, exercises judgment, and fulfills its constitutional responsibilities shall remain enduring.

### Section 2. Principles and Institutional Character

A constitutional principle is an objective governing standard by which the Company’s conduct, judgment, decisions, responsibilities, and exercise of authority shall be evaluated.

Institutional character is the Company’s enduring disposition to embody its constitutional principles consistently across circumstances, changes in leadership, and generations of institutional life.

Institutional character shall be demonstrated through conduct rather than declaration. It shall be most clearly revealed when adherence to constitutional principle involves difficulty, uncertainty, opposition, inconvenience, sacrifice, or cost.

The Company shall not claim constitutional character through reputation, branding, public representation, or stated values that are not materially reflected in its internal and external conduct.

### Section 3. Integrated and Consistent Application

The principles established by this Article shall operate concurrently as an integrated constitutional system.

The sequence in which they appear is organizational and explanatory. It shall not establish rank, chronological exclusivity, or a fixed decision procedure.

More than one principle may govern the same action, decision, relationship, or exercise of authority. Each applicable principle shall retain meaningful effect, and no principle shall be invoked selectively to evade, diminish, or violate another.

The principles shall govern the Company’s internal and external conduct alike. Legitimate differences in role, responsibility, authority, relationship, circumstance, risk, law, confidentiality, and constitutional obligation may affect their application but shall not justify materially contradictory institutional character.

This Article shall be interpreted consistently with the purpose and identity established in Article I and the responsibilities established in Article II.

The principles established here shall not create a separate hierarchy among themselves. Where constitutional responsibilities genuinely conflict, the order of responsibility established in Article I shall govern.

Prudence shall guide selection among constitutionally permissible courses of action. It shall not authorize the Company to reorder constitutional priorities or balance away superior constitutional responsibilities.

### Section 4. Integrity

The Company shall maintain substantive coherence and fidelity among its constitutional principles, commitments, representations, judgments, decisions, and conduct.

The Company shall honor the substance of its commitments rather than rely upon selective interpretation, technical compliance, ambiguity, or formal adherence that defeats their legitimate purpose.

The Company shall not profess one standard externally while materially tolerating another within its own institution.

Integrity requires consistency between what the Company claims, what it promises, what it decides, and how it acts.

The Company shall preserve this fidelity when doing so is difficult, inconvenient, unpopular, commercially disadvantageous, or costly.

Institutional character under this principle requires reliability, consistency, courage under pressure, and refusal to abandon constitutional commitments for expedience.

Integrity shall not require rigid literalism where constitutional responsibilities must be interpreted together. It requires good-faith fidelity to constitutional substance, lawful commitments, and legitimate institutional obligations.

### Section 5. Truthfulness and Intellectual Integrity

The Company shall pursue, evaluate, qualify, revise, and communicate knowledge and professional judgment according to evidence, disciplined reasoning, honest evaluation, and intellectual integrity.

The Company shall distinguish carefully among verified fact, reasoned professional judgment, informed assumption, and uncertainty, communicating each honestly, accurately, and without misrepresentation.

The Company shall communicate material limitations, risks, assumptions, and uncertainties necessary for informed understanding by those legitimately entitled to rely upon its judgment.

The Company shall not distort evidence, exaggerate certainty, conceal material limitations, or shape professional conclusions according to convenience, popularity, ideology, personal preference, commercial interest, institutional pressure, or other improper influence.

The Company shall remain intellectually humble. It shall test assumptions, recognize the limits of its knowledge, remain open to credible evidence, and revise conclusions, methodologies, and recommendations when better understanding is honestly established.

Candor shall be required where silence, omission, or ambiguity would materially mislead those entitled to rely upon the Company’s judgment.

Professional analysis and judgment shall remain free from improper influence that would compromise the Company’s constitutional Duty to Truth.

Institutional character under this principle requires honesty, precision, intellectual humility, candor, evidentiary discipline, independence of judgment, and willingness to acknowledge uncertainty or error.

Truthfulness and Intellectual Integrity shall not require indiscriminate disclosure. Information shall be communicated consistently with law, confidentiality, privilege, privacy, contractual duties, and the legitimate entitlement of the recipient to receive it.

### Section 6. Human Dignity

The Company shall recognize and protect the inherent worth of every person affected by its conduct, authority, systems, technologies, and professional work.

Human worth shall not depend upon organizational rank, economic value, productivity, technical ability, professional status, agreement with leadership, commercial usefulness, convenience, or relationship to the Company.

The Company shall not reduce people merely to labor, cost, capacity, risk, data, output, or instruments of organizational efficiency.

The Company shall treat people with respect and shall consider their agency, legitimate interests, boundaries, development, working conditions, and the reasonably foreseeable human consequences of institutional decisions.

The Company shall not pursue performance, efficiency, innovation, growth, or technical sophistication through exploitation, humiliation, objectification, dehumanization, or disregard for affected people.

Institutional character under this principle requires respect, restraint, serious consideration of human consequences, recognition of legitimate agency, and refusal to treat persons as expendable means.

Equal Human Dignity shall not require identical roles, responsibilities, decisions, treatment, or outcomes. Legitimate differences may be recognized where they are consistent with Fairness and the Company’s constitutional responsibilities.

### Section 7. Fairness

The Company shall exercise institutional judgment, discretion, differentiation, allocation, evaluation, and authority according to relevant facts, legitimate distinctions, consistent standards, reasoned judgment, proportional considerations, and constitutional responsibilities.

The Company shall protect institutional judgment from improper distortion by favoritism, prejudice, retaliation, personal animus, arbitrary preference, undisclosed self-interest, selective enforcement, or other illegitimate influence.

Impartiality shall operate within Fairness as freedom from improper distortion of judgment. It shall not require indifference among constitutionally different responsibilities, elimination of legitimate discretion, or identical treatment of materially different circumstances.

The Company may distinguish among persons, circumstances, roles, responsibilities, performance, conduct, capability, risk, need, contractual obligation, and institutional purpose where those distinctions are relevant, legitimate, and proportionate.

The Company shall not use discretion as concealment for arbitrary or self-serving authority.

Materially consequential decisions shall be capable of reasoned justification according to the standards and responsibilities legitimately governing them.

Institutional character under this principle requires evenhandedness, consistency, restraint, willingness to examine bias, recognition of legitimate differences, and resistance to favoritism, retaliation, and improper influence.

Fairness shall not require equal outcomes, absolute neutrality, or the elimination of necessary and constitutionally justified distinctions.

### Section 8. Stewardship

The Company shall regard its purpose, authority, institutional assets, knowledge, reputation, relationships, opportunities, and capabilities as entrusted responsibilities to be administered in service of its constitutional purpose.

No founder, owner, executive, manager, employee, successor, or other holder of institutional authority shall possess unrestricted constitutional dominion over the Company’s permanent purpose, governing principles, institutional truth, human responsibilities, or obligations to future generations.

Those entrusted with institutional authority shall exercise it for legitimate constitutional purposes rather than personal entitlement, extraction, institutional vanity, dependency creation, or improper private advantage.

The Company shall preserve, strengthen, develop, and responsibly transmit its institutional capability, knowledge, relationships, and resources for the benefit of its continuing mission and those who will inherit the consequences of present decisions.

Stewardship requires regard for future generations without treating the Company’s current form, ownership, structure, scale, or continued existence as ends in themselves.

Responsible contraction, restructuring, transformation, transfer, sale, merger, discontinuation, or orderly dissolution may constitute faithful Stewardship where necessary to preserve constitutional purpose, prevent unsustainable harm, or avoid maintaining institutional existence at the expense of superior constitutional responsibilities.

Institutional character under this principle requires service, restraint, intergenerational responsibility, responsible administration of power, protection against short-term depletion, and willingness to preserve constitutional purpose through changing institutional forms.

References to entrusted responsibility or stewardship establish a constitutional standard governing the exercise of lawful authority. They shall not, without separate legal action, create a technical legal trust, eliminate lawful ownership rights, alter equity interests, replace applicable fiduciary duties, or create property rights in stakeholders or future leaders.

### Section 9. Prudence

The Company shall exercise reasoned, proportionate, evidence-informed, and constitutionally bounded judgment when choosing and acting under uncertainty, risk, limited information, competing considerations, and consequential circumstances.

Prudence shall require consideration proportionate to the matter of:

* Evidence and uncertainty.
* Reasonable alternatives.
* The risks of action and inaction.
* Timing, delay, and reversibility.
* Institutional capability and readiness.
* Human, organizational, and long-term consequences.
* The constitutional priorities established in Article I.

The Company shall remain capable of timely and decisive action where delay would create greater risk or prevent legitimate benefit.

Prudence shall protect the Company from both recklessness and paralysis. It shall not be interpreted as habitual caution, resistance to change, protection of obsolete systems, or a requirement that uncertainty be eliminated before responsible action may occur.

The Company may undertake responsible experimentation where the purpose is legitimate, risks are proportionate, evidence and uncertainty are represented honestly, capability is adequate or responsibly supplemented, appropriate oversight exists, and revision or discontinuation remains possible.

Prudence shall account for both the risks of novelty and the risks of preserving inadequate methods merely because they are familiar.

Institutional character under this principle requires sound judgment, foresight, proportionality, intellectual humility, responsible risk-taking, adaptability, restraint where warranted, and decisiveness where required.

Prudence shall operate beneath the constitutional priorities established in Article I. It may determine the wisest constitutionally permissible method, timing, scope, and allocation of action. It shall not authorize the sacrifice of a superior constitutional responsibility for a subordinate interest or convenience.

### Section 10. Excellence

The Company shall fulfill its constitutional responsibilities with substantive quality, rigor, care, effectiveness, usability, reliability, and durability proportionate to the purpose, significance, risk, consequences, and reasonably available capability associated with the responsibility undertaken.

The constitutional center of Excellence shall be fitness for purpose.

The Company’s work shall be suited to the actual responsibility, organizational need, intended outcome, operating context, and people reasonably expected to rely upon it.

Excellence shall require competent professional practice, rigor proportionate to significance and risk, material effectiveness, appropriate clarity, disciplined execution, and avoidance of deficiencies that could reasonably have been prevented.

The Company shall manage necessary complexity responsibly and shall not transfer unnecessary complexity to those who rely upon its work.

The Company shall pursue usability, reliability, maintainability, transferability, and durability to the extent appropriate to the intended purpose and expected lifespan of the work.

The Company shall possess, obtain, or responsibly supplement the competence required for consequential responsibilities. Material limitations of capability shall be recognized and communicated honestly, and the Company’s role shall be structured accordingly.

Institutional character under this principle requires care, competence, preparation, discipline, craftsmanship, clarity, follow-through, and continual improvement in light of evidence and experience.

Excellence shall not be measured primarily by appearance, prestige, complexity, novelty, technical sophistication, volume of effort, or claims of superiority.

Excellence shall not require perfection, guaranteed outcomes, unlimited time or expenditure, maximum intensity in every circumstance, elimination of uncertainty, unilateral expansion of contractual scope, unsustainable workloads, institutional depletion, or continued adherence to obsolete methods.

Excellence shall not justify sacrificing ethical integrity, long-term organizational health, Human Dignity, or the legitimate interests and well-being of affected people.

### Section 11. Accountability

The Company shall maintain continuing answerability, ownership, oversight, and corrective responsibility for its authority, professional judgment, commitments, decisions, delegated responsibilities, implementation, and reasonably foreseeable consequences.

Accountability shall operate before, during, and after the exercise of authority.

It shall require clear and proportionate attribution of authority and responsibility, oversight appropriate to significance and risk, attention to emerging consequences, and willingness to intervene when correction becomes necessary.

The Company shall acknowledge material error, failure, or harm honestly when responsibility exists.

Where correction is reasonably possible and constitutionally appropriate, the Company shall take proportionate action to correct information, decisions, systems, recommendations, conduct, or resulting harm.

The Company shall not evade responsibility through hierarchy, delegation, automation, fragmented ownership, technical compliance, contractual narrowness, concealment, or blame transfer.

Delegation may transfer defined tasks or authority. Delegation shall not, by itself, eliminate responsibility for the decision to delegate, the conditions surrounding that delegation, or the oversight and consequences for which the Company remains constitutionally answerable under the principles of this Article.

Accountability shall recognize individual, leadership, shared, and systemic responsibility. The Company shall not assign responsibility exclusively to individuals where institutional systems, incentives, training, resourcing, oversight, or leadership materially contributed to the outcome.

Institutional character under this principle requires ownership, willingness to explain, openness to examination, acknowledgment of error, corrective discipline, refusal to deflect responsibility, acceptance of proportionate consequences, and preservation of institutional learning.

Accountability shall be corrective and proportionate rather than indiscriminately punitive. It shall distinguish good-faith error from misconduct without prescribing the procedures or consequences governing that distinction.

Accountability shall not require universal disclosure and shall remain consistent with law, confidentiality, privacy, privilege, Fairness, and legitimate institutional responsibilities.

### Section 12. Coordinated Application and Institutional Implementation

The principles established by this Article shall govern as one constitutional system.

Supporting qualities, disciplines, and practices may arise through the coordinated application of multiple principles without becoming separate constitutional principles.

Responsible Innovation is one such coordinated discipline.

New or materially changed technologies, systems, methodologies, services, business models, and institutional structures shall remain governed by all applicable principles of this Article.

Innovation shall remain a means rather than a constitutional end. Novelty, speed, technological sophistication, and market attention shall possess no independent constitutional value.

Responsible experimentation conducted consistently with constitutional principle may fail without necessarily constituting constitutional misconduct. Innovation shall nevertheless remain subject to proportionate judgment, appropriate oversight, correction, and institutional learning.

Delegation to employees, partners, vendors, technologies, automated systems, or distributed structures shall remain governed by the applicable principles of Integrity, Truthfulness and Intellectual Integrity, Human Dignity, Fairness, Stewardship, Prudence, Excellence, and Accountability.

Whether the Constitution requires a distinct doctrine governing the non-delegation of constitutional responsibility remains reserved for later analysis under the Constitutional Admission Test.

Governance documents, policies, methodologies, professional standards, and operational systems may implement the principles established by this Article.

They shall not redefine, diminish, contradict, selectively displace, or reduce those principles to technical compliance.

Implementation methods may evolve. Constitutional standards shall remain controlling.

Comprehensive doctrines governing Constitutional Supremacy, Good-Faith Interpretation, Constitutional Harmony, Burden of Constitutional Consistency, amendment, interpretive authority, and any distinct constitutional treatment of delegated technological authority are reserved for later constitutional development.

Nothing in this Section shall create a ninth substantive principle, an independent constitutional objective of innovation, an operational innovation policy, or an unexamined constitutional doctrine.

The enduring function of this Article is to ensure that the Company fulfills every constitutional responsibility in accordance with coherent constitutional principles and demonstrated institutional character.
