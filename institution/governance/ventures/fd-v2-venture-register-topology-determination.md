## Approved architecture

**Institutional plane.** Huerta Group LLC controls venture standing,
charter, grants, reserved authority, lifecycle conditions,
parent/venture relationships, and other institution-wide
determinations.

**Operational plane.** Each admitted venture may govern and operate
itself within the authority actually delegated to it.

**Core rule.** Repository location does not determine authority. The
governing plane of the fact does.

**Authority rule.** Institutional authority remains singular.
Operational governance may be distributed. Crossing from operational
authority into institutional authority requires an explicit
institutional-plane act.

## Existing Huerta Group Publishing records

1. Preserve Huerta Group Publishing's existing historical operational
   governance records.
2. Do not rewrite historical records merely because their language
   refers to "Founder Office."
3. A venture-level Founder Office decision may govern matters within
   delegated venture authority.
4. A venture-level determination cannot create or modify
   institutional-plane authority.
5. Any matter crossing from the venture operational plane into the
   institutional plane requires escalation into the parent governance
   system and an authorized parent determination.

## Institutional register rule

1. Huerta Group LLC's determinations register remains the single
   institutional-plane determination register.
2. Venture repositories may maintain operational decision histories,
   standards, validation systems, blueprints, as-built records, and
   other governance assets within delegated authority.
3. Venture operational records may be authoritative within their
   delegated plane without becoming parallel institutional authority.

## Identity and reference rules

1. Use identifier-first governance identity.
2. Repository paths are locations, not institutional identities.
3. Governance identity must survive repository restructuring, path
   changes, documentation reorganization, future tooling, and
   repository migration.
4. Prefer point-in-time cross-repository references over live
   synchronization between governance systems.
5. Preserve sufficient provenance for a cross-plane reference to
   identify the governing artifact and its relevant authoritative
   state.

## Synchronization doctrine

1. Synchronization does not create authority.
2. Mirrors, indexes, registries, dashboards, and future application
   surfaces may represent governance facts but do not originate those
   facts unless explicitly authorized as governing instruments.
3. Where a representation conflicts with governing authority, correct
   the representation. Do not reinterpret the governing authority to
   accommodate stale representation.
