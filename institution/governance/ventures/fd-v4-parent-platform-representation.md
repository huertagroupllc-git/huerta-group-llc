## 1. Purpose

Determine how Huerta Group LLC truthfully represents an admitted venture on its public corporate platform without collapsing the distinctions among the parent institution (Huerta Group LLC), an admitted venture (Huerta Group Publishing), and a platform a venture operates (the Author Operating System). This is governance and representation architecture — the public-truth boundary the FD-V2 instrument required before any corresponding website implementation. It is not website implementation, not a launch decision, and not brand architecture.

## 4. Representation Principles

1. **Representation is derivative.** The parent platform represents governed institutional truth; it never creates venture standing, authority, capability, readiness, or relationships (Blueprint §25.1).
2. **The governing plane of the fact controls** (FD-V2 core rule). Institutional-plane facts (standing, charter, grants, relationship) come only from institutional-plane authority. Venture-operational facts remain governed within the venture's delegated plane and are referenced by the parent only as attributed venture facts (§8).
3. **Truth discipline binds every sentence** (Guardrail 8; Blueprint §18.1): current truth distinguished from future direction; no planned capability presented as existing; no commercial availability implied where none exists.
4. **Silence remains a governed posture** (Blueprint §18.2). Representation is added by determination, never by existence, maturity, or momentum — and can be truthful silence again by determination.
5. **Sufficiency without duplication.** Parent representation is sufficient for institutional understanding and stops there; the venture's own platform carries the venture's own story (§14, §15).
6. **Identifier-first identity** (FD-V2): representation facts trace to registry entries, determination records, and charter versions — never to repository paths or page URLs as authority.
7. **Distinct identities, one institution.** Parent, venture, and platform each keep their own name, voice, and surface; the relationship is stated, never blended.
8. **Machine-readable surfaces are public claims** (Blueprint §18.6): metadata, structured data, and SEO artifacts obey every rule above.

## 5. Parent Institution Representation

Venture representation changes nothing about how the parent presents itself:

1. Huerta Group LLC remains publicly what its governed copy states: an organizational systems company. The existence of ventures does not recast it as a holding company, venture studio, portfolio firm, or publisher, and no such vocabulary enters parent copy.
2. The parent's dual character (Blueprint §5) — operating practice and governing institution — may be expressed through its existing long-view narrative ("built to be an institution"), which repository evidence shows already carries the honest register for this (About: "The Company's work is designed to take more than one form… None of that is promised here").
3. The parent's own capability claims remain governed by the fd-0006 truth-alignment discipline; venture representation may not be used to imply parent-practice capabilities (publishing is not a parent service — §9.5).
4. The parent speaks **about** its ventures in its own voice and Design System; it never adopts a venture's visual language, product voice, or platform vocabulary as its own.

## 6. Venture Representation Model

The approved model for representing any admitted venture on the parent platform is a **minimum governed fact set**, expressed in parent prose:

| Element | Content | Source (§7) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Identity | The venture's registered name; "venture" as the classifying term (Blueprint §4 vocabulary rule — descriptive terms like "imprint" may appear only as description, never as classification) | Registry `id`/`name` |
| Relationship | A venture **of** Huerta Group LLC: institutionally distinct, legally identical; operating under delegated authority within an approved charter; owned and governed by the parent | Registry `institutionalRelationship`, `legalEntityStatus`; Charter §9 |
| Purpose | The venture's chartered purpose, summarized without adopting it as parent capability | Charter §2 |
| Governed standing | Formally admitted and chartered by Founder Office determination, with the admission's honest history (regularization: the venture operated before formal chartering) | Registry `admission`; fd-record |
| Maturity | Only the approved maturity facts of §10 | Registry `lifecycle`; §10 |
| Autonomy and governance | The venture governs its own operations within delegated authority; institutional authority, ownership, strategy, and portfolio governance remain with the parent | Charter §7/§8; registry `delegatedAuthority`, `reservedParentAuthority` |
| Pathway | How a reader reaches the venture's own platform — under the linking rule of §13 only | §13 |

Rules of the model:

1. **Introduction:** a venture is introduced as what it institutionally is — the first formal venture of Huerta Group LLC — in the parent's institutional narrative, not as a product announcement, service line, or portfolio tile.
2. **Expression of relationship:** ownership and governance are stated plainly (a venture *of* the Company, governed by it, not a separate company); autonomy is stated as delegated ("governs its own editorial and operational work within its charter"), never as independence.
3. **Distinctness:** the venture's name appears as a named entity the parent describes; parent pages do not restyle themselves in venture branding, embed venture UI, or present venture pages as parent sections.
4. **Placement:** at current scale (one venture), representation lives **within the existing information architecture** — the About page's long-view narrative is the evidenced seam — with no new route and no navigation change (gr-0003 records that any nav addition overflows and requires a Design System navigation-architecture decision). A dedicated venture route is a future implementation question (§23.6), not part of this model.
5. **Proportionality** (Blueprint §8; FD-V11 as transmitted): the representation is one governed passage per venture, not a system. No portfolio machinery exists or is implied (§21).

## 7. Authorized Institutional Fact Sources

Parent representation of institutional-plane facts derives **only** from:

1. The **Venture Registry** entry (identity, classification, relationship, admission, lifecycle, grants, reserved authority) — the authoritative metadata mirror; the underlying determinations govern.
2. The **Venture Charter** (purpose, scope, delegated/reserved authority, name grant, boundaries).
3. **Founder Office determination records** (fd-#### — admission, topology, and any subsequent venture determinations).
4. **Capability grants** as recorded in the Charter §20 register and the registry — exhaustively; no grant may be represented that is not recorded.
5. **Parent/venture relationship records** and any future institutional-plane records admitted under FD-V2's register rule.

Derivation rules: facts are taken from these sources at writing time, identifier-first with point-in-time provenance (FD-V2); public copy cites no file paths and exposes no registry internals; where a source is silent, the parent says nothing rather than inferring; and no fact may be represented at a strength exceeding its source (e.g., "Chartered" never becomes "launched"; "Active — internal operations" never becomes "serving the public").

## 8. Venture-Operational Fact References

Venture-operational facts — what the venture's platform does, how its editorial work runs, what its membership includes — are governed within the venture's delegated plane (FD-V2). The parent may reference them under all of the following conditions:

1. **Attribution:** always as the venture's facts ("Huerta Group Publishing operates…", "its platform provides…"), never as parent capability, offering, or achievement.
2. **Summary level:** identity-level description only (what the venture does and through what platform), never operational detail — capability lists, workflows, membership mechanics, pricing, editorial method, and product specifics belong to the venture surface (§15).
3. **Evidence verification:** each referenced operational fact is verified against venture repository evidence at writing time and carries the truth constraints the venture itself publishes (its own honest non-goals: no billing, no self-serve access, no distribution, unlaunched public posture).
4. **No ownership transfer:** referencing a venture-operational fact does not admit it to the parent corpus, create parent responsibility for it, or freeze it — it remains the venture's fact, corrected on the venture's surface first.
5. **Point-in-time, never live** (FD-V2 synchronization doctrine): the parent never builds machinery that reads venture systems; representation is prose reviewed on the triggers of §18.

## 9. Huerta Group Publishing Representation

Applying §§6–8 to the first venture, parent representation of Huerta Group Publishing may state, in the parent's own voice:

1. **Status:** Huerta Group Publishing is the first formal venture of Huerta Group LLC — admitted and chartered by Founder Office determination (2026-08-12), having operated as the Company's publishing endeavor before formal chartering (the regularization is honest history and may be stated).
2. **What it is:** an operational venture — not a separate company, subsidiary, or independent brand owner; owned and governed by Huerta Group LLC; conducting its own editorial and operational work under delegated authority within its approved charter.
3. **Purpose:** it exists to publish books under the Huerta Group Publishing imprint and to build and operate the software through which that publishing is done — an Author Operating System whose stated purpose is preserving authorship.
4. **Publishing role:** the publisher of record for its publications is Huerta Group LLC; the imprint presented to readers is Huerta Group Publishing (the Charter §9 model, stated as fact, not as a rights claim).
5. **Boundaries of parent description:** parent copy must not present publishing as a parent-practice service or capability; must not present the venture as commercially transacting, selling, distributing, or open for public author signup; must not present the Author Operating System as a product offering; must not republish venture pricing, membership, or method detail; and must not name unrecorded grants or imply venture access to parent methodology, knowledge, IP, or education (Charter §20: no such grants exist).
6. **Linkage:** governed by §13 — at present, representation without linking.

Illustrative register (not final copy; provided only to make the rule intelligible): *"The Company's first formal venture, Huerta Group Publishing, publishes books under its own imprint and builds the Author Operating System — the software through which that publishing is done. It is a venture of Huerta Group LLC: governed by the Company, running its own editorial work within a chartered mandate."*

## 10. Operational Maturity Representation

What the parent may truthfully say about Huerta Group Publishing's maturity, preserving each recorded distinction:

| Fact | May be represented as | Never as |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Formally admitted venture (Chartered) | "formally chartered," "the Company's first formal venture" | a launch, a product release, a new business line opening |
| Operational venture (Active — internal editorial/production operations) | real, current work: "develops and produces books today," "operations are internal" | public availability, open enrollment, "serving authors everywhere" |
| Implemented publishing platform | "has built and operates its platform" | a commercial software product, a SaaS offering |
| Current publishing operations | editorial development and deterministic production of publication artifacts, memberships without payment collection | sales, billing, distribution, retail presence |
| Future author services | unpromised direction only, in the About register ("none of that is promised here") | existing or scheduled capability |
| Future commercial expansion | not represented, or expressly unpromised direction | anything — commercial readiness is Not Assessed (Corporate Headquarters) |

Binding rules: parent copy never implies capabilities beyond repository evidence; the venture's own published non-goals are a ceiling the parent cannot exceed; the venture's public-disclosure state (Disclosure Review Required; platform deployed pre-launch, no custom domain, no promotion) means the parent must not describe the venture's site as launched or invite the public to use it (§13); and lifecycle dimensions never collapse into a single "status" in public prose any more than in metadata.

## 11. Venture vs. Platform Representation

The governed distinction, stated as representation rules:

1. **Huerta Group Publishing is the venture** — the institutional construct the parent charters, governs, and represents.
2. **The Author Operating System is a platform operated by that venture** — software in the venture's technical custody, through which the venture works.
3. The parent may name the Author Operating System **only with venture attribution** ("Huerta Group Publishing builds/operates the Author Operating System") — never as a parent system, never as a venture itself, never as a stand-alone brand, and never as a product offering.
4. Platform representation confers nothing: naming the platform creates no Platform Governance, no software-product claim, no readiness statement, and no separate institutional identity. A venture may operate one or several platforms without any of them acquiring venture standing (Blueprint §4: a venture is not a repository, and platforms are custody surfaces).
5. If the institution ever wishes to present the Author Operating System as a named public software identity in its own right, that is a new representation and brand question (Branding authority / FD-V3 territory), not an extension of this determination.

## 12. Brand Relationship

Within fd-0001's constraint (Branding is a recognized future authority with no governing document) and creating no brand doctrine:

1. **Name usage:** the parent uses the venture's registered name under the venture's recorded name grant (Charter §12); the parent's own marks remain its own. This determination grants nothing new and creates no general name-use rule — FD-V3 remains open and untouched.
2. **Parent attribution:** publisher-of-record attribution ("Huerta Group LLC" as legal entity, "Huerta Group Publishing" as imprint) is stated as recorded fact where relevant (§9.4).
3. **Venture identity and visual independence:** each platform keeps its own visual language (the parent's DDR-governed Design System on parent pages; the venture's own design constitution on venture pages — the evidenced, deliberate state). The parent does not display venture logos or wordmarks (no such grant exists) and does not imitate venture styling; the venture's obligations toward parent branding remain exactly its charter's and no more.
4. **Linking behavior** as brand behavior: a link is a labeled doorway between two distinct properties (§13), never a shared header, embedded surface, or co-branded frame.
5. **Co-branding:** none exists; none is authorized (Charter §12 grants no co-branding); any future co-branding is a Founder Office (future Branding authority) act.

## 13. Cross-Site Linking

The determination that resolves representation against the venture's reserved launch:

1. **Linking is a disclosure act.** The venture's platform is deployed pre-launch: no custom domain, no promotion, no indexing invitation, and official launch is reserved parent authority (Charter §8.5, §11.4). A parent-platform link inviting the public to the venture's site would effect promotion the launch reservation withholds.
2. **Therefore: representation precedes linking.** Upon approval of FD-V4, the parent may represent the venture (§9) **without** linking to the venture platform. No parent→venture public link exists until the Founder Office effects the venture's official-launch determination or expressly authorizes a pre-launch link.
3. **When linking is authorized:** the link is labeled as a pathway to the venture's own platform (e.g., "Huerta Group Publishing's own site"), rendered in the parent's existing link conventions; the parent implementation currently evidences no external public links, so the first venture link follows a recorded Design System decision (DDR/GR) at implementation time — tab behavior included — rather than an invented convention here.
4. **When venture detail is sought, the venture site carries it:** the parent links rather than republishes (§14/§15); one pathway suffices — the venture is not woven through parent navigation.
5. **No subsection implication in either direction:** the venture site is never presented as a section of the parent site, and the parent is never presented as a page of the venture. Cross-navigation is by labeled doorway, not shared chrome. Any venture-site link back to the parent remains the venture's decision within its charter — this determination imposes none.

## 14. Parent-Site Information Boundary

The parent corporate platform carries, per venture, at most:

1. Venture identity and the "venture" classification;
2. The institutional relationship (owned and governed by Huerta Group LLC; delegated authority; not a separate company);
3. High-level chartered purpose (§9.3);
4. Governed standing (admitted, chartered, with honest regularization history);
5. The approved maturity facts of §10;
6. The pathway to the venture platform, when §13 permits one.

Nothing else. Specifically excluded from the parent surface: venture pricing and membership terms, editorial method and workflow, platform capability inventories, product documentation, support and FAQ content, venture-internal governance detail, and any venture content whose home the venture surface already is.

## 15. Venture-Site Information Boundary

The venture platform retains — as its delegated, self-governed public surface (Charter §7, truth discipline inherited):

1. Detailed venture operations and what-exists-today honesty (its evidenced register);
2. Venture-specific methodology and editorial practice as it chooses to present them within its charter;
3. Platform capabilities and product or service detail (the Author Operating System's own story);
4. Venture-specific workflows, membership, pricing template, support, FAQ, and legal pages;
5. The venture's own public experience, voice, and design.

This determination adds no obligation to the venture surface and removes none; the venture's public copy remains governed by its charter and the universal truth discipline, not by this document.

## 16. Public Truth Boundary

The rule the FD-V2 instrument required, stated for public surfaces:

1. The parent public platform communicates **governed institutional truth**: every venture representation follows current governing facts (§7) at the strength those facts support.
2. **Public copy creates nothing:** no standing, authority, grant, readiness, launch, or commercial state comes into being by being described (Blueprint §25.1; FD-V2 synchronization doctrine).
3. **Future remains distinguishable from current** on every surface, in the About register already evidenced ("none of that is promised here") — for ventures exactly as for parent capabilities (fd-0006 discipline).
4. **Representation is corrected when governing facts change** (§18, §19) — promptly, as a governed change.
5. **Stale representation never constrains governance:** where public copy and governing authority diverge, the copy is corrected; the authority is never reinterpreted, delayed, or softened to match the page (FD-V2, verbatim in spirit).

## 17. Future Capability Representation

1. Venture futures (author services, commercial expansion, distribution, public launch, Spanish-locale launch) are represented on the parent platform either not at all or as expressly unpromised direction — never as existing, scheduled, or implied capability.
2. The venture's own future-capability statements bind the parent as a ceiling (§10); the parent never runs ahead of the venture's own honesty.
3. Graduation vocabulary (FD-V9) never appears as trajectory: no "on its way to," no stage projections, no independence narrative (FD-V10 — success is fulfillment of chartered purpose, and no expectation of progression exists for Huerta Group Publishing).
4. Where a future capability becomes real, representation changes **after** the governing determination and evidence, through §18 — never in anticipation of it.

## 18. Representation Change Triggers

Parent venture-representation copy is reviewed — as a governed, human change; no runtime synchronization — upon any of:

1. Venture admission (a new venture's representation is a fresh §21 application, requiring its own disclosure determination);
2. Charter amendment or supersession;
3. Venture retirement, dormancy with public consequence, or supersession;
4. Any graduation determination (FD-V9);
5. Legal-structure change (Corporate Headquarters fact);
6. Material public-readiness change — above all the venture's official-launch determination, which activates §13.3;
7. Material capability-grant change (grants added or revoked);
8. Venture-name or name-grant change;
9. Material operational-maturity change recorded in the registry (e.g., commercial readiness ceasing to be Not Assessed);
10. Any superseding Founder Office determination touching representation, including supersession of this one.

The implementing office verifies venture-representation copy against the registry and charter as part of executing each triggering determination's implementation phase; the determinations register remains the event's record.

## 19. Stale Representation Correction

1. A representation that no longer matches governing facts is a **defect of the representation** (FD-V2): the correction changes the copy, never the fact, and never waits on convenience.
2. Corrections are governed changes in the established pattern (fd-0006 idiom): evidence-cited, bounded, wording-only unless separately authorized, passing the verification gate, deployed and verified on the live surface per the Development Standards deployment rule.
3. Correction authority follows the governing plane: institutional-fact corrections trace to institutional records; venture-operational references are corrected to match venture evidence (§8.4).
4. No correction rewrites history: prior public statements are superseded on the surface and remain visible in git history (§20).

## 20. Historical Traceability

1. Git history is the version record of every public representation change (the established rule for governed artifacts).
2. **Material** representation changes — adding a venture's representation, removing it, changing its standing or maturity claims — are additionally traceable to their authorizing determination: the implementing commit cites the fd-record (or the fd-record's affected-artifacts list names the surface), preserving the instrument → determination → representation chain.
3. Public surfaces carry no visible changelog; traceability is a repository discipline, not a reader-facing feature.
4. Removed representations are not erased from history and may be cited as evidence of the posture governed at their time (Blueprint §24 spirit).

## 21. Future Venture Applicability

The minimum repeatable model every future admitted venture inherits:

1. **The §6 fact set, per venture, from its own registry entry and charter** — identity, relationship, purpose, standing, maturity per its own lifecycle values, pathway per its own disclosure posture.
2. **Representation by determination, per venture** (Blueprint §18.3): admission never places a venture on the parent platform by itself; each venture's parent representation requires its own disclosure-posture determination applying this model. FD-V4 supplies the *how*; each venture still needs its *whether*.
3. **Placement stays proportionate:** representations live within the existing information architecture until evidence shows existing routes cannot responsibly carry them. **No portfolio pages, venture directories, subsidiary systems, or multi-venture navigation are designed or implied** — a second or third represented venture is the trigger for a future, separately authorized information-architecture decision (the second-consumer idiom, applied to representation), which will also face gr-0003's navigation constraint.
4. The model itself is amended only by Founder Office determination superseding this one.

## 22. Explicit Non-Authority Rules

1. Parent representation creates no venture standing, authority, capability, grant, readiness, launch state, or relationship — for the represented venture or any other.
2. Public visibility does not determine institutional standing; absence from the parent platform diminishes nothing (silence is governed posture), and presence elevates nothing.
3. Representation does not convert a venture into a parent capability, a platform into a venture, or an imprint into a brand grant.
4. The parent platform is never a fact source: no institutional record may cite parent public copy as authority for any venture fact.
5. Linking creates no endorsement of venture content beyond the governed relationship, no shared responsibility for venture operations, and no representation grant in the reverse direction.
6. Nothing in this determination amends the Charter, enlarges or reduces delegated authority, resolves FD-V3, establishes Platform Governance or Branding doctrine, or reopens any settled determination.
7. This determination itself, once approved, is authority for representation only — it authorizes no implementation until the Founder Office authorizes the bounded categories of §23.

## 27. Determination

Upon approval by the Founder Office:

1. **FD-V4 is effected:** the venture representation model of §§4–8 and §§13–22 becomes the governing representation architecture for admitted ventures on the Huerta Group LLC public platform.
2. **The Huerta Group Publishing representation (§§9–10) is approved** as that venture's governed parent-platform representation, superseding parent-platform silence as the governed posture — with **representation preceding linking** (§13) and implementation proceeding only under §23's separate authorization.
