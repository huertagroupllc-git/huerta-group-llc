## 6. How Reading A is now represented

**In the determination record and in the prepared wording — not yet in Draft
v4.** Stated so the distinction cannot be lost: `fd-0028` records that the four
listed items are trail-source **categories** and "not a conjunctive four-part
completion gate," and the prepared A10 text says so in the method's own voice:

> These are categories of trail evidence, not four limbs that must all exist and
> all be obtained before the procedure counts as performed.

The prepared text restates trail-first as the determination defines it — work
discovered from evidence of the last actual instance **before** any described
process is accepted as the representation of how work occurs — and sets out the
five performance conditions as five clauses of one sentence, so the procedure has
a stated completion test rather than an implied one.

## 7. Materially relevant source-category treatment

**Prepared, under the heading *Pursued, not picked*.** Every category
**materially relevant** to the workflow is pursued where it **appears to exist**
and is **reasonably accessible within the authorized evidence boundary**, and
four things are recorded for each: whether it appears to exist; whether it is
reasonably accessible; whether it was actually obtained; and **why it was not**,
where it was not.

Two guards carry the determination's anti-convenience rule:

- **The silent-omission guard:** "A materially relevant category that appears to
  exist is never dropped because another category already produced a plausible
  trail."
- **The familiarity guard:** "**Prior familiarity with the workflow is not trail
  evidence** (A17 rule 6) and satisfies none of this" — cross-referenced to the
  existing rule so it is enforced by machinery the method already has, rather
  than by a new one.

## 8. Missing / unavailable / unobtained source treatment

**Prepared.** A missing category **does not void the procedure**. It bears on
**evidentiary sufficiency** — what findings may legitimately be made, the
confidence and context recorded through the method's existing non-scored
vocabulary, Instrument 5's marker, and, through A18, possibly advancement. The
missing category and its reason **stay visible**.

And the anti-inference rule, in the method's own words:

> **Silence is never read as agreement:** an unobtained source is never treated
> as though it would have corroborated what was obtained.

This sits beside A2 rule 6 (*unopposed is not corroborated*) rather than
restating it: rule 6 governs a claim with no opposing account; this governs a
trail category never obtained. **No new evidence class was created for either.**

## 9. Instrument 5 sufficiency treatment

**Prepared** as an addition to Instrument 5's existing recording rules,
displacing none, using **only** the three CS-17 values already in the method:

- **completed** — materially relevant categories obtained, no material gap
  remaining;
- **completed with named gaps** — procedure performed, one or more materially
  relevant categories unavailable or unobtained, remaining trail still supporting
  a meaningful workflow representation;
- **blocked by insufficient evidence** — available trail too incomplete to
  support one.

With the determination's guard against the failure mode this most invites:
**"An artifact-derived or partial trail is not marked *completed* automatically**,
and the marker never reports the procedure's completion in place of the
evidence's sufficiency."

## 10. A10 performed versus A18 advancement

**Prepared**, as its own short paragraph headed *Performed is not sufficient*:

> That the procedure was performed establishes nothing about whether the
> workflow is sufficiently understood. **A18 decides that, independently** —
> *A10 performed, gaps remaining, A18 not satisfied* is a permitted and
> expected state.

The permitted condition is stated **in the method** and not only in the
determination, because the failure this guards against is an application
reasoning from *procedure complete* to *understanding sufficient* without
noticing it has done so. **A18 is not amended**; its minimum-evidence element 5
and its outcome logic are untouched.

## 15. Repository / versioning treatment — the issue returned

**Development stopped before choosing, under the stop rule the instrument itself
carries** (`fd-0028` §2.9): *"If existing governance cannot represent an
authorized same-version semantic reconciliation truthfully without creating

**The governing rule.** Methodology Foundation v1 §6.4 lists nine dimensions
whose change makes a refinement **substantive**, and provides: **"Substantive
refinements require a new version and approval appropriate to the change."** The
A10 reconciliation changes **four** of the nine — meaning, requirements, required
evidence, limitations — and appears on **none** of the five non-substantive ones
(spelling, formatting, broken references, metadata errors, clerical
inconsistencies).

**Two further repository facts, neither of which settles it.**

1. **The program has applied §6.4 twice, both times as a new version.** Draft
   v3's header: "produced under §6.4 as a new version, never as an edit of a
   tested baseline." Draft v4's header says the same.
2. **Draft v4's own text forbids what the reconciliation requires** — *"this
   text is not altered in place"*; *"**Amendment:** by a later version under
   Methodology Foundation v1 §6.4, never by silent edit."* Applying the
   reconciliation in place requires **editing the sentence that forbids editing
   it in place**. Development will not make a self-authorizing edit of that kind
   on its own judgment.

**The instrument forbids Draft v5. §6.4 requires a new version. Both cannot hold
as the rule is written** — which is the stop condition.

**Four candidate representations are set out at
