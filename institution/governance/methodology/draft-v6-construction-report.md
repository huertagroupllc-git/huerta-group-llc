## 13. RQ-1 A15 implementation

**The determination as adopted:** an affirmative answer requires the
capability's existence to be **Observed** (A2). *Already exists* means
**established to exist** — expressly **not** described, claimed, planned,
referenced in a record, believed by a participant, or merely held in a reachable
system.

**Implemented as six paragraphs, each required by `fd-0032`:**

1. **The question itself** — carried unchanged.
2. **What answers it affirmatively** — the Observed requirement, the six
   excluded meanings, and **the reason the threshold is here and nowhere else**:
   an affirmative answer can stop this test before its substantive questions
   determine the outcome, so the condition it rests on is established rather
   than asserted.
3. **Observed keeps its ordinary meaning, and A2 is unchanged** — A2 rule 4
   restated as controlling: a record asserting a capability proves the record and
   its assertion, **not the condition**. Plus the non-ordering guard (§15) and
   V6-07's recording requirement.
4. **Where the assertion is Client-Stated** (§16).
5. **Access is not existence** (§18).
6. **Where existence is affirmatively established** — Draft v5's routing and
   termination limbs **carried unchanged**, now gated by the threshold; and
   **a records-only diagnosis may be unable to use the shortcut at all, and that
   blocks nothing.**

**Untouched in A15:** its seven questions; its *Rule* that the outcome may
legitimately be "no software intervention warranted"; its evidence-derived test
structure; its Output.

**This is the one change in Draft v6 that alters methodology semantics**, and it
does so under express determination.

## 15. A2 non-ordering preservation

**A2 is byte-identical to Draft v5** — its three classes and all six rules,
verified by direct comparison. **A2 was not opened.**

**The guard is stated inside A15, where the threshold lives:**

> **This is a threshold on one question, not a ranking of evidence.** A2 acquires
> no ordering from it, and Observed is not better evidence than Client-Stated
> here or anywhere else in this method; what this particular question needs is a
> condition **established**, and only one of A2's classes reports a condition
> established rather than asserted.

**That last clause is deliberate.** It grounds the requirement in what the
classes *mean* — Observed reports that the diagnostic saw it, Client-Stated that
someone said it — rather than in what they are *worth*. A definitional
distinction is not a hierarchy.

**Prohibited-term sweep.** Every occurrence in Draft v6 of *hierarchy · ranking ·
weighting · maturity level · percentage · traffic light* was inspected — nine in
total. **Every one is a prohibition or a denial.** None introduces one.

**And the risk this cannot test, recorded at `fd-0032` §4 Deficiency 3:** whether
an applier reading A15's Observed requirement nonetheless comes to treat Observed
as better evidence elsewhere is **behavioural**, and no consistency review reaches
it. It is named as the thing the next validation should look for.

## 16. Client-Stated capability treatment

Implemented in both A15 and Instrument 9, exactly as `fd-0032` §2.4 requires:

- The assertion is **recorded with its provenance and its class like any other
  evidence** — it does not become inadmissible or suspect.
- The terminating condition is recorded as **not established affirmatively**.
- **The ordinary test proceeds.**
- **Failing the shortcut does not mean the assertion is false**, and **does not
  mean anything should be built.**
- **The full test may still reach *existing system should be improved rather than
  replaced*** where the evidence developed inside it supports that outcome.

**This is the half of the determination most likely to be lost in reading**, and
it is written into both places rather than one.

## 17. Records-only diagnosis treatment

> **A records-only diagnosis may be unable to use the shortcut at all, and that

## 18. Access-versus-existence treatment

Alternative C is **expressly declined in the method's text**, not merely omitted:

> **Access is not existence.** That a system is reachable does not establish that

> limitations, coverage, what may be verified, and A18 — and it is not a

**Access's existing roles are preserved**, and the sentence says so rather than
leaving it to inference.

## 33. Recommended smallest meaningful Draft v6 validation stage

**Recommendation only. `fd-0032`: recommendation is not authorization.**

> **One bounded internal application of Draft v6, read-only, by an authoring
> context that did not draft it, against a subject where the A15 preceding
> question can actually arise — and, if at all obtainable, with participant
> access.**

**Why this and nothing larger.** Seven of the eight changes are clarification,
disclosure, renaming or presentation, and each needs only to be met once in an
application to show whether it is workable. **The eighth — RQ-1 — is the only
semantic change, and it is the reason a validation is warranted at all.**

**What it must specifically look for**, in descending order of importance:

1. **Whether the Observed threshold stays where it was put.** `fd-0032` §4
   Deficiency 3 names this as the determination's principal risk and as the one
   thing no consistency review can reach: does an applier reading A15's Observed
   requirement begin, anywhere else, to treat Observed as better evidence? **A2
   acquiring an ordering by drift would be a worse outcome than the ambiguity
   RQ-1 removed.**
2. **Whether the ordinary test actually proceeds** when a capability assertion is
   Client-Stated — or whether an applier reads the failed shortcut as a finding
   about the problem, which both A15 and Instrument 9 forbid.
3. **Whether A15's eight questions behave** now that they become operative in the
   records-only case where Draft v5 left them for-the-record only (§17). **This
   is wholly untested.**
4. **Whether V6-05's reference-carriage preserves attribution**, which requires an
   application with **at least one stakeholder session** to test the
   re-derivation limb at all.
5. **Whether the renamed *opposing source* field discriminates**, which needs
   accounts and therefore participant access.

**Why participant access is named as a condition rather than a preference.**
Three of the five targets above cannot be reached without it, and the
participant-facing half of this method remains unexercised in every version. **A
sixth records-only application would test items 1–3 and leave 4 and 5 exactly
where they are.** If participant access cannot be obtained, the honest course is
to run the application anyway and **record items 4 and 5 as unexercised** rather
than treat a partial result as a full one.
