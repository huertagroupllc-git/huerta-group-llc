# Organizational Diagnostic — Generation One: One-Sided Evidence Reporting Disposition (Stage 5)

**Classification: Institutional Record** — proposal record (evidentiary,
non-governing). The treatment commissioned by `fd-0023` §2.4 for the reporting
deficiency Stage 3 exposed and Stage 4 half-fixed. **Introduced here as
reporting composition only.** It amends no governed comparison vocabulary, and
the exact methodology-governance decision it would need in order to become a
standing requirement is returned at §8, not made.

## 1. The deficiency

Stage 3 produced sixteen comparisons classified **Unresolved**. At least five
of them are not unresolved at all: `C-45`, `C-55`, `C-59`, `C-63`, `C-64`. In
each, the relationship between the two halves is perfectly determinate — one
half addresses the subject and the other is silent. The records show four
places an open matter is kept and the account names none; the records show five
recorded execution stops and the account names none; the account names three
tools the records have never heard of.

*Unresolved — further evidence required* means the evidence is insufficient to
determine the **relationship** between the halves. Here the relationship is
known. Using the label anyway states something false about the diagnosis's own
evidence position, and it states it in the direction that makes the diagnosis
look more confused than it is — while hiding the more useful fact, which is
precisely **which side is silent**.

Stage 4 fixed the dangerous half of this — Draft v3's **A2 rule 5**, *unopposed
is not corroborated*, and Instrument 1's **opposing account** field. Those stop
one-sided evidence from being read as agreement. They do not tell a reader of
the comparison output what state a row is actually in.

## 2. The existing vocabulary, inspected

**The seven comparison classifications** (`fd-0020` §2.12, restated `fd-0022`
§2.4, restated `fd-0023` §2.4) — Corroboration · Contradiction · Difference in
Terminology · Missing Records · Unsupported Stakeholder Assertion · Record No
Longer Describes Actual Operation · Unresolved — Further Evidence Required.
Fixed by determination. None of them names a one-sided subject:

- *Corroboration* is forbidden here by A2 rule 5 and by `fd-0023` §2.4.
- *Contradiction* requires disagreement; silence is not disagreement, and
  A17 rule 4 forbids reading absence as proof of non-occurrence.
- *Missing records* fits only where the **account** identifies a condition and
  the records hold nothing — one of the two directions, and only where a
  positive claim exists to be unmatched.
- *Unsupported stakeholder assertion* likewise requires an assertion.
- *Unresolved* is what the vocabulary reaches for when nothing else fits — which
  is exactly what `fd-0023` §2.4 forbids.

**Draft v3's evidence model.** A2's three classes (Observed / Client-Stated /
Inferred) describe an **evidence item**. A2 rule 5 describes the **status of
corroboration**. Instrument 1's *opposing account* field records **what was
done about the second side**. Nothing in the model describes the **shape of a
comparison** — which is a property of the pair, not of either item.

**The gap, stated exactly:** the method can say what each item is, and whether
a second source was sought. It cannot say, in the comparison output, that this
comparison has only one side and which one.

## 3. The proposed treatment — the single-source qualifier

A qualifier carried **alongside** a comparison's existing classification,
never in place of it:

> **`single-source (account silent)`** — the records-derived half addresses the
> subject; the account does not.
>
> **`single-source (records silent)`** — the account addresses the subject; the
> records-derived half does not.
>
> Each carries: **opposing side sought / not sought / sought and unavailable**,
> with the reason, taken from Instrument 1's existing field.

And, for completeness of the shape, the state where neither side speaks:

> **`both silent`** — neither half addresses the subject. This is an evidence
> gap, not a comparison; it is recorded in the register and not classified.

**Form.** Three descriptive values and one existing field. No new
classification, no redefinition of any of the seven, no change to any
classification outcome, no scoring, no weighting, no ordering.

**Worked examples, from Stage 3's own record:**

| Row | Classification (unchanged) | Qualifier |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `C-45` | Unresolved | `single-source (account silent)` · opposing side sought (Q17 limbs), not supplied |
| `C-55` | Unresolved | `single-source (account silent)` · opposing side sought (Q21), answered on an adjacent subject |
| `C-63` | Unresolved | `single-source (account silent)` · opposing side sought (Q25 limbs), not supplied |
| `C-35-R6` | Unresolved | `single-source (account silent)` · opposing side sought twice (Q14, then the clarification), enumerated neither time |
| `C-70-R3` | Corroboration (as to *not established*) | `single-source (records silent)` on the intention limb · opposing side not sought — no record could hold an intention |

## 4. Descriptive reporting, not semantic classification

The qualifier states **which half of an already-classified comparison contains
evidence, and what was done about the other half**. Both are facts about the
diagnostic's own evidence-gathering, observable in its own register:

- It asserts nothing about the organization.
- It asserts nothing about the truth of the evidence it qualifies.
- It changes no classification's meaning, membership, or outcome.
- Removing it from a report removes information about the diagnosis, not about
  the subject.

That is the test for reporting composition rather than semantics, and the
qualifier passes it on every limb.

## 5. Why it does not imply corroboration

The qualifier names **the missing side**. Its entire content is that one half is
silent. Nothing in "the account did not address this" moves the other half
closer to being corroborated — and A2 rule 5 independently forbids the move:
*"Absence of disagreement is recorded as the absence of a second source, never
as agreement."* The qualifier is the reporting expression of that rule, not an
exception to it.

## 6. Why it does not imply contradiction

Silence is not disagreement. A17 rule 4: *"Absence of a record is not proof of
non-occurrence without a stated basis for that inference."* The qualifier
records that a side is silent and stops; it offers no basis for any inference
about what that side would have said, and it never appears on a row where the
two halves actually conflict.

## 7. Why it does not infer state of mind

The qualifier records **acts and outcomes**: was the other side sought; was it
supplied; if not, why not. Seeking is an act the diagnostic performed and can
evidence. Supplying or not supplying is an outcome observable in the record.
Neither is a judgement about the participant.

It contains no value for credibility, intent, certainty, honesty, motive, or
knowledge. It cannot say a participant withheld, forgot, avoided, or was
unaware — and `not sought` attaches the reason **to the diagnostic team**, not
to the participant. Where an answer was sought and not given, the qualifier
says exactly that and no more; Draft v3's Instrument 1 already forbids the team
assigning any category to a speaker's state of mind, and this qualifier adds
none.

**The narrowest form was chosen deliberately.** Richer candidates were
considered and rejected: a fourth value distinguishing *declined* from *not
answered* (requires reading intent); an evidence-strength scale (the global
instrument rules forbid scoring); a "coverage" percentage (an aggregate the
method does not require and cannot support from one application).

## 8. The methodology-governance decision required — returned, not made

| Level | Can Stage 5 do it? | Status |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Use the qualifier in Stage 5's own comparison outputs** — a composition choice by the office writing the report | **Yes.** It adds no classification, redefines none, and changes no outcome | **Done.** Used in the Q3/Q14/Q29 completion record and the HGP application record, both marked as reporting composition |
| **Make the qualifier a required field of every comparison record** — i.e. a rule in `stakeholder-evidence-procedure.md` §7 | **No.** That procedure records the comparison method under `fd-0020` §2.12 | **Returned to the Founder Office** |
| **Put the qualifier into the diagnostic itself** — a field in Instrument 1 or a rule in A2 | **No.** Draft v3 is frozen for Stage 5 and would in any case need a version under Methodology Foundation v1 §6.4 | **Returned to the Founder Office / Methodology room**, as a Draft v4 candidate |
| **Add an eighth comparison classification** | **No, and not proposed.** The set is fixed by determination, and `fd-0023` §2.4 prefers a descriptive treatment over an inferential classification | **Not sought** |

**The exact decision required:** *Is the single-source qualifier adopted as a
required element of the comparison record — and if so, does it enter through the
comparison procedure (reporting), or through the next draft of the method
(instrument field and evidence rule), or both?*

Development's reading, offered and not acted on: the reporting level is
sufficient for correctness and is the cheaper of the two; the instrument level
would make it survive a change of author. Nothing depends on the answer that
Stage 5 has already done.

## 9. What this does not fix

- It does not make one-sided evidence better evidence. Five Stage 3 rows are
  still one-sided after it, and one of them (`C-55`) concerns the most-evidenced
  stall in the organization's history.
- It does not tell a reader whether the silent side *could* have spoken. That
  needs the boundary and access facts Draft v3's A3 already carries separately.
- It has been exercised on two records by one office on one day. Whether it
  survives a second author is untested, which is itself an argument for the
  instrument level over the reporting level.
