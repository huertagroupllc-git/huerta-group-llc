## Part A — The Method

### A0. Purpose

To determine how Huerta Group can enter an organization it does not already
understand, establish an evidentiary baseline, diagnose its actual
organizational condition without prematurely prescribing a solution, determine
what meaning and authority must be established before design, and determine
whether any organizational, procedural, informational, managerial, or
technical intervention is warranted.

The diagnostic's product is **not a solution**. It is a confirmed,
evidence-labeled account of what the organization is, produces, decides, owns,
knows, and stalls on — with the questions the organization's own authority
must answer listed rather than answered, and with what the diagnostic team
could not see stated as plainly as what it could — sufficient to decide
*whether* to proceed to establishing meaning and authority, and *what* must be
governed first if design follows.

### A1. Organization neutrality and terminology discovery

The diagnostic must not assume: a Founder Office; Huerta Group's constitutional
architecture; a Corporate Headquarters; a Corporate Command Center;
Operational Records; Founder Attention; Huerta Group's venture model;
repository governance; a particular technology stack; software as outcome; a
single apex individual; **a distributed authority structure**; or Huerta Group
terminology.

**Terminology discovery precedes translation.** Before any neutral term below
is used for an organization, the organization's own words for the concept —
as spoken by its people and as written in its records — are recorded in
Instrument 1 (as *Observed* usage, with who used the word and where) and are
used in every instrument. The neutral terms exist so that the diagnostic
team can reason across organizations; they are never a vocabulary the
organization is asked to adopt, and each is **conditional**: it applies only
if the organization has the thing it names. "No equivalent found" is a
permitted and expected entry.

| Analytical concept (Huerta Group's own term, not to be used with a client) | Conditional neutral term — used only if the condition holds |
| --- | --- |
| Founder Office / the Founder | **the accountable authority** for a given decision right — *if* anyone holds it: a person, board, committee, or office; possibly several; possibly unresolved. Never presumed to be one person |
| Founder Attention | **the attention layer** — *if* some mechanism brings exceptions to an accountable authority; otherwise "no attention mechanism evidenced" |
| Corporate Headquarters | **the coordinating level** — *if* the organization assembles cross-unit state anywhere; otherwise "no coordinating level evidenced" |
| Headquarters representation / Operational Record | **a representation** — *if* some summary, register, dashboard, list, or report stands for facts owned elsewhere |
| Operational Instance | **the matter** — the real-world thing a representation stands for |
| Corporate Operations & Command Management | **the representation owner** — *if* anyone is responsible for keeping a representation current; otherwise "unowned" |
| Source function | **the source owner** — *if* anyone holds authoritative truth for a fact; otherwise "owner unresolved" |
| Venture | **a subordinate unit** — *if* any unit holds delegated authority whose detail is not reproduced at a coordinating level |
| Repository / determinations register / evolution log | **durable, organization-controlled, independently retrievable institutional memory** — by whatever mechanism, *if* any |
| Dependency / Blocker / Handoff | analytical distinctions between *relies on*, *cannot presently proceed because of*, and *expected next action transferred to* — aids, never imposed vocabulary |
| Material Change | **a consequential change** — one that alters what should be decided, done, or expected |
| Since Last Review | **what changed since an accountable authority last looked** — *if* any authority reviews at intervals |

Where the organization has different valid semantics for any of these, the
organization's terms are recorded and used, and the analytical distinction is
applied beneath them. Where the organization's term and the neutral term
diverge in meaning, the divergence is a finding, recorded in Instrument 1.

### A2. Evidence classification

Every material diagnostic claim carries exactly one of three classes:

- **Observed** — directly witnessed, documented, measured, demonstrated, or
  otherwise evidenced organizational condition.
- **Client-Stated** — condition asserted by an organizational participant or
  supplied institutional source but not yet independently corroborated.
- **Inferred** — analytical interpretation drawn from available evidence and
  explicitly distinguished from direct fact.

Rules:

1. Do not silently convert client-stated information into observed fact.
2. Do not silently convert inference into fact.
3. Preserve source and provenance for material diagnostic claims; record
   conflicting evidence rather than normalizing it away; record evidence
   limitations explicitly.
4. **A document proves that the document exists and contains an assertion.**
   A document supplied by the organization is *Observed* as to its existence
   and content, and *Client-Stated* as to the organizational condition it
   asserts, until the condition is corroborated by observation of operation.
   Where both facts matter, the register carries **two rows** (Instrument 1).

### A3. Evidentiary baseline (Stage 0)

**Purpose.** Establish what evidence exists, what does not, whose claims are
represented, what access the team actually has, and what limitations
constrain the diagnosis — before any finding is drawn.

**Required questions.**

1. What authoritative, operational, historical, and stakeholder evidence is
   available?
2. What evidence is unavailable?
3. Who supplied each material claim?
4. What can be independently verified?
5. What contradictions exist?
6. What access limitations materially affect confidence?

**Evidence requested at the outset** (recorded as requested, supplied, or not
supplied): the organization's governing and operating records as it names
them; **an inventory of the organization's people and roles, as the
organization describes them**; the systems and record sets in use; the
organization's own vocabulary for its work (A1).

**Procedure.**

1. Record the point in time and the state of every corpus, system, or record
   set inspected (identifier, version or date, access mode).
2. Inventory evidence by kind — authoritative (charters, policies,
   constitutions, approvals), operational (records, systems, logs), historical
   (prior audits, decisions, correspondence), stakeholder (accounts of the
   people who do the work).
3. For each material claim, record its source, its class (A2), and **how the
   team came to hold it** (A17 rule 6).
4. Record what was requested and not supplied, what exists but cannot be
   accessed, and what the organization says exists but cannot produce — each
   as a register row, never as an inference.
5. Record contradictions as contradictions; do not choose between them at this
   stage.
6. Record the access limitations and their effect on confidence.
7. Never fill a gap by inference. Unavailable evidence is recorded as
   unavailable; a missing record is not proof that the thing it would record
   does not exist.
8. **Stakeholder-access plan.** Record who will be asked, in what order, on
   what subjects, and what is done if access is refused, delayed, or
   unavailable. A diagnosis in which no stakeholder account is obtained is
   labelled **"records-only"** on every output it produces (A18).
9. **Citation check.** Every authority cited by a record that a material
   finding relies upon is checked for being held by the organization; for the
   wider corpus a sample is checked and the sampling basis is recorded.
   "Cited, not held" is a standard register row.
10. **Access statement.** Record, for each corpus, system, and person in the
    plan, whether access was **full, partial, or none**, and what "partial"
    excluded. The access statement is carried into the header of every
    instrument and into the A18 outcome.

**Outputs.** Evidence & Limitations Register (Instrument 1); initial
provenance map; known evidence gaps; stakeholder-access plan; access
statement.

### A4. Purpose and identity discovery

**Questions.** What does the organization say it exists to do? What does its
actual operation suggest it exists to do? What outputs, outcomes, or
obligations define its existence? Where do stated identity and observed
operation diverge?

**Evidence to request or observe.** Founding, governing, or strategic records
if any; public self-description; what the organization measures and rewards;
what its people say it is for, in their words; what it actually spends its
time on.

**Evidence-derived supplementary questions** (from the Discovery Report; not
part of the instrument's required set): Which of the organization's stated
capabilities are current, planned, or aspirational? Does the organization
distinguish these on its own surfaces?

**Non-prescription.** Do not rewrite mission, identity, strategy, or values
during diagnosis. Do not infer identity from public copy alone.

### A5. Outputs and value-creation discovery

**Questions.** What does the organization actually produce, enable, protect,
deliver, or sustain? Who depends upon those outputs? Which recurring
activities materially create value? Which activities exist mainly because of
legacy process or tooling?

**Evidence to request or observe.** The outputs themselves; who receives
them; what happens when they are late or wrong; the activities that consume
the most effort and their traceable connection to an output.

**Consumption classification.** Each output is recorded as **externally
consumed** (received by parties outside the organization) or **internally
consumed** (received by the organization itself — its own structure,
governance, systems, or capability), and the proportion of observed effort on
each is recorded — counted where records permit, characterized otherwise,
with the difference stated. Neither class is a judgement; the proportion is a
finding.

**Relevance test** (evidence-derived): for any activity, record, or
information the organization maintains — *what decision, coordination need,
risk, obligation, or action does it support?* Where there is no credible
answer, record that.

### A6. Authority and decision-rights discovery

**Questions.** Who formally holds authority? Who actually exercises authority?
Which decisions recur? Who can approve, reject, escalate, or redirect them?
Where are formal and informal decision rights misaligned? Where does ambiguity
cause delay or conflict?

**Evidence to request or observe.** Organization charts and delegations (as
Client-Stated); the record of actual decisions and who made them (Observed);
accounts from the people who wait for decisions; the escalation path as
practiced.

**Fields to establish, per decision class.** Reserved to whom; delegated to
whom and by what instrument; observed to be exercised by whom; **held by the
same actor as** which other decision classes; **unresolved** where no one can
say; configurable pending what; the statement that access, custody,
participation, and naming confer nothing.

**Role compression.** Consultation and escalation are recorded as **"none
evidenced (basis: …)"** or **"none formally required (instrument: …)"** —
never as "not applicable." Where the evidence resolves every decision class
to one actor, **role compression** is recorded as a finding in Instrument 2
with its continuity consequence and with the evidence that established it;
it is never assumed from the organization's size, founding, or the number of
names encountered.

**Distinction maintained throughout.** Authority (the right to decide) ≠
responsibility (the duty to see something done) ≠ representation (keeping a
summary current) ≠ execution (doing the work) ≠ custody (holding the systems
or records). Each is recorded separately, even where one person holds all
five.

**Output.** Authority / Decision-Rights Map (Instrument 2).

**Non-prescription.** Do not create authority for the organization. Do not
treat formal organization charts as proof of actual authority. Do not treat
one person's presence in every record as proof that authority is theirs.

### A7. Functions and responsibilities

**Questions.** What organizational functions actually exist? What
responsibilities are formally assigned? What work exists only through habit or
individual ownership? Where are responsibilities duplicated? Where are
responsibilities unowned? Where is responsibility confused with authority?

**Evidence to request or observe.** Formal assignments (Client-Stated); what
each function is observed to produce; work that stops when a particular
person is absent; work two functions both believe they own; work no one will
claim.

**Derivation rule** (evidence-derived). Functions are recorded from what the
evidence shows exists and does work — never enumerated from an org chart
alone. Each function records **what it is evidenced by**: work · document
only · name only · not evidenced. A function that appears in no evidence is
recorded as "not evidenced," not as absent.

**Three kinds of quiet** (evidence-derived). Distinguish: a function with no
evidence at all; a function with evidence but nothing consequential now; and a
function whose activity is consequential but invisible at any coordinating
level.

**Output.** Function / Responsibility Map (Instrument 3).

### A8. Recurring decisions

**Questions.** What decisions recur? What triggers them? What evidence do
they require? Who makes them? What dependencies affect them? Where does
decision latency arise?

**Evidence to request or observe.** A sample of recent decisions of each
recurring kind, from trigger to disposition; the queue of decisions awaiting
someone; **the cadence of decision-making** (when, how often, and on what
occasion decisions are actually taken); how long each waited and why, **where
records or accounts permit**.

**Measurement guidance** (evidence-derived). Count rather than characterize
where the evidence allows: how many decisions awaited an accountable
authority at a point in time; **across how many places they were recorded**
(Instrument 7); how many were rediscovered rather than tracked. Where latency
cannot be established, record "latency not establishable from available
evidence" and record the distribution instead.

### A9. Information and source-of-truth discovery

**Questions.** What information materially supports operation and
decision-making? Where does authoritative truth reside? Which systems are
representations rather than authorities? Where are duplicate or contradictory
records? What critical information exists only in conversation or individual
memory?

**Procedure.** For each information domain that supports a recurring decision
or workflow, record: the authoritative source (the one place a fact is
established); its owner; **whether the organization can itself retrieve it**;
every representational system that also shows the fact; duplicate or
conflicting sources; how current each representation is and who keeps it so;
access limitations; and the treatment any new layer would need to apply —
*hold directly* (only what the new layer would legitimately own),
*reference*, *summarize*, *do nothing*, or *never authoritative by existence*
(conversation, working notes, speculation).

**Rules** (evidence-derived). A representation is not an authority because it
is convenient, current, or widely used. Conversation and working context are
not authoritative merely because they exist. A summarized fact must remain
distinguishable from its authoritative record. Where the owner of a fact
cannot be identified, record "owner unresolved" — do not appoint one.

**Three facts never collapsed.** For every candidate authoritative source,
record separately, each with its own evidence class: (a) **the source
exists** and what it contains; (b) **a stakeholder is confident in it** — who,
and how confident; (c) **the organization treats it as authoritative** — by
what act, instrument, or practice. Confidence is not authority; existence is
neither.

**One matter, many representations.** Where one organizational matter appears
in several systems, the matter's identity is stated first and its
representations are then listed against it. It is recorded as **one matter
with N representations**, never as N matters. Where the identity of a matter
is itself disputed, the dispute is recorded as a contradiction.

**Output.** Source-of-Truth Inventory (Instrument 4).

### A10. Workflow, handoff, dependency and obligation discovery

**Questions.** What work recurs? Where does work cross boundaries? Who is
expected to act next? What relies upon what? Which dependencies actually
prevent meaningful progress? Which obligations or time boundaries materially
affect operation?

**Trail-first procedure.** Work is discovered from its trail before it is
discovered from its description: the artifacts produced, the records and
timestamps they left, the correspondence around them, and a stakeholder
walking the team through **the last actual instance** of the work, step by
step, in their own words. Only then is any described or documented process
recorded — as a separate row — and the two are compared. The team does not
begin from a process it already knows.

**Analytical distinctions** (used as aids, never imposed as vocabulary):

- *relies on* — one matter depends on another; work may still proceed;
- *cannot presently proceed because of* — the dependency currently prevents
  meaningful advancement; a time-bounded condition, not a property of the
  dependency;
- *expected next action transferred to* — who is now expected to act, on
  what, with what consequence; transfers expected action, never authority;
  acknowledgment is not completion;
- *obligation with a consequential time boundary* — a date whose passing has
  consequence, as distinct from a target date, a continuation requirement,
  or an expected follow-up.

**Questions for every handoff** (evidence-derived): what was handed off; to
whom; why; whether action is expected; current disposition; whether another
party is waiting on the result; whether escalation is required; **whether the
act has consequences the actor may not see** (A13).

**Next action, always.** For every matter, the **owner of the next expected
action** is recorded — or "unrecorded" — whether or not a handoff occurred.

**Neutrality rule.** Do not require Huerta Group's exact operational
vocabulary where the client has different valid semantics. Use the
distinctions as analytical aids, not imposed terminology.

**Observation preference** (evidence-derived). Where a workflow is
load-bearing, observe it as performed rather than accept its description;
record the difference.

**Output.** Workflow / Handoff / Dependency Inventory (Instrument 5).

### A11. Governance gap and governance-excess discovery

**Questions.** What matters require durable institutional authority? Where is
necessary authority missing or ambiguous? Where is governance duplicated or
contradictory? Where does excessive governance impede legitimate execution?
Which policies exist only as informal expectations?

**Excess test** (evidence-derived). Governance depth is measured against
operational load: governance that governs nothing operational, prescribes
mechanisms for work that does not occur, or is bypassed in practice by faster
direct authorization is recorded as excess or as drift — with the consequence
stated, not assumed to be harmful.

**Output.** Governance Gap / Excess Assessment (Instrument 8).

**Non-prescription.** Do not treat every governance gap as requiring formal
policy. Do not treat every friction point as a governance failure.

### A12. Institutional-memory risk discovery

**Principle** (mechanism-neutral). Institutionally significant knowledge should
become durable, organization-controlled, independently retrievable
institutional memory. Individual memory, conversation, contributor memory, and
transient working environments do not become authoritative merely because they
exist. A governed repository is one implementation of this principle, not the
universal one; the diagnostic assesses the principle, never a mechanism.

**Questions.** What critical knowledge exists only in individuals? What
institutional knowledge is trapped in transient systems? What knowledge would
be lost if a contributor left? Can the organization independently retrieve
significant decisions and operational history? Who controls durable
institutional memory? **What knowledge about the organization exists only
with its implementers, advisors, or vendors — including the diagnostic team
itself?**

**Evidence-derived supplementary questions.** Has the organization lost
significant records before, and how is that loss recorded? Is the chain by
which decisions travel from an accountable authority to the people who act
reliable, and is it recorded? Do corrections overwrite prior state or preserve
it?

**Output.** Institutional-Memory Risk Inventory (Instrument 6).

### A13. Systems and tooling discovery

**Questions.** Which systems support real operation? Which systems contain
authoritative information? Which systems duplicate or contradict one another?
Which tools impose obsolete workflows? Where is manual work appropriate? Where
is tooling absent but justified? **Which routine acts carry material,
irreversible, externally visible, governance, legal, financial, technical, or
institutional consequences — where the evidence warrants the question — and
does the organization's authorization practice carry that fact?**

**Rules** (evidence-derived). Current software is not proof of actual
workflow: observe the workflow, then the tool. A tool whose vocabulary cannot
express the organization's true condition is recorded as such (it is a finding
about the tool, not about the organization). Where a tool is used outside its
sanctioned path, record the path actually used. Which acts warrant the
consequence question is a judgement recorded with its basis; no list of
"risky acts" is imposed.

### A14. Operational friction and bottleneck discovery

**Questions.** Where is information repeatedly reconstructed? Where does
context get lost? Where do handoffs fail? Where does stale state persist?
Where are deadlines rediscovered instead of managed? Where are decisions
delayed? Where does work routinely bypass intended controls? **In how many
places is the same open matter kept?**

**Prompts** (evidence-derived, from six observed fragmentation problems):
state reconstruction (no single surface shows the current state; someone
assembles it); attention reconstruction (a fact exists somewhere without
becoming "action is required"); dependency invisibility (one party waits on
another without the wait being visible); temporal fragmentation (obligations
are documented correctly but invisible until someone revisits the record);
change detection (no concise answer to "what changed since I last looked");
context without authority distinction (working context is mistaken for
established fact).

**One-matter rule.** The A9 rule applies here: friction arising from one
matter kept in several places is recorded once, against the matter, with its
places counted and listed — never as several frictions.

**Output.** Operational-Friction Inventory (Instrument 7).

### A15. Technology-worthiness

**Questions.** Is the underlying organizational problem sufficiently
understood? Is the workflow recurring enough to justify technical support?
Would technology preserve or clarify institutional meaning? Would technology
merely automate an unclear or dysfunctional process? Does an authoritative
system already exist? Is a new representation layer justified? Can manual
operation provide better learning first?

**Rule.** The outcome may legitimately be "no software intervention
warranted."

**Preceding question.** Before the test is applied to a problem: *does a
capability already exist — built, standby, or dormant — awaiting a decision
rather than a build?* A "yes" routes to the outcome *existing system should
be improved rather than replaced* or to Instrument 10, and the test proceeds
only if the organization's authority has decided otherwise.

**Evidence-derived test structure.** (1) Is the need *recurring*, on
evidence rather than anecdote? (2) Has it *outgrown* the organization's
existing documents and systems as the sole mechanism — a judgement by the
accountable authority, recorded as such? (3) Is the problem *understood* well
enough that automating it would not freeze premature assumptions? (4) Is the
*meaning* of what technology would encode established by the organization's
authority? (5) Does an *authoritative system already exist* that should be
improved rather than duplicated? (6) What would remain *deliberately manual*,
and why? (7) What would the technology be *structurally unable to do*? A
"need established" outcome authorizes nothing; authorization is the
organization's separate act.

**Output.** Technology-Worthiness Test (Instrument 9).

### A16. Questions reserved to client authority

**Purpose.** Explicitly separate diagnostic findings from decisions Huerta
Group does not have authority to make on behalf of the organization.

**Examples.** Organizational purpose; strategy; capital allocation; legal
structure; reserved decision rights; risk acceptance; employment authority;
policy adoption; major operating-model choices.

**Rule.** A question reserved to the organization's authority is recorded with
why the diagnostic team cannot decide it, which authority holds it, what
depends on it, and the consequence if it remains unresolved — and is not
answered by the diagnostic.

**Output.** Questions Reserved to Client Authority (Instrument 10).

### A17. Evidence and provenance rules

1. Every material claim traces to its evidence and carries its class (A2).
2. Unavailable evidence is recorded as unavailable and never reconstructed from
   summaries, secondary description, memory, or conversation. A missing item
   is a register row with source **"not supplied," "not accessible,"** or
   **"not known to exist,"** and every finding that would have used it names
   the gap.
3. Contradictory evidence is preserved as contradictory, including
   disagreement between measurements; the diagnostic does not choose the
   flattering figure.
4. Absence of a record is not proof of non-occurrence without a stated basis
   for that inference.
5. Client-specific and confidential material is handled under Methodology
   Foundation v1 §7.5: it is never converted into general institutional
   methodology without review, abstraction, confidentiality protection, and
   approval.
6. **Admissibility.** Every evidence item records how the team came to hold
   it — *method step*, *prior knowledge*, or *volunteered*. An item held by
   prior knowledge supports **no finding** until it has been obtained through
   a method step (requested, observed, or supplied); until then it is
   recorded as prior knowledge so that the method — not the team's
   familiarity — is what is evaluated. A volunteered item is recorded with its
   class and traced to the step that would have requested it; it does not
   excuse the step.
7. **Custody.** The diagnostic team's own working material about the
   organization — notes, extracts, registers, drafts, transient workspaces —
   is itself inventoried in Instrument 6 under *implementer-side / advisor-side
   custody*, and its custody disposition (returned, retained under agreement,
   destroyed) is agreed with the organization before the diagnosis closes.
   Significant organizational evidence does not become dependent on the team.

### A18. Minimum diagnostic outputs and advancement logic

**Minimum outputs.** Evidence & Limitations Register; access statement;
identity-of-record statement (or its explicit absence); Authority /
Decision-Rights Map; Function / Responsibility Map; Workflow / Handoff /
Dependency Inventory; Source-of-Truth Inventory; Operational-Friction
Inventory; Institutional-Memory Risk Inventory; Governance Gap / Excess
Assessment; explicit exclusions (what must not enter any system; what should
remain manual; non-goals); Technology-Worthiness Test outcome; Questions
Reserved to Client Authority.

**Rule.** Diagnosis does not advance to design merely because data collection
is complete.

**Minimum evidence before design.** Material evidence limitations are
explicit; organizational purpose and outputs are sufficiently understood;
authority/decision-rights ambiguity relevant to the problem is visible; key
source-of-truth boundaries are identified; critical workflows and dependencies
are sufficiently understood; major diagnostic contradictions have been
preserved rather than hidden; questions requiring client authority are
separated from analytical findings; prescription would not depend primarily
upon unverified inference.

**How the judgement is recorded.** Each element of the minimum-evidence test
is answered with the same evidence classes as a finding (Observed /
Client-Stated / Inferred) and with its evidence. Where the diagnostic team
authored records it is judging, the outcome states **"author-of-records"**.
The outcome carries the **access statement** (A3) and, where no stakeholder
account was obtained, the label **"records-only"**.

**Possible outcomes.** Proceed to Establish Meaning & Authority. Request
additional evidence. Return unresolved questions to client authority. Narrow
the engagement problem. Conclude that no material intervention is presently
warranted.

**Constraints on outcomes.** A **records-only** diagnosis may produce only
*request additional evidence*, *return unresolved questions to client
authority*, or *narrow the engagement problem*. A diagnosis whose access
statement records "none" for a corpus, system, or person that a material
finding depends on cannot *proceed* on that finding.

### A19. Non-prescription rules

Do not prescribe software during initial discovery merely because software
could be built. Do not impose Huerta Group governance structures. Do not create
authority for the client. Do not resolve client strategic choices on the
client's behalf. Do not infer missing organizational facts merely to make the
diagnostic complete. Do not treat formal org charts as proof of actual
authority. Do not treat current software as proof of actual workflow. Do not
treat stakeholder consensus as proof of factual accuracy. Do not treat a
missing documented process as proof that no real process exists. Do not treat
every organizational friction point as a governance failure. Do not treat every
governance gap as requiring formal policy. Do not treat every manual activity
as an automation opportunity. Do not conceal contradictory evidence.

### A20. Known limitations

Self-application does not establish external validity. Huerta Group and HGP
are related cases, not independent external organizations. Founder-led
governance may not generalize to distributed authority structures. Repository
maturity in Huerta Group may create evidence access not available elsewhere.
Internal participants may share context unavailable in a real engagement.
Hostile, low-trust, political, or uncooperative stakeholder environments are
not fully testable internally. Large organizational scale is not validated.
Regulated-industry applicability is not validated merely through Huerta Group
use. Cross-cultural and multinational applicability remains untested.

Evidence-derived additions: at the freeze of this version, **no people-facing
procedure has been exercised** — the stakeholder-evidence procedures in A1,

## Part B — Provisional Working Instruments

**Status: PROVISIONAL WORKING FORMS — NOT STANDARDIZED.** Global rules: no
numerical scoring; no maturity levels; no weighted formulas; no automated
conclusion generation; no standardized consulting questionnaire; no software
implementation; no implication that these forms are final standards. Every
form is a table or list kept by hand; every entry cites evidence and carries an
evidence class; every form's header carries the access statement (A3 step 10)
and, where applicable, the label "records-only."

### Instrument 1 — Evidence & Limitations Register

- **Stage used:** A3 (opened first; maintained throughout).
- **Purpose:** the single place where every material evidence item, its
  source, its class, its limits, and the way the team came to hold it are
  recorded, so that every finding can be traced back and every gap is
  visible.
- **Minimum fields:** evidence identifier; source; source owner; evidence
  class (observed / client-stated / inferred); **obtained via (method step /
  prior knowledge / volunteered)**; subject; what the evidence supports;
  limitations; conflicts; confidence/context; provenance.
- **Recording rules:** one row per evidence item; "what the evidence
  supports" states the claim, not the conclusion; conflicts cross-reference
  the conflicting row; provenance records how and when the item was obtained
  and by whom; items requested but not supplied are rows with source "not
  supplied," "not accessible," or "not known to exist"; **"cited, not
  held"** is a row type for authorities a record cites that the organization
  does not hold; **the organization's own terms** (A1) are rows, with who
  used the term and where; a supplied document yields **two rows** where both
  its existence and its asserted condition matter (existence and content:
  observed; asserted condition: client-stated); a prior-knowledge row
  supports no finding until a method-step row exists for the same item;
  confidence/context is prose, never a score.

### Instrument 2 — Authority / Decision-Rights Map

- **Stage used:** A6 (also informs A8, A11, A16).
- **Purpose:** make visible who may decide what, who actually does, where
  the two diverge or no one can say, and where several decision rights rest
  on one actor.
- **Minimum fields:** decision/domain; formal authority; observed authority;
  **held by the same actor as…**; responsible function/role; required
  consultation/input; escalation path; evidence; ambiguity/conflict.
- **Recording rules:** "formal authority" is Client-Stated unless a governing
  instrument was observed; "observed authority" cites decisions actually
  made; "unresolved" is a permitted and expected value for either;
  consultation and escalation are recorded as "none evidenced (basis: …)" or
  "none formally required (instrument: …)" — **never "not applicable"**;
  where every row resolves to one actor, a **role-compression finding** is
  recorded against the map with its continuity consequence and the evidence
  that established it; the map never assigns authority — it records or
  records the absence of it; responsibility is recorded separately from
  authority even where the same person holds both.

### Instrument 3 — Function / Responsibility Map

- **Stage used:** A7.
- **Purpose:** record the functions that demonstrably exist, what they are
  assigned and observed to do, what evidences each, and where responsibility
  is duplicated, unowned, or held only by habit.
- **Minimum fields:** function; purpose; **evidenced by (work / document
  only / name only / not evidenced)**; formal responsibilities; observed
  responsibilities; key outputs; dependencies; overlap; unowned work;
  evidence.
- **Recording rules:** functions are derived from evidence of work, never
  enumerated from a chart; "unowned work" is recorded against the map as a
  whole where no function claims it; "observed responsibilities" that no
  formal assignment covers are flagged as habit- or individual-held; a
  function evidenced by name only is not a finding that it does nothing.

### Instrument 4 — Source-of-Truth Inventory

- **Stage used:** A9 (informs A13, A15).
- **Purpose:** for each information domain that matters operationally,
  establish the one authoritative source, its owner, whether the organization
  can retrieve it, and every representation of it — so that duplication,
  contradiction, staleness, and "authority by convenience" become visible.
- **Minimum fields:** information domain; authoritative source; source owner;
  **retrievable by the organization (yes / no / partial, with basis)**;
  representational systems; duplicate/conflicting sources; freshness
  concerns; access limitations; evidence.
- **Recording rules:** "authoritative source" may be "unresolved" — it is
  never appointed by the diagnostic; the three facts of A9 (exists /
  confidence / treated as authoritative) are recorded separately with their
  own classes; conversation, working notes, and speculation are recorded as
  "never authoritative by existence"; a representational system that is
  treated as authoritative in practice is recorded as such under "conflicts";
  one matter in several systems is one row with N representations (A9);
  "retrievable" is the pointer — the loss-risk analysis lives in Instrument
  6; freshness concerns are described qualitatively, never scored.

### Instrument 5 — Workflow / Handoff / Dependency Inventory

- **Stage used:** A10 (informs A8, A14).
- **Purpose:** record recurring work as it actually flows — who acts, what is
  handed to whom, who is expected to act next, what relies on what, what
  presently prevents progress, and which time boundaries carry consequence.
- **Minimum fields:** workflow/matter; trigger; participants; expected
  outputs; handoffs; **owner of the next expected action (or unrecorded)**;
  dependencies; blocking conditions; obligations/time boundaries; failure
  points; evidence.
- **Recording rules:** each workflow has a **trail row** (as discovered from
  artifacts and the last actual instance) before any **described row**;
  each handoff answers the eight handoff questions (A10); "dependencies" and
  "blocking conditions" are separate columns — a dependency is recorded as
  blocking only where evidence shows work cannot presently proceed;
  obligations record the consequence of the boundary passing; observed flow
  and described flow are both recorded where they differ.

### Instrument 6 — Institutional-Memory Risk Inventory

- **Stage used:** A12.
- **Purpose:** locate knowledge the organization would lose, cannot retrieve,
  or does not control — including knowledge held only by its implementers,
  advisors, vendors, or the diagnostic team — without prescribing how it
  should be kept.
- **Minimum fields:** knowledge/domain; current holder/location;
  organizational control; independent retrievability; authority status; loss
  risk; continuity consequence; evidence.
- **Recording rules:** "current holder/location" may be a person, a
  conversation, a transient system, a personal device, a durable
  organization-controlled record, or **implementer-side / advisor-side
  custody** (a row type: knowledge held by a consultant, developer, vendor,
  transient workspace, or implementer-controlled system — the diagnostic
  team's own material included, per A17 rule 7) — recorded as found;
  "organizational control" and "independent retrievability" are answered as
  yes / no / partial with the basis stated; "authority status" records
  whether the organization treats the item as authoritative and on what
  basis; loss risk and continuity consequence are prose.

### Instrument 7 — Operational-Friction Inventory

- **Stage used:** A14 (informs A15).
- **Purpose:** record where work is reconstructed, context lost, state stale,
  obligations rediscovered, controls bypassed, or one matter scattered — with
  frequency and consequence — as the evidence base for any later
  intervention.
- **Minimum fields:** friction; where observed; **places recorded (count and
  list)**; frequency; consequence; affected roles/functions; underlying cause
  known/unknown; current workaround; evidence.
- **Recording rules:** "underlying cause" is recorded as unknown where it is
  unknown; "current workaround" records what people actually do, including
  bypassing intended controls; frequency is counted where possible and
  characterized otherwise, and the difference is stated; one matter kept in
  several places is one row (A14).

### Instrument 8 — Governance Gap / Excess Assessment

- **Stage used:** A11.
- **Purpose:** identify where durable authority is missing, ambiguous,
  duplicated, contradictory, or in excess of operational load — as findings,
  never as policy.
- **Minimum fields:** subject; current authority/governance; gap / excess /
  ambiguity / conflict; operational consequence; evidence; client authority
  required; diagnostic recommendation only.
- **Recording rules:** every row ends in "diagnostic recommendation only" —
  a description of what the organization might decide, never a decision;
  "excess" is asserted only against evidence of operational load or bypass;
  "client authority required" names the authority, or "unresolved."

### Instrument 9 — Technology-Worthiness Test

- **Stage used:** A15 (after Instruments 4, 5, 7 for the problem in question).
- **Purpose:** decide, for one diagnosed recurring problem at a time, whether
  a technical intervention is warranted, premature, or unnecessary.
- **Preceding question:** does a capability already exist — built, standby,
  or dormant — awaiting a decision rather than a build? If yes, record the
  outcome *existing system should be improved rather than replaced* or refer
  the decision to Instrument 10, and continue only if the organization's
  authority has decided otherwise.
- **Minimum questions:** Is the problem recurring? Is the problem
  sufficiently understood? Is authoritative ownership understood? Would
  technology reduce material friction? Would technology duplicate an existing
  authoritative system? Would technology freeze premature assumptions? Can
  manual operation generate needed learning first? Is technical
  representation likely to preserve institutional meaning?
- **Permitted outcomes:** Technical intervention appears warranted. Manual /
  process intervention should precede technology. Existing system should be
  improved rather than replaced. Insufficient evidence. No technical
  intervention presently warranted.
- **Recording rules:** one test per problem; each question answered in prose
  with evidence, never as a score; the outcome is one of the five permitted
  outcomes and nothing else; "warranted" is a finding about need, never an
  authorization to build.

### Instrument 10 — Questions Reserved to Client Authority

- **Stage used:** A16 (maintained throughout).
- **Purpose:** keep the decisions that belong to the organization out of the
  diagnostic's findings, while making their dependencies and consequences
  visible.
- **Minimum fields:** question; why diagnostic team cannot decide it;
  responsible client authority; evidence/context; decision dependency;
  consequence if unresolved.
- **Recording rules:** the question is stated neutrally, without a
  recommended answer; "responsible client authority" may be "unresolved"
  and, if so, that is itself a finding for Instrument 2; "decision
  dependency" names the diagnostic outputs or later stages that wait on it.
