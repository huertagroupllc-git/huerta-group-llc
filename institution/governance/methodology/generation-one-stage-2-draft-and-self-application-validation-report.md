## 8. Evidentiary-baseline procedure (A3)

Purpose: establish what evidence exists, what does not, whose claims are
represented, and what limitations constrain the diagnosis — before any
finding is drawn. Required questions: what authoritative, operational,
historical, and stakeholder evidence is available; what is unavailable; who
supplied each material claim; what can be independently verified; what
contradictions exist; what access limitations materially affect confidence.
Procedure: (1) record the point in time and state of every corpus, system,
or record set inspected; (2) inventory evidence by kind — authoritative,
operational, historical, stakeholder; (3) record each material claim's source
and class; (4) record what was requested and not supplied, what exists but
cannot be accessed, and what the organization says exists but cannot produce;
(5) record contradictions as contradictions; (6) record access limitations and
their effect on confidence; (7) never fill a gap by inference — a missing
record is not proof that the thing it would record does not exist. Outputs:
Evidence & Limitations Register; initial provenance map; known evidence gaps.

## 9. Purpose and identity discovery (A4)

Questions: what the organization says it exists to do; what its actual
operation suggests it exists to do; what outputs, outcomes, or obligations
define its existence; where stated identity and observed operation diverge.
Evidence: founding, governing, or strategic records if any; public
self-description; what is measured and rewarded; what people say it is for;
what it actually spends its time on. Supplementary (evidence-derived): which
stated capabilities are current, planned, or aspirational, and whether the
organization distinguishes these on its own surfaces. Non-prescription: do
not rewrite mission, identity, strategy, or values; do not infer identity from
public copy alone.

## 10. Outputs and value-creation discovery (A5)

Questions: what the organization actually produces, enables, protects,
delivers, or sustains; who depends on those outputs; which recurring
activities materially create value; which exist mainly because of legacy
process or tooling. Evidence: the outputs; their recipients; what happens
when they are late or wrong; the activities that consume the most effort and
their traceable connection to an output. Relevance test: for any activity,
record, or information maintained — what decision, coordination need, risk,
obligation, or action does it support? Where there is no credible answer,
record that.

## 11. Authority and decision-rights discovery (A6)

Questions: who formally holds authority; who actually exercises it; which
decisions recur; who can approve, reject, escalate, or redirect them; where
formal and informal decision rights are misaligned; where ambiguity causes
delay or conflict. Evidence: organization charts and delegations (as
Client-Stated); the record of actual decisions and who made them (Observed);
accounts from the people who wait for decisions; the escalation path as
practiced. Fields per decision class: reserved to whom; delegated to whom and
by what instrument; observed to be exercised by whom; **unresolved** where no
one can say; configurable pending what; the statement that access, custody,
participation, and naming confer nothing. Distinction maintained: authority
≠ responsibility ≠ representation ≠ execution ≠ custody, each recorded
separately. Output: Authority / Decision-Rights Map. Non-prescription: do
not create authority for the organization; do not treat formal charts as
proof of actual authority.

## 12. Functions and responsibilities (A7)

Questions: what functions actually exist; what responsibilities are formally
assigned; what work exists only through habit or individual ownership; where
responsibilities are duplicated; where unowned; where responsibility is
confused with authority. Derivation rule: functions are recorded from
evidence of work, never enumerated from a chart; a function that appears in no
evidence is "not evidenced," not absent. Three kinds of quiet: no evidence
at all; evidence but nothing consequential now; consequential activity
invisible at the coordinating level. Output: Function / Responsibility Map.

## 13. Recurring decisions (A8)

Questions: what decisions recur; what triggers them; what evidence they
require; who makes them; what dependencies affect them; where latency arises.
Evidence: a sample of recent decisions of each kind from trigger to
disposition; the queue awaiting someone; how long each waited and why.
Measurement guidance: count rather than characterize where possible — how
many decisions awaited the accountable authority at a point in time; across
how many places they were recorded; how many were rediscovered rather than
tracked.

## 14. Information and source-of-truth discovery (A9)

Questions: what information materially supports operation and
decision-making; where authoritative truth resides; which systems are
representations rather than authorities; where duplicate or contradictory
records exist; what critical information exists only in conversation or
individual memory. Procedure per information domain: authoritative source;
owner; representational systems; duplicate or conflicting sources; currency
and who keeps it; access limitations; the treatment any new layer would apply
— hold directly (only what it would legitimately own), reference, summarize,
do nothing, or never authoritative by existence. Rules: a representation is
not an authority because it is convenient, current, or widely used;
conversation and working context are not authoritative merely because they
exist; a summarized fact must remain distinguishable from its authoritative
record; where an owner cannot be identified, record "owner unresolved" — do
not appoint one. Output: Source-of-Truth Inventory.

## 15. Workflow / handoff / dependency / obligation discovery (A10)

Questions: what work recurs; where it crosses boundaries; who is expected to
act next; what relies on what; which dependencies actually prevent meaningful
progress; which obligations or time boundaries materially affect operation.
Analytical distinctions used as aids, never imposed as vocabulary: *relies
on*; *cannot presently proceed because of* (a time-bounded condition, not a
property of the dependency); *expected next action transferred to* (transfers
expected action, never authority; acknowledgment is not completion);
*obligation with a consequential time boundary* (distinct from a target date,
a continuation requirement, or an expected follow-up). Seven questions for
every handoff: what was handed off; to whom; why; whether action is expected;
current disposition; whether another party is waiting; whether escalation is
required. Neutrality rule: do not require Huerta Group's exact operational
vocabulary where the client has different valid semantics. Observation
preference: where a workflow is load-bearing, observe it as performed rather
than accept its description; record the difference. Output: Workflow /
Handoff / Dependency Inventory.

## 16. Governance gap / excess assessment (A11)

Questions: what matters require durable institutional authority; where
necessary authority is missing or ambiguous; where governance is duplicated or
contradictory; where excessive governance impedes legitimate execution; which
policies exist only as informal expectations. Excess test: governance depth
is measured against operational load — governance that governs nothing
operational, prescribes mechanisms for work that does not occur, or is
bypassed in practice by faster direct authorization is recorded as excess or
drift, with the consequence stated rather than assumed harmful. Output:
Governance Gap / Excess Assessment. Non-prescription: not every gap requires
formal policy; not every friction point is a governance failure.

## 17. Institutional-memory risk assessment (A12)

Principle (mechanism-neutral, §43): institutionally significant knowledge
should become durable, organization-controlled, independently retrievable
institutional memory; individual memory, conversation, contributor memory,
and transient working environments do not become authoritative merely because
they exist; a governed repository is one implementation, not the universal
one. Questions: what critical knowledge exists only in individuals; what is
trapped in transient systems; what would be lost if a contributor left; can
the organization independently retrieve significant decisions and history;
who controls durable memory. Supplementary: prior loss and how it is
recorded; reliability of the chain from the accountable authority to the
people who act; whether corrections overwrite or preserve prior state.
Output: Institutional-Memory Risk Inventory.

## 18. Systems / tooling assessment (A13)

Questions: which systems support real operation; which contain authoritative
information; which duplicate or contradict one another; which tools impose
obsolete workflows; where manual work is appropriate; where tooling is absent
but justified. Rules: current software is not proof of actual workflow —
observe the workflow, then the tool; a tool whose vocabulary cannot express
the organization's true condition is recorded as a finding about the tool;
where a tool is used outside its sanctioned path, record the path actually
used.

## 19. Operational-friction assessment (A14)

Questions: where information is repeatedly reconstructed; where context is
lost; where handoffs fail; where stale state persists; where deadlines are
rediscovered instead of managed; where decisions are delayed; where work
routinely bypasses intended controls. Prompts (from six observed fragmentation
problems): state reconstruction; attention reconstruction; dependency
invisibility; temporal fragmentation; change detection; context without
authority distinction. Output: Operational-Friction Inventory.

## 20. Technology-worthiness method (A15)

Questions: is the underlying problem sufficiently understood; is the workflow
recurring enough to justify technical support; would technology preserve or
clarify institutional meaning; would it merely automate an unclear or
dysfunctional process; does an authoritative system already exist; is a new
representation layer justified; can manual operation provide better learning
first. Rule: the outcome may legitimately be "no software intervention
warranted." Evidence-derived test structure: (1) recurring on evidence; (2)
outgrown existing documents and systems — a judgement by the accountable
authority, recorded as such; (3) understood well enough not to freeze
premature assumptions; (4) meaning established by the organization's
authority; (5) an authoritative system already exists that should be improved
rather than duplicated; (6) what remains deliberately manual and why; (7)
what the technology would be structurally unable to do. "Need established"
authorizes nothing. Output: Technology-Worthiness Test.

## 21. Evidence / provenance rules (A17)

(1) Every material claim traces to its evidence and carries its class. (2)
Unavailable evidence is recorded as unavailable and never reconstructed from
summaries, secondary description, memory, or conversation. (3) Contradictory
evidence is preserved as contradictory, including disagreement between
measurements. (4) Absence of a record is not proof of non-occurrence without
a stated basis. (5) Client-specific and confidential material is handled under
Methodology Foundation v1 §7.5. (6) The team records where it benefited from
prior knowledge the method did not formally obtain.

## 22. Observed / client-stated / inferred classification (A2)

Observed — directly witnessed, documented, measured, demonstrated, or
otherwise evidenced condition. Client-Stated — asserted by a participant or
supplied institutional source, not yet independently corroborated. Inferred —
analytical interpretation explicitly distinguished from direct fact. Rules: no
silent conversion of client-stated to observed or of inference to fact;
provenance preserved; conflicts recorded; limitations explicit. A supplied
document is Observed as to existence and content and Client-Stated as to the
condition it asserts, until corroborated by observation of operation.

## 23. Minimum diagnostic outputs and advancement logic (A18)

Minimum outputs: Evidence & Limitations Register; identity-of-record
statement or its explicit absence; Authority / Decision-Rights Map; Function /
Responsibility Map; Workflow / Handoff / Dependency Inventory; Source-of-Truth
Inventory; Operational-Friction Inventory; Institutional-Memory Risk
Inventory; Governance Gap / Excess Assessment; explicit exclusions;
Technology-Worthiness outcome; Questions Reserved to Client Authority. Rule:
diagnosis does not advance to design merely because data collection is
complete. Minimum evidence before design: limitations explicit; purpose and
outputs sufficiently understood; authority ambiguity visible; source-of-truth
boundaries identified; workflows and dependencies sufficiently understood;
contradictions preserved; client-authority questions separated; prescription
would not depend primarily on unverified inference. Possible outcomes:
proceed to Establish Meaning & Authority; request additional evidence; return
unresolved questions to client authority; narrow the engagement problem;
conclude that no material intervention is presently warranted.

## 24. Questions Reserved to Client Authority (A16)

Purpose: separate diagnostic findings from decisions Huerta Group has no
authority to make on the organization's behalf. Examples: organizational
purpose; strategy; capital allocation; legal structure; reserved decision
rights; risk acceptance; employment authority; policy adoption; major
operating-model choices. Rule: recorded with why the team cannot decide it,
which authority holds it, what depends on it, and the consequence if
unresolved — and not answered.

## 25. Explicit non-prescription rules (A19)

Do not prescribe software during initial discovery merely because software
could be built. Do not impose Huerta Group governance structures. Do not
create authority for the client. Do not resolve client strategic choices on
the client's behalf. Do not infer missing organizational facts merely to make
the diagnostic complete. Do not treat formal org charts as proof of actual
authority. Do not treat current software as proof of actual workflow. Do not
treat stakeholder consensus as proof of factual accuracy. Do not treat a
missing documented process as proof that no real process exists. Do not treat
every organizational friction point as a governance failure. Do not treat
every governance gap as requiring formal policy. Do not treat every manual
activity as an automation opportunity. Do not conceal contradictory evidence.

## 26. Known limitations (A20)

Self-application does not establish external validity. Huerta Group and HGP
are related cases, not independent external organizations. Founder-led
governance may not generalize to distributed authority structures.
Repository maturity in Huerta Group may create evidence access not available
elsewhere. Internal participants may share context unavailable in a real
engagement. Hostile, low-trust, political, or uncooperative stakeholder
environments are not fully testable internally. Large organizational scale is
not validated. Regulated-industry applicability is not validated merely
through Huerta Group use. Cross-cultural and multinational applicability
remains untested. Added: no stakeholder interview has been conducted under
this method; no instrument has been used more than once; the evidence base is
weeks old and single-operator.

## 27. Working Instrument 1 — Evidence & Limitations Register

Stage A3, maintained throughout. Minimum fields: evidence identifier; source;
source owner; evidence class (observed / client-stated / inferred); subject;
what the evidence supports; limitations; conflicts; confidence/context;
provenance. Rules: one row per item; "supports" states the claim, not the
conclusion; conflicts cross-reference; provenance records how, when, and by
whom obtained; items requested but not supplied are rows with source "not
supplied"; confidence is prose, never a score.

## 28. Working Instrument 2 — Authority / Decision-Rights Map

Stage A6. Minimum fields: decision/domain; formal authority; observed
authority; responsible function/role; required consultation/input; escalation
path; evidence; ambiguity/conflict. Rules: formal authority is Client-Stated
unless a governing instrument was observed; observed authority cites decisions
actually made; "unresolved" is permitted and expected; the map never assigns
authority; responsibility is recorded separately from authority even where one
person holds both.

## 29. Working Instrument 3 — Function / Responsibility Map

Stage A7. Minimum fields: function; purpose; formal responsibilities; observed
responsibilities; key outputs; dependencies; overlap; unowned work; evidence.
Rules: functions derived from evidence of work; unowned work recorded against
the map where no function claims it; observed responsibilities with no formal
assignment flagged as habit- or individual-held.

## 30. Working Instrument 4 — Source-of-Truth Inventory

Stage A9. Minimum fields: information domain; authoritative source; source
owner; representational systems; duplicate/conflicting sources; freshness
concerns; access limitations; evidence. Rules: "authoritative source" may be
"unresolved" and is never appointed; conversation, notes, and speculation are
"never authoritative by existence"; a representation treated as authoritative
in practice is recorded under conflicts; freshness described qualitatively.

## 31. Working Instrument 5 — Workflow / Handoff / Dependency Inventory

Stage A10. Minimum fields: workflow/matter; trigger; participants; expected
outputs; handoffs; dependencies; blocking conditions; obligations/time
boundaries; failure points; evidence. Rules: each handoff answers the seven
questions; dependencies and blocking conditions are separate columns —
blocking recorded only where evidence shows work cannot presently proceed;
obligations record the consequence of the boundary passing; observed and
described flow both recorded where they differ.

## 32. Working Instrument 6 — Institutional-Memory Risk Inventory

Stage A12. Minimum fields: knowledge/domain; current holder/location;
organizational control; independent retrievability; authority status; loss
risk; continuity consequence; evidence. Rules: holder/location recorded as
found (a person, a conversation, a transient system, a personal device, a
durable record); control and retrievability answered yes / no / partial with
basis; authority status records whether the organization treats the item as
authoritative and on what basis; risk and consequence are prose.

## 33. Working Instrument 7 — Operational-Friction Inventory

Stage A14. Minimum fields: friction; where observed; frequency; consequence;
affected roles/functions; underlying cause known/unknown; current workaround;
evidence. Rules: cause recorded as unknown where unknown; workaround records
what people actually do, including bypassing controls; frequency counted where
possible and characterized otherwise, with the difference stated.

## 34. Working Instrument 8 — Governance Gap / Excess Assessment

Stage A11. Minimum fields: subject; current authority/governance; gap / excess
/ ambiguity / conflict; operational consequence; evidence; client authority
required; diagnostic recommendation only. Rules: every row ends in a
recommendation, never a decision; excess asserted only against evidence of
load or bypass; client authority named or "unresolved."

## 35. Working Instrument 9 — Technology-Worthiness Test

Stage A15. Minimum questions: recurring? sufficiently understood?
authoritative ownership understood? would technology reduce material
friction? duplicate an existing authoritative system? freeze premature
assumptions? can manual operation generate needed learning first? is
technical representation likely to preserve institutional meaning? Permitted
outcomes: technical intervention appears warranted; manual/process
intervention should precede technology; existing system should be improved
rather than replaced; insufficient evidence; no technical intervention
presently warranted. Rules: one test per problem; answers in prose with
evidence, never scored; "warranted" is a finding about need, never an
authorization to build.

## 36. Working Instrument 10 — Questions Reserved to Client Authority

Stage A16, maintained throughout. Minimum fields: question; why the
diagnostic team cannot decide it; responsible client authority;
evidence/context; decision dependency; consequence if unresolved. Rules:
stated neutrally without a recommended answer; "responsible authority" may be
"unresolved," which is itself a finding for Instrument 2; dependency names
what waits on it.

## 43. Institutional-memory principle refinement

Superseded provisional wording (Discovery Report §12, principle 11):
"Institutional memory is repository-native." Refined provisional principle
(`fd-0019` §2.9): "Institutionally significant knowledge should become
durable, organization-controlled, independently retrievable institutional
memory. Individual memory, conversation, contributor memory, and transient
working environments do not become authoritative merely because they exist."
Implementation boundary: Huerta Group's governed repository is one
implementation of that principle, not the universal implementation. The draft
carries the refined principle verbatim (A12) and the dry run applied it
mechanism-neutrally (OK-8). The refinement was corroborated by this stage's
own evidence twice: four accepted instruments existed only in Development's
transient session records; and the organization's highest authority (the
constitutional text) is not independently retrievable by the organization.

Exposed (`AS-1`). The team had every record; the draft's baseline procedure
assumes the team can inventory the organization's records and says nothing
about a client-supplied subset. Every "Observed" register row benefited from
this. Correction proposed: C-2 (check that every cited authority is held) and

open.
