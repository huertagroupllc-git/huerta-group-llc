# Organizational Diagnostic — Generation One: Stage 6 Replication Comparison

**Classification: Institutional Record** — validation evidence (evidentiary,
non-governing). The post-seal comparison authorized by `fd-0024` §2.9, between
the Stage 5 first-author application and the Stage 6 second-author replication
of the **same frozen Draft v3** against the **same subject at `e28f12a`**.

- **Performed after sealing.** The second-author result was frozen at commit
  `9260509`, combined digest `d07b88e7…c29f0bd8`, before this comparison began.
  The second author never received any Stage 5 material and has still not seen
  it.
- **No score.** No numerical reproducibility score, percentage agreement,
  weighted consistency index, or method maturity score is created
  (`fd-0024` §2.9). Material agreement or disagreement on substantive findings
  is the evidence.
- **Leaked-identifier exclusion applied.** Per the rule adopted from the second
  author's own independence statement §7.2, convergence on any identifier it
  already held — the commit hashes, `e28f12a`, `fd-` numbers, `39/39`,
  `Q1–Q30` — is **excluded** from the reproducibility evidence. Nothing below
  rests on such a convergence.
- **Who wrote this.** The **first** author. The office whose work is being
  compared is the office comparing it. That is the fourth independence defect
  recorded at `d8-evidence-base-qualification.md` §5.4, it is not cured by the
  seal, and it should be read as bearing hardest on §4 and §6 below, where the
  first author's work is found wanting.

## 1. A defect in this test's own design, recorded first

**The two authors were not given the same evidence boundary, and the first
author caused that.**

`fd-0024` §2.9 required the replication to run across *"the same authorized HGP
diagnostic boundary used in Stage 5."* To protect isolation, the first author's
brief forbade the second author the entire parent `institution/` directory.
Stage 5 had **used** that directory: its authority findings rest substantially
on FD-V1 §§7–23 — the venture charter, its delegation and reserved-authority
lists, its decision-routing table, and its record that the venture steward is
the Founder.

Isolation and boundary-equality were in direct conflict, the first author chose
isolation, and **did not record the trade-off at the time.** The consequence is
concentrated in one dimension (§4) and is classified there as an
**access-boundary effect**, not as a disagreement between the authors.

A cleaner design was available and was not seen: supply the second author with
the parent-side venture governance records as part of its package — they contain
no Stage 5 material — while still withholding the methodology directory.

## 2. The seventeen dimensions

### 2.1 Evidence selected — **EVIDENCE-SELECTION DIFFERENCE**

Both authors read the same corpus classes: the governing canon, the twenty
blueprints, the thirteen as-built records, the validation program and its
registers, the migration set, the commit history, the code inventory. Both
recorded a two-pass access statement and both recorded "none" for the hosted
database, the live deployment, author-confidential material and every person.

**Where they differ, the second author was more thorough on named-but-unheld
sources.** It recorded, as distinct access rows, four classes the first author
did not record at all: the **Founder Office instruments cited across 36 files**
and held nowhere; a **fourth constitution** cited in 11 documents and not
present; a **Claude Design Handoff Evaluation** named in `PROVENANCE.md`; and
the **Reviewer v3/v4 verification scripts** named in the globalization records.
Each is a source the corpus itself says exists and does not contain — precisely
what A3 step 9's "cited, not held" row type exists to capture. The first author
recorded the general condition and not the inventory.

The first author held one class the second could not: the **parent-side venture
governance records** (§1).

### 2.2 Terminology discovered — **SUBSTANTIVELY REPRODUCED**

Both independently located the subject's **ratified terminology canon**, both
recorded its enforcement into code as pinned by test, and both used the
subject's words untranslated throughout.

**The strongest single convergence in the comparison:** both authors
independently found, and both recorded as significant, that the subject
**removes "Source of Truth" from its interface** as software jargon while
permitting it in engineering prose — the exact phrase the method's own
Instrument 4 is named after. The first author called it "the sharpest result";
the second gave it a numbered finding (F-20). Neither could have taken it from
the other, and it is not a leaked identifier.

### 2.3 Source-of-truth findings — **INTERPRETATION DIFFERENCE**

Both found that the subject legitimately operates **more than one** authoritative
store and neither collapsed them; both recorded the required agreement between
the migration files and the hosted ledger; both recorded the drift-guard tests.
That much is materially equivalent.

**They diverge on whether the boundaries are *established*.** The first author
answered A18's element 4 **yes**, and called it "the strongest result of the
application." The second answered **partial**, on grounds the first did not
apply: A9 requires three facts kept separate, and **fact (b) — stakeholder
confidence — is unobtainable for every domain without accounts**; the permanent
record's owner is unresolved; and **capability status has no authoritative
source and no establishing act at all**.

**Development's assessment, against its own work:** the second author's reading
is better supported by A9's text. "Identified" and "established" are different,
A9 makes them different, and the first author's answer treated a well-mapped
inventory as an established boundary. Recorded as an interpretation difference
because both readings are available on the text — and noted as one where the
second author was more faithful to it.

### 2.4 Authority findings — **ACCESS-BOUNDARY EFFECT**, with an interpretation component

**The largest divergence in the comparison.**

- **First author:** recorded **role compression** — every named authority
  resolving in practice to one person — citing FD-V1 §21's record that the
  venture steward is the Founder, and recorded concentration as a dated observed
  condition.
- **Second author (F-10):** **declined the inference and recorded that it
  declined it.** With the venture corpus alone, the evidence resolves most
  decision classes to *no identified actor*; it recorded observed authority
  **unresolved for fourteen of eighteen** decision classes, and cited A6's
  non-prescription rule — *"Do not treat one person's presence in every record
  as proof that authority is theirs"* — as forbidding the step. It states
  explicitly that **nothing in its result should be read as finding authority
  either concentrated or distributed.**

**Primary classification: access-boundary effect.** The record that carried the
first author's finding was outside the second author's boundary (§1). Given only
what the second author had, its refusal is the correct application of A6.

**Secondary, and not excused by the boundary:** even holding FD-V1, that record
is a parent-plane document asserting a condition about the venture — *Observed*
as to its content, *Client-Stated* as to the condition (A2 rule 4). The first
author cited it and then stated role compression as a finding without carrying
that class into the finding. The second author's discipline here is better.

**Both nonetheless converged on the two acts with the widest consequence** —
putting a change onto `main` (which is deployment), and granting the staff role
— and both recorded that neither has a recorded authorizing authority. The
second author states it more sharply (F-11); the first recorded the same
condition for the push under A13.

### 2.5 Function/responsibility findings — **SUBSTANTIVELY REPRODUCED**

Both derived functions from evidence of work rather than from any chart. Both
recorded a function evidenced only by citation or name.

**The reproduction that matters:** both recorded habit-held responsibility as
**"capability not exercised — observation unavailable"** — the exact disposition
the method prescribes — rather than reporting that no habit-held work was found.
Two authors, no contact, same label, same reason.

### 2.6 Recurring-decision findings — **MATERIALLY EQUIVALENT — DIFFERENT WORDING**

Both recorded **latency not establishable from available evidence** and recorded
the distribution instead, as A8 permits. Both observed that where cadence is
visible it is **same-day**: the first from a verification session that observed,
corrected and closed in one sitting; the second as a general finding (F-17).

### 2.7 Workflow/handoff/dependency findings — **METHOD AMBIGUITY**

- **First author:** completed one trail row — a nine-step trail from a
  production refusal through a failed fix to closure — and answered element 5
  **partially**.
- **Second author:** A10's trail-first procedure **could not be completed for
  any workflow**; two workflows blocked entirely; element 5 **not met**.

**The cause is in the method's text, not in either author.** A10 says work is
discovered from its trail — *"the artifacts produced, the records and timestamps
they left, the correspondence around them, **and** a stakeholder walking the team
through the last actual instance."* The list is conjunctive. The first author
treated the artifact trail as sufficient; the second read the stakeholder
walkthrough as required and therefore blocking. **The method does not say which
is right**, and two faithful readings produce different element-5 answers and
different instrument completeness.

Both agree on the underlying fact: **A10's observation-of-work limb has never
been exercised in any application of this method**, and both cite A20 for it.

### 2.8 Governance gap/excess findings — **SUBSTANTIVELY REPRODUCED**

- Both identified the **three constitutions reading "proposed, awaiting
  approval" while being treated as binding** as the principal governance
  ambiguity.
- Both **found the subject's stated blueprint convention** — that older
  blueprints keep their original status lines as history — and both used it to
  avoid reading eleven stale status lines as eleven findings.
- Both **declined to assert governance excess**, and both gave an evidentiary
  reason rather than a judgement.

The second author went one step further, and correctly: it recorded that the
stated convention **covers blueprints and does not reach** the globalization
index or the three constitutions. The first author recorded those as separate
items without connecting them to the convention's limit.

### 2.9 Institutional-memory findings — **MATERIALLY EQUIVALENT**, with one first-author miss

Both recorded the same principal risk: **Founder Office directives cited by the
venture's records and held nowhere**, against the subject's own constitutional
rule that nothing important lives only in conversation. The second author
quantified the spread across the corpus; the first author noted the condition.

Both recorded the hosted store's durability as **not established**.

**The first author missed one thing the second caught (F-30):** A17 rule 7
requires the diagnostic team's own custody disposition to be **agreed with the
organization before the diagnosis closes**. No party was available to either
author. The second author recorded this as **a defect in its own closure** —
"it closes unagreed" — rather than resolving it unilaterally. **Stage 5 closed
unagreed too and did not say so.** The finding applies to the first author's
work and is adopted here.

### 2.10 Systems/tooling findings — **SUBSTANTIVELY REPRODUCED**, with a first-author omission

Both recorded the same inventory shape, the same single sanctioned path to
production, and — independently — the same set of acts carrying irreversible or
externally visible consequence, headed by **push to `main` being deployment**.

**A contradiction the first author had the data for and did not record:** the
subject's README states "**Nine** runtime dependencies"; the manifest lists
**fourteen**. The first author counted fourteen, wrote fourteen, and never
compared it to the stated figure. The second author recorded it as CX-03. This
is not an access difference and not an interpretation difference — it is a miss.

### 2.11 Operational-friction findings — **MATERIALLY EQUIVALENT — DIFFERENT WORDING**

Both found the same dominant friction and both recorded it once per matter with
its places counted, as A14 requires: **capability status kept in several places
of different vintages**. The second author counted five places; the first
recorded three matters with two representations each. Same condition, different
decomposition, neither wrong under the rule.

### 2.12 Technology-worthiness outcome — **INTERPRETATION DIFFERENCE**

- **First author:** *Insufficient evidence*, on the ground that the premise the
  platform rests on is asserted and not evidenced.
- **Second author:** *Existing system should be improved rather than replaced*
  for the one testable problem, and *insufficient evidence* for every other
  candidate.

Both are among the five permitted outcomes; both applied the preceding question
about existing or standby capability; neither concluded that a build was
warranted. The second author records that the preceding question **changed its
answer** — "[w]ithout that question I would have written a recommendation."

Recorded as an interpretation difference, not a divergence in substance:
neither author recommended anything be built.

### 2.13 Questions reserved to subject authority — **MATERIALLY EQUIVALENT — DIFFERENT WORDING**

First author: six. Second author: nine. Both stated neutrally, both with no
recommended answer, both with the holding authority named or recorded
unresolved.

**Overlapping in substance:** the approval status of the three constitutions;
whether there is an authoritative source for "what exists today"; whether the
cited Founder Office directives should be durably preserved; what the validation
program's single observation means; the durability of the permanent record.

**Second author only:** whether the seven named authorities are distinct actors
(RQ-01); what an "(Opus 4.8)" designation in the corpus records and whether the
approvals it carries stand (RQ-03); who holds the right to put a change onto
`main` (RQ-06); whether granting the staff role should leave a durable record
(RQ-07).

**First author only:** whether the blueprint convention should be made legible
inside the blueprints themselves.

### 2.14 Stakeholder-evidence boundary — **SUBSTANTIVELY REPRODUCED**

**Both authors hit it, both stopped, both derived their own neutral questions,
and neither sought or supplied a single answer.** First author: ten question
sets. Second author: twelve, each decomposed into limbs before being put, as A3
step 8 requires.

Topic convergence is high and independent: people and who holds what authority;
the organization's boundary; where a participant actually goes for an answer;
work as it actually flows; the status of the constitutions; the permanent
record's durability; what the validation program's evidence means; what awaits a
decision rather than a build.

**One the second author asked and the first did not: custody of the diagnosis's
own material** (SQ-11) — the question A17 rule 7 requires and Stage 5 omitted
(§2.9).

### 2.16 Single-source evidence treatment — **METHOD AMBIGUITY**

The second author was not told the Stage 5 phrases existed, and **did not
independently invent them**. It met the same evidentiary condition — evidence on
one side only, with the absence of the other side known — and handled it
differently: it recorded per-row "opposing account" values as A2 rule 5
requires, and then recorded (MO-09) that the field **degenerates when the answer
is uniform**, because every row reads "not sought — no account obtainable."

**What this is evidence of, and what it is not.** It is evidence that the
condition is real and that a second author hits it unprompted. It is **not**
evidence that the Stage 5 qualifier is the right treatment: a second author,
free to reach for anything, reached instead for a criticism of the Stage 4
mechanism. That is an argument for continuing to hold the qualifier as
*available reporting composition* rather than adopting it as required — which is
where `fd-0024` §2.7 left it.

they were corrections at all. Half of the Stage 4 corrections drew independent
criticism from the first outside reader they met.

## 3. Unresolved replication differences

Two, and both are recorded as unresolved rather than adjudicated:

1. **Whether A10's trail-first procedure is satisfiable from artifacts alone**
   (§2.7). Both readings are faithful to the text; the difference changes an
   element answer and an instrument's completeness. The method must say which.
   Neither author is treated here as correct.
2. **Whether "identified" satisfies A18's element 4 where the identified
   boundaries are unresolved as to authority** (§2.3). Development's assessment
   favours the second author's reading and does not impose it — A18's wording
   permits both, which is itself the problem.

## 5. Access-boundary effects, isolated

- **Authority findings** (§2.4) — the dominant effect, caused by the first
  author's package design (§1).
- **The parent-side charter's delegation and reserved-authority lists** were
  available to one author and not the other, which also explains part of the
  reserved-question difference (§2.13).
- **Everything else was equal:** both had the full venture repository at the
  same commit, and both had "none" for the database, the deployment,
  author-confidential material and every person.

## 6. What this comparison says about the first author's Stage 5 work

Recorded because the comparison would be worthless if it only examined the
second author:

1. **Four classes of named-but-unheld source were not inventoried** (§2.1).
2. **A stated-versus-observed contradiction was missed** with the data in hand
   (§2.10).
3. **Element 4 was answered more confidently than A9 supports** (§2.3).
4. **A finding was stated without carrying its evidence class** (§2.4).
5. **A17 rule 7's custody agreement was not obtained and not recorded as a

   author and adopted here.
6. **The replication boundary was set unequal to Stage 5's, and the trade-off
   was not recorded at the time** (§1).

None of these is corrected by editing Stage 5. Under `fd-0024` §2.4's rule
against rewriting prior reports, they are recorded here and the Stage 5 report
stands as returned.
