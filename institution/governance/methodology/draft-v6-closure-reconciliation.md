## 3. Determination: A17 rule 7 is DISCHARGED for this application

**Four grounds, each checkable.**

**(a) A disposition now exists, made by the competent authority.** A17 rule 7
requires the disposition *"agreed with the organization."* The organization's
accountable authority has determined it. The rule requires agreement, not a
particular form of agreement, and Draft v7's A17 rule 7 records what this case
demonstrated — that a **standing arrangement** can satisfy it.

**(b) The disposition reaches the material this application's inventory
names.** Completion report §44 recorded the residual material in two parts. The
**institutionally significant part** — the application record, the participant
questions, the responses, the reconciliation — has been **admitted to the
organization's own governed corpus** through the existing admission mechanism,
where the organization owns it, controls it and can retrieve it independently.
The **transient part** — agent session transcripts and working context held by
a model provider, the engagement scratchpad, and a contributor memory index
outside the repository — is determined **not authoritative and not the
institutional record**.

**(c) The rule's stated purpose is satisfied in fact, not merely in form.** A17
rule 7 exists so that *"Significant organizational evidence does not become
dependent on the team."* It does not. Every institutionally significant output
of this application is in the governed corpus, counted by the corpus validators,
and retrievable by the organization without reference to the diagnostic team.

**(d) Nothing was inferred to reach this.** The determination is quoted, not
construed. Where it is silent, §4 says so rather than filling the silence.

## 4. What the determination does **not** decide — stated so the discharge is not read as wider than it is

**No retention period, deletion requirement, or expiry is set for the residual
transient copies** held outside the organization. RQ-G determines their
**status** — not authoritative, not the institutional record — and determines
where the record **does** live. It does not direct that they be returned or
destroyed, and this record does not imply that it did.

**That is not a gap in the discharge.** A17 rule 7 asks for a disposition among
*returned, retained under agreement, destroyed*; the organization has made one
that retains the significant material under its own standing arrangement and
determines the rest to be non-record. **But if the Founder Office intends a
retention or deletion rule for the transient copies, that is a further decision
and this record does not make it.**

## 5. Closure posture after the discharge — and what it does *not* change

**The unresolved-closure statement no longer applies to this application.** The
application may now be represented without it.

**And the diagnosis is still not complete.** A18's four states are independent,
and discharging one moves only that one:

| State | Before | After |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Application or stage work completed | **yes** | **yes** |
| **Subject diagnosis complete** | **no** | **no — unchanged** |
| **Advancement permitted** | **no** | **no — unchanged** |
| Closure condition unresolved | **yes** | **DISCHARGED** |

**Advancement is still refused, for an entirely separate reason.** The one
failing A18 minimum-evidence element is *critical workflows and dependencies
sufficiently understood* — the walkthrough's step sequence was never obtained
and the transmission step has no trail evidence of any kind. **That has nothing
to do with custody**, and RQ-G does not touch it. The advancement state remains
***request additional evidence***.

**The language rule still binds in part.** "Closed" and "complete" may not be
used unqualifiedly of the **subject diagnosis**, because it is not complete —
not because a closure condition is outstanding, which it no longer is.
