**Rule observed** (`fd-0021` §2.8): "Do not create corrections merely to

not sanitize the evidence." No proposal below removes a contradiction from the
record, softens a finding, or makes the method look better on the evidence it

and asymmetries could not be represented cleanly, not to make them go away.

Each proposal records the seven fields the instrument requires: identifier;
validation evidence; method or instrument affected; problem exposed; proposed

## PT-01 — A classification for a subject only one half addresses

- **Validation evidence:** C-45 (records show one open matter kept in ≥4
  places; the account names neither a list nor a multiplicity), C-55 (records
  hold five recorded execution stops; the account names three resource
  constraints and no stall), C-59 (records hold realized losses; the account's
  limb unanswered), C-63 (records hold five systems the account does not
  name), C-64 (records hold four consequential events; the account says "I
  don't know"). Sixteen of seventy-two comparisons landed on *Unresolved*, and
  at least five of them are not genuinely unresolved — they are **one-sided**.
- **Affected:** the comparison procedure (`stakeholder-evidence-procedure.md`
  §7.3) and the seven-classification set at `fd-0020` §2.12.
- **Problem exposed:** *Unresolved — further evidence required* means the
  evidence is insufficient to determine the **relationship** between the two
  halves. It is being made to carry a different condition: the relationship is
  determinate and the halves are simply about different subjects. Collapsing
  the two loses the most actionable distinction in the comparison — "we do not
  know" versus "they did not say, and we do."
- **Proposed correction:** add two sub-classifications under Unresolved (not
  an eighth top-level class): **not addressed by the account** and **not
  addressed by the records**, each requiring the evidence the addressing half
  holds and an explicit statement of whether the subject was asked.
- **Necessary before external validation?** **Yes.** In an external engagement
  the great majority of subjects will be one-sided, and a diagnosis that
  reports them all as "unresolved" will misstate its own evidence position to
  a client.
- **Remaining uncertainty:** whether these are best sub-classes of Unresolved
  or a field on every comparison. One internal application is thin evidence for
  the shape.

## PT-02 — A representation for contradictions inside a single account

- **Validation evidence:** five conflicts inside the account (evidence set §6
  pairs A–E): Q24 against Q5 and Q14; Q19 against Q18; Q28 against Q17; Q27
  against Q5, Q14 and Q18; Q15 against Q12. C-60, C-48, C-68 and C-67 each had
  to record a within-account contradiction inside a block whose classification
  vocabulary is defined for account-versus-records.
- **Affected:** capture rule 5 (which keeps both and cross-references, and
  stops there); the comparison procedure §7.3–§7.4; A17 rule 3.
- **Problem exposed:** the method can *hold* two conflicting stakeholder
  statements but has nowhere to **classify** the conflict, no requirement to
  surface it as a finding, and no rule about what it does to the weight of
  either statement. Here the conflicts were among the most diagnostically
  significant material in the session.
- **Proposed correction:** make within-account contradiction an explicit
  comparison outcome with its own row, requiring: both statements verbatim;
  whether they can be read as being about different objects; whether the
  diagnostic is doing that reading (and if so, that it is *Inferred*); and
  what evidence would settle it. Neither statement's evidence class changes.
- **Necessary before external validation?** **Yes.** With multiple
  stakeholders, contradiction *between* accounts and *within* one account are
  both routine, and the method presently represents neither.
- **Remaining uncertainty:** whether within-account and between-account
  contradiction want the same treatment. This session tested only the first.

## PT-03 — A disclosure for a stakeholder who authored the records

- **Validation evidence:** C-13 (the purpose statement the account gives is
  the Vision Foundation's own sentence, approved by the same authority);
  C-03, C-49, C-51, C-57 similarly [same-source]. Six of twenty-three
  corroborations are one source agreeing with itself. Predicted as an
  unexercised assumption at Stage 3 report §33.3; now realized.
- **Affected:** A18 ("[w]here the diagnostic team authored records it is
  judging, the outcome states 'author-of-records'"); Instrument 1's evidence
  classes; the comparison procedure.
- **Problem exposed:** Draft v2 discloses when the **team** authored the
  records it judges. It has no equivalent for when the **stakeholder** did.
  Corroboration between an account and a record with the same author is
  consistency evidence, not independent evidence, and nothing in the frozen
  method makes a reader see that.
- **Proposed correction:** a per-comparison marker — *account and record share
  a source* — plus an A18 outcome statement of how many corroborations carry
  it. Corroboration is not downgraded automatically; it is labelled.
- **Necessary before external validation?** **Recommended, not blocking.** It
  bites hardest where a client supplies its own governance corpus authored by
  the stakeholder being interviewed, which is common in owner-led
  organizations — the very segment the Vision Foundation describes.
- **Remaining uncertainty:** how far "same source" reaches — the same person,
  the same office, the same approval chain.

## PT-04 — Limb-level capture, and a "did not address the question" row

- **Validation evidence:** nineteen unanswered limbs (`S-N01`…`S-N19`) across
  fourteen of thirty questions. Three answers addressed an adjacent subject
  rather than the one asked: Q14 (asked what functions do; answered where they
  came from), Q16 (asked which decisions recur; answered what triggers ideas),
  Q21 (asked where work stalls; answered which resources are scarce).
- **Affected:** Instrument 1's recording rules; the stakeholder procedure §5.
- **Problem exposed:** the capture rules treat a question as a unit. A
  multi-limb question answered in part is recorded as answered, and an answer
  that lands beside the question is recorded as evidence with no note that it
  did not respond. Both distort the evidence position — always in the
  direction of looking better informed than the diagnosis is.
- **Proposed correction:** record limbs, not questions: each question's limbs
  are enumerated when it is written, and each is *answered* or *not supplied*.
  Add a row type **answer does not address the question asked**, recording
  what was asked, what was answered, and that no follow-up was taken.
- **Necessary before external validation?** **Yes.** Cheap, mechanical, and it
  changes what a diagnosis can honestly claim to have asked.
- **Remaining uncertainty:** whether limb enumeration makes questions feel
  like a questionnaire in delivery — which A19 and the global instrument rules
  forbid becoming.

## PT-05 — A follow-up trigger for under-determined terminology answers

- **Validation evidence:** C-07 ("Process." against eleven adopted terms for
  the same territory), C-02 (no names supplied for any internal part), C-70
  ("All of them," unenumerable), C-58 and C-65 (deictic referents — "those
  things," "these things" — never resolved).
- **Affected:** A1 (terminology discovery); the procedure §4 (follow-ups
  permitted only to clarify the stakeholder's own words, and recorded as
  follow-ups).
- **Problem exposed:** A1 assumes an organization will supply terms for the
  concepts it has. When the answer is one word, or a totality, or a pronoun,
  the method records it faithfully and has **no rule about what happens next**.
  In this session three of the most diagnostically loaded answers stayed
  ambiguous for want of a single clarifying question — and one of them (C-46)
  was enough on its own to refuse advancement.
- **Proposed correction:** a stated trigger — where an answer supplies fewer
  terms than the concept set it was asked about, or resolves to a totality or
  an unresolved deictic, a **clarification-only** follow-up is required before
  the answer is used in a finding, and is recorded as a follow-up. If no
  follow-up is possible, the finding carries "under-determined answer, no
  follow-up available."
- **Necessary before external validation?** **Recommended.** Internally the
  cost was three ambiguous answers; with a client, an ambiguous answer used
  confidently is a worse failure than a gap.
- **Remaining uncertainty:** the anti-leading boundary. A clarification that
  offers candidate words is leading; one that asks the stakeholder to say more
  may not clarify. The procedure's own anti-leading check had to rewrite four
  of thirty questions, so this is a live risk, not a theoretical one.

## PT-06 — Establish the organization's boundary before asking what is outside it

- **Validation evidence:** C-60 (Q24 "None," against five recorded forms of
  externally-held knowledge and the account's own Q5 and Q14); C-62 (three
  named tools absent from the systems record); C-11 (a named holding the
  corpus does not know exists).
- **Affected:** A12 ("[w]hat knowledge about the organization exists only with
  its implementers, advisors, or vendors"); A13; Instrument 6's custody rows.
- **Problem exposed:** the method asks what lies *outside* the organization
  without ever establishing where the organization ends. The stakeholder and
  the diagnostic team may be drawing that line in different places — and here,
  demonstrably, the answer to Q24 turns entirely on it. A contradiction was
  recorded that may be a boundary disagreement rather than a factual one, and
  the method gave no way to tell.
- **Proposed correction:** in A4, add a boundary step — the organization's own
  statement of what it counts as itself: which people, tools, environments,
  vendors and units are inside. Carry that boundary into A12, A13 and
  Instrument 6, and record where the diagnostic team's boundary differs.
- **Necessary before external validation?** **Recommended, and rising to
  necessary** wherever contractors, agencies, platforms or AI tools do
  material work — which the Vision Foundation's own target segment describes.
- **Remaining uncertainty:** whether the boundary belongs in A4 (identity) or
  A3 (baseline). It was needed in this session before Q24 was asked, which
  argues for A3.

## PT-07 — Habit-held responsibility cannot be obtained from a single-actor account

- **Validation evidence:** C-38 — the one capability `fd-0020` §2.11 required
  to be tested that produced **no finding**. Q15's habit limb was asked and
  the answer generalized ("all of it goes through me"); records cannot show
  habit; three matters proved unowned rather than habit-held.
- **Affected:** A7; Instrument 3 ("'observed responsibilities' that no formal
  assignment covers are flagged as habit- or individual-held"); A20.
- **Problem exposed:** Instrument 3 detects habit by comparing observed
  responsibility against formal assignment. Where one actor holds every formal
  assignment, the comparison yields nothing, and the account cannot supply the
  distinction either, because from inside it there is none.
- **Proposed correction:** two parts. (a) State in A7 that habit-held
  responsibility is obtained by **observing work as performed**, not by asking,
  and that an account-only application records the capability as unexercised.
  (b) Add to A20 that in single-actor organizations the habit/role distinction
  may be unobtainable in principle, and that the diagnosis says so rather than
  reporting no habit-held work.
- **Necessary before external validation?** **Recommended.** External
  organizations are more likely to have several actors, where the instrument
  works; but a single-actor client is exactly the Vision Foundation's stated
  segment.
- **Remaining uncertainty:** whether observation is available in a real
  engagement at all. A10's "observe as performed" has never been exercised in
  this program.

## PT-08 — Derive the access statement from the account, not only from the team's inventory

- **Validation evidence:** the access statement gained **three** rows at
  access = **none** solely because the stakeholder named holdings the team did
  not know to look for: ChatGPT rooms and projects (C-11), the "Founder Office
  conversation" (C-46), Curbside Spa's holdings (C-08). Two of the three carry
  material findings, and together they are a principal ground of the refusal
  to advance.
- **Affected:** A3 step 10 (access statement); A18's access constraint.
- **Problem exposed:** A3 step 10 records access for "each corpus, system, and
  person **in the plan**" — the plan being the team's. Holdings the team never
  knew existed cannot enter it. In this session the account changed the access
  picture materially and for the worse, and only an explicit re-derivation
  caught it.
- **Proposed correction:** make the access statement a **two-pass** artifact:
  built from the team's inventory at A3, then **re-derived after each
  stakeholder session** from holdings the account names, with new rows carrying
  the question they came from. A18 evaluates the revised statement.
- **Necessary before external validation?** **Yes.** This is the proposal that
  most changes what a diagnosis concludes: without it, a diagnosis can pass
  A18 on an access statement the stakeholder has already falsified.
- **Remaining uncertainty:** none about the need; the mechanism is simple. What
  is untested is how a client reacts to being told access is worse after
  talking than before.

## PT-09 — Distinguish an asserted condition from a stakeholder judgement

- **Validation evidence:** C-56 ("I feel like everything is pretty governed" —
  a perception that had to be compared against ten recorded governance gaps);
  C-69 ("I'm sure I will find one of these problems at some point" — an
  expectation); C-25 ("[t]here's no immediate value"); C-01 ("I'd say…").
- **Affected:** A2's three evidence classes; Instrument 1.
- **Problem exposed:** *Client-Stated* covers both "this is how the
  organization works" and "this is how I feel about how it works." The
  comparison then has to do work the capture should have marked, and there is a
  standing temptation to treat a perception as a weak assertion — which
  `fd-0021` §2.7 validation 11 expressly forbids, and which the method's own
  vocabulary does nothing to prevent.
- **Proposed correction:** a capture attribute on Client-Stated rows —
  *asserted condition* / *judgement or perception* / *expectation* — taken from
  the stakeholder's own framing, never assigned by the team, and never used to
  discount the row.
- **Necessary before external validation?** **Recommended.** Not blocking, but
  the cost of getting it wrong with a client — reporting a leader's feeling as
  a finding about their organization — is high.
- **Remaining uncertainty:** the risk that marking a statement a "perception"
  reads as dismissal. The framing must come from the speaker's words.

## PT-10 — Instrument 4 should record where the stakeholder would actually go

- **Validation evidence:** C-46 and §7 of the comparison — the first time in
  this program that "a source exists," "it is trusted," and "it is treated as
  authoritative" pointed at different places. In the dry run they collapsed
  because the team held all three.
- **Affected:** Instrument 4 (Source-of-Truth Inventory); A9.
- **Problem exposed:** Instrument 4 records the authoritative source, its
  owner, retrievability, and representations. It has no field for **the place
  the person actually goes when two records disagree** — which is the question
  A9 asks and the single most revealing answer of this session.
- **Proposed correction:** add a field *source of resort (per stakeholder,
  Client-Stated)* alongside the authoritative source, with divergence between
  them recorded under "conflicts." The instrument still never appoints an
  authoritative source.
- **Necessary before external validation?** **Yes.** It is where informal
  authority becomes visible, and it is one field.
- **Remaining uncertainty:** with several stakeholders there will be several
  sources of resort; whether that is one row with N values or N rows is
  untested.

## What is deliberately not proposed

- **Nothing that would remove a contradiction from the record.** The nine
  contradictions at comparison §4 stand; no proposal reclassifies, softens, or
  reconciles any of them.
- **No change to the seven top-level classifications.** PT-01 proposes
  sub-classes precisely so that the instrument-fixed set at `fd-0020` §2.12
  is not amended by a Development proposal.
- **No relaxation of the refusal to advance.** Nothing here would have let this
  diagnosis proceed to design.
- **No new instrument, register, numbering system, or software.** Every
  proposal is a field, a rule, or a step inside instruments that already exist.
- **Nothing arising from the Huerta-specific findings.** The organization's
  own conditions surfaced by this session belong to Huerta Group, not to the
  method; routing them is Stage 2 §64 Q7, still open.
