## 10. Technical isolation limitations

**Isolation was partial. It was measured, not estimated.**

The second author's harness injected the user-level auto-memory index into its
context before any instruction could prevent it. Rather than guess at exposure,
it was required to quote the index verbatim before beginning, and did.

**What leaked:** subject product-status claims ("shipped+verified", "39/39",
"production @ `e28f12a`"), Stage 3-era program facts, two frozen draft digests,
inherited operating norms, and a corpus search technique.

**What did not leak, and this is the material point.** The index line it
received is a **stale snapshot** naming Stage 3. The line in force at the time
named Stage 5, the applied draft, **the refused advancement**, and D-1…D-8.
The second author therefore did not receive the existence of Stage 5, the
existence of Draft v3 or its application to this subject, **the advancement
outcome**, the deficiency count, or the fact that a replication was expected. It
confirms independently: no literal occurrence of "Stage 5", no named prior
author.

**Two further limitations the second author raised and the first had not:**

1. **Model correlation.** Both authors are the same model family. *"Correlated
   errors will look like reproducibility."* No internal arrangement removes it.
2. **The subject was pre-framed.** The harness placed it inside the subject
   repository. *"I did not select or discover the subject; I was placed in it."*

**Rule adopted from its own §7.2 and applied throughout the comparison:**
convergence on any identifier it already held is **excluded** from the
reproducibility evidence.

**Assessment, recorded before the replication ran:** partial, bounded, and
sufficient for a *qualified* test — weaker than the instrument hoped, stronger
than a sham. A cleaner test exists and was unavailable: a different model, no
memory injection, package only.
