## Part A — The Method

### A0. Purpose

To determine how Huerta Group can enter an organization it does not already
understand, establish an evidentiary baseline, diagnose its actual
organizational condition without prematurely prescribing a solution, determine
what meaning and authority must be established before design, and determine
whether any organizational, procedural, informational, managerial, or
technical intervention is warranted.

The diagnostic's product is **not a solution**. It is a confirmed,
evidence-labeled account of what the organization is, produces, decides, owns,
knows, and stalls on — with the questions the organization's own authority
must answer listed rather than answered, and with what the diagnostic team
could not see stated as plainly as what it could — sufficient to decide
*whether* to proceed to establishing meaning and authority, and *what* must be
governed first if design follows.

**The words this method uses of itself.** A1 requires the organization's
vocabulary to be recorded precisely; the method holds its own to the same
standard.

- **The organization** — the subject of the diagnosis. *The subject* is a
  synonym, used where the sentence is about the diagnosis rather than about the
  organization.
- **The organization's authority** — the person, office, or body inside the
  organization holding the right to decide a given matter. **"Client
  authority" is the same thing**, retained only inside the fixed names *Questions Reserved to Client Authority* (A16, Instrument 10) and in
the instrument field names of that lineage (*client authority required*;
*responsible client authority*), so that established names stay stable across
the corpus; **in prose the method says *the organization's authority***. Neither term ever means the diagnostic team.
- **The diagnostic team** — the actual institutional or individual actor
  materially performing the diagnostic. Both layers exist and A18 discloses
  both where they differ.
- **Huerta Group** — the institution that supplies the method. It is not
  automatically the diagnostic team, and never the organization.

### A1. Organization neutrality and terminology discovery

The diagnostic must not assume: a Founder Office; Huerta Group's constitutional
architecture; a Corporate Headquarters; a Corporate Command Center;
Operational Records; Founder Attention; Huerta Group's venture model;
repository governance; a particular technology stack; software as outcome; a
single apex individual; **a distributed authority structure**; **authority
concentrated in any one actor**; **any particular environment in which matters
are worked through**; **that the place a matter is worked through is the place
its truth is established**; or Huerta Group terminology.

**Terminology discovery precedes translation.** Before any neutral term below
is used for an organization, the organization's own words for the concept —
as spoken by its people and as written in its records — are recorded in
Instrument 1 (as *Observed* usage, with who used the word and where) and are
used in every instrument. The neutral terms exist so that the diagnostic
team can reason across organizations; they are never a vocabulary the
organization is asked to adopt, and each is **conditional**: it applies only
if the organization has the thing it names. "No equivalent found" is a
permitted and expected entry.

**An organization that governs its own vocabulary.** Where the organization
maintains an **authoritative vocabulary** — a ratified glossary, a terminology
canon, a controlled set of terms it enforces on itself — that vocabulary is
**adopted as the organization's terms**, and its governing status and the
mechanism by which it is enforced are recorded as evidence. Elicitation is used
only for concepts the vocabulary does not cover. Adoption is not endorsement:
A1's rule that a divergence between a stated term and observed usage is a
finding applies to a governed vocabulary exactly as to a spoken one.

**Where the organization's apparatus reaches evidence itself.** An
organization may govern not only what things are called but how *its own*
evidence is classified — an evidence taxonomy, a validation vocabulary, a
strength framework. Where it does, that scheme is recorded faithfully as the
organization's terminology and governance, and is used when describing how the
organization classifies its own evidence. **It does not replace, redefine, or
silently modify A2**, which governs the diagnostic's own evidence
classification across subjects. Where the organization's scheme appears
stronger, more precise, or more useful than A2, that observation is preserved
as evidence for later methodology evolution and is **not substituted during the
engagement**. A1 preserves the organization's language; A2 preserves the
diagnostic's evidence semantics; neither erases the other.

**Where a term's condition does not hold at all.** A conditional term may be
not merely absent but **malformed** for a given organization — a question that
presupposes a structure the organization does not have. That is recorded as
**"the question presupposes a structure this organization does not have"**,
which is a different entry from *no equivalent found*: the first says the
question does not apply, the second says the thing has another name or none.

**Generality is recorded as generality.** An organization's word may be
*broader* than the concept it is offered for — one everyday term covering much
of what the method distinguishes. Where it is, the word is recorded at the
breadth the organization gives it and is **never narrowed into a specialized
sense the organization did not give it**. A general term is evidence about the
organization's vocabulary; it is not an answer to the concept set it was asked
about, and the concepts it leaves unnamed are recorded as unnamed — a finding
about the state of the evidence, not about the organization. The same word may
also carry two different meanings within one organization; where it does, both
are recorded against the questions they arose in, and neither is corrected.

| Analytical concept (Huerta Group's own term, not to be used with the organization) | Conditional neutral term — used only if the condition holds |
| --- | --- |
| Founder Office / the Founder | **the accountable authority** for a given decision right — *if* anyone holds it: a person, board, committee, or office; possibly several; possibly unresolved. Never presumed to be one person |
| Founder Attention | **the attention layer** — *if* some mechanism brings exceptions to an accountable authority; otherwise "no attention mechanism evidenced" |
| Corporate Headquarters | **the coordinating level** — *if* the organization assembles cross-unit state anywhere; otherwise "no coordinating level evidenced" |
| Headquarters representation / Operational Record | **a representation** — *if* some summary, register, dashboard, list, or report stands for facts owned elsewhere |
| Operational Instance | **the matter** — the real-world thing a representation stands for |
| Corporate Operations & Command Management | **the representation owner** — *if* anyone is responsible for keeping a representation current; otherwise "unowned" |
| Source function | **the source owner** — *if* anyone holds authoritative truth for a fact; otherwise "owner unresolved" |
| Venture | **a subordinate unit** — *if* any unit holds delegated authority whose detail is not reproduced at a coordinating level |
| Repository / determinations register / evolution log | **durable, organization-controlled, independently retrievable institutional memory** — by whatever mechanism, *if* any |
| Dependency / Blocker / Handoff | analytical distinctions between *relies on*, *cannot presently proceed because of*, and *expected next action transferred to* — aids, never imposed vocabulary |
| Material Change | **a consequential change** — one that alters what should be decided, done, or expected |
| Since Last Review | **what changed since an accountable authority last looked** — *if* any authority reviews at intervals |

Where the organization has different valid semantics for any of these, the
organization's terms are recorded and used, and the analytical distinction is
applied beneath them. Where the organization's term and the neutral term
diverge in meaning, the divergence is a finding, recorded in Instrument 1.

### A2. Evidence classification

Every material diagnostic claim carries exactly one of three classes:

- **Observed** — directly witnessed, documented, measured, demonstrated, or
  otherwise evidenced organizational condition.
- **Client-Stated** — condition asserted by an organizational participant or
  supplied institutional source but not yet independently corroborated.
- **Inferred** — analytical interpretation drawn from available evidence and
  explicitly distinguished from direct fact.

Rules:

1. Do not silently convert client-stated information into observed fact.
2. Do not silently convert inference into fact.
3. Preserve source and provenance for material diagnostic claims; record
   conflicting evidence rather than normalizing it away; record evidence
   limitations explicitly.
4. **A document proves that the document exists and contains an assertion.**
   A document supplied by the organization is *Observed* as to its existence
   and content, and *Client-Stated* as to the organizational condition it
   asserts, until the condition is corroborated by observation of operation.
   Where both facts matter, the register carries **two rows** (Instrument 1).
5. **Source kind is not evidence quality.** Every Client-Stated row records
   **what kind of thing supplied the claim** — at minimum: a *person or
   stakeholder account*; an *organizational or institutional record*; a
   *supplied institutional source*; a *systematic verification or validation
   record*; or another kind the evidence genuinely requires. The attribute
   answers only *what kind of thing supplied this claim*, never *how good,
   reliable, strong, credible, or persuasive it is*. **The kinds are not
   ordered, ranked, weighted, or scored, and carry no implied hierarchy**: a
   systematic verification record is distinguished from a narrative record
   because they are different kinds of artifact, not because one outranks the
   other. Evidence quality is expressed separately and only through
   limitations, contradictions, provenance, freshness, coverage, and
   contextual explanation. **A2 acquires no ordering from this attribute.**
6. **Unopposed is not corroborated.** Evidence available from only one
   participant, source, or side does not become corroborated because no
   contrary account exists or could be obtained. It keeps its class, and the
   register records whether a second account was **sought**, **not sought**, or
   **sought and unavailable**, with the reason. Absence of disagreement is
   recorded as the absence of a second source, never as agreement. The same
   holds where an account agrees with a record the same party authored or
   approved: that is consistency, marked **"account and record share a
   source,"** and it is not independent corroboration (A18).

### A3. Evidentiary baseline (Stage 0)

**Purpose.** Establish what evidence exists, what does not, whose claims are
represented, what access the team actually has, and what limitations
constrain the diagnosis — before any finding is drawn.

**Required questions.**

1. What authoritative, operational, historical, and stakeholder evidence is
   available?
2. What evidence is unavailable?
3. Who supplied each material claim?
4. What can be independently verified?
5. What contradictions exist?
6. What access limitations materially affect confidence?

**Evidence requested at the outset** (recorded as requested, supplied, or not
supplied): the organization's governing and operating records as it names
them; **an inventory of the organization's people and roles, as the
organization describes them**; the systems and record sets in use; the
organization's own vocabulary for its work (A1); **the organization's own
statement of its boundary** — which people, units, tools, environments,
vendors, and services it counts as part of itself (step 11).

**Procedure.**

1. Record the point in time and the state of every corpus, system, or record
   set inspected (identifier, version or date, access mode) — **and, where the
   engagement specifies a state to be examined, verify that the state
   inspected is that state**, at the opening of the work and again at its
   close. A diagnosis of a different state than the one it was asked about is
   not a partial answer; it is an answer to another question.
2. Inventory evidence by kind — authoritative (charters, policies,
   constitutions, approvals), operational (records, systems, logs), historical
   (prior audits, decisions, correspondence), stakeholder (accounts of the
   people who do the work).
3. For each material claim, record its source, its class (A2), and **how the
   team came to hold it** (A17 rule 6).
4. Record what was requested and not supplied, what exists but cannot be
   accessed, and what the organization says exists but cannot produce — each
   as a register row, never as an inference.
5. Record contradictions as contradictions; do not choose between them at this
   stage.
6. Record the access limitations and their effect on confidence.
7. Never fill a gap by inference. Unavailable evidence is recorded as
   unavailable; a missing record is not proof that the thing it would record
   does not exist.
8. **Stakeholder-access plan, and how answers are taken.** Record who will be
   asked, in what order, on what subjects, and what is done if access is
   refused, delayed, or unavailable. A diagnosis in which no stakeholder
   account is obtained is labelled **"records-only"** on every output it
   produces (A18). **Each question is decomposed into its limbs before it is
   put**, and every limb is recorded *answered* or *not supplied*; a response
   that addresses a different question than the one asked is recorded as such,
   not as an answer to the question asked (Instrument 1).
   **Clarification-only follow-up.** Where an answer supplies fewer terms than
   the concept set it was asked about, resolves to a totality that can be
   neither verified nor falsified, or turns on a reference the team would have
   to guess at, a clarification-only follow-up is required before that answer
   supports a finding. It asks the participant to say more about their own
   words; it never offers candidate words, structures, or answers; it is
   recorded as a follow-up with its own date; and the original answer, together
   with the fact that it was under-determined, is preserved beside it
   unchanged. Where no follow-up is available, every finding drawing on the
   answer carries **"under-determined answer; no follow-up available."**
9. **Citation check.** Every authority cited by a record that a material
   finding relies upon is checked for being **held in the corpus the diagnostic
   is authorized to read**; for the wider corpus a sample is checked and the
   sampling basis is recorded. **"Cited, not held in the authorized corpus"**
   is a standard register row — and it is the honest form, because a diagnostic
   can establish what its own access reaches and cannot establish what the
   organization holds elsewhere (A17 rule 4).
10. **Access statement, in two passes.** Record, for each corpus, system,
    environment, and person, two facts separately and never merged:
    (a) **stated to exist or identified** — by whom, when, and on what basis;
    and (b) **access actually obtained** — **full, partial, or none**, how it
    was verified, and what "partial" excluded. What an organization says it
    has and what the diagnostic actually reached are different facts, and a
    diagnosis recording only the first overstates its own evidence position.
    The statement is built first from the team's own inventory and is
    **re-derived after every stakeholder session**, adding any corpus, system,
    environment, or person the account names, with the question it came from.
    The statement in force is the re-derived one; it is carried into the header
    of every instrument and into the A18 outcome. **Where no stakeholder
    session occurs, the statement is re-derived zero times and the team's own
    inventory stands as the statement in force**, recorded as such. The second
    pass distinguishes four conditions and never flattens them: evidence that
    **does not exist**; evidence that **exists and was refused**; evidence that
    **exists and could not be reached**; and evidence that **exists, is
    reachable, and the diagnostic was instructed not to obtain** — an imposed
    scope boundary, which says something entirely different about a diagnosis
    than an access failure.
11. **Organizational boundary.** Record the organization's own statement of
    what it counts as itself — which people, units, tools, environments,
    vendors, and services are inside it — as **Client-Stated**, and record
    where the diagnostic team's working boundary differs. Where the boundary is
    unresolved, that is recorded, and no question depending on it — A12's
    "outside the organization," A13's systems inventory — is answered as though
    it were settled.

**Outputs.** Evidence & Limitations Register (Instrument 1); initial
provenance map; known evidence gaps; stakeholder-access plan; **two-pass
access statement**; **boundary statement**.

### A4. Purpose and identity discovery

**Questions.** What does the organization say it exists to do? What does its
actual operation suggest it exists to do? What outputs, outcomes, or
obligations define its existence? Where do stated identity and observed
operation diverge?

**Evidence to request or observe.** Founding, governing, or strategic records
if any; public self-description; what the organization measures and rewards;
what its people say it is for, in their words; what it actually spends its
time on.

**Evidence-derived supplementary questions** (from the Discovery Report; not
part of the instrument's required set): Which of the organization's stated
capabilities are current, planned, or aspirational? Does the organization
distinguish these on its own surfaces?

**Output — the identity-of-record statement.** A4 produces it. It is a
**sourced** statement of what the organization's **own authoritative or
governing evidence** establishes about what the organization is, what it exists
to do, and the obligations or purposes that define it — with explicit notation
where **no authoritative identity record exists**, where authoritative sources
**conflict**, or where **identity authority is unresolved**. A3 may supply the
evidence through the evidentiary baseline and source inventory; A4 owns the
output.

It is **not**: a mission statement written by the diagnostic team; public copy
treated as authoritative by default; a summary inferred from observed activity;
or a judgement about what the organization ought to become. Where authoritative
identity evidence is absent or conflicted, **the output is still required and
states that truthfully** — it does not disappear because the organization has
no settled answer.

**Non-prescription.** Do not rewrite mission, identity, strategy, or values
during diagnosis. Do not infer identity from public copy alone.

### A5. Outputs and value-creation discovery

**Questions.** What does the organization actually produce, enable, protect,
deliver, or sustain? Who depends upon those outputs? Which recurring
activities materially create value? Which activities exist mainly because of
legacy process or tooling?

**Evidence to request or observe.** The outputs themselves; who receives
them; what happens when they are late or wrong; the activities that consume
the most effort and their traceable connection to an output.

**Consumption classification.** Each output is recorded as **externally
consumed** (received by parties outside the organization) or **internally
consumed** (received by the organization itself — its own structure,
governance, systems, or capability), and the proportion of observed effort on
each is recorded — counted where records permit, characterized otherwise,
with the difference stated. Neither class is a judgement; the proportion is a
finding.

**Relevance test** (evidence-derived): for any activity, record, or
information the organization maintains — *what decision, coordination need,
risk, obligation, or action does it support?* Where there is no credible
answer, record that.

### A6. Authority and decision-rights discovery

**Questions.** Who formally holds authority? Who actually exercises authority?
Which decisions recur? Who can approve, reject, escalate, or redirect them?
Where are formal and informal decision rights misaligned? Where does ambiguity
cause delay or conflict?

**Evidence to request or observe.** Organization charts and delegations (as
Client-Stated); the record of actual decisions and who made them (Observed);
accounts from the people who wait for decisions; the escalation path as
practiced.

**Fields to establish, per decision class.** Reserved to whom; **delegated** to
whom and by what instrument; observed to be exercised by whom; **contested** —
where two or more parties claim or dispute it, each claim and its basis
recorded, none preferred; **held by the same actor as** which other decision
classes; **unresolved** where no one can say; **anticipated but not
established** — a delegation or distribution the organization intends but has
not made, recorded as Client-Stated intent with the trigger the organization
names, never as a plan of record; configurable pending what; the statement that
access, custody, participation, naming, sensemaking, clarification, and routing
confer nothing.

**Role compression.** Consultation and escalation are recorded as **"none
evidenced (basis: …)"** or **"none formally required (instrument: …)"** —
never as "not applicable." Where the evidence resolves every decision class
to one actor, **role compression** is recorded as a finding in Instrument 2
with its continuity consequence and with the evidence that established it;
it is never assumed from the organization's size, founding, or the number of
names encountered.

**Role compression depends on observation.** Establishing that every decision
class resolves to one actor requires **observed** authority for every class,
which requires the same observation of work and the same accounts that A7's
habit test requires. Where those are unavailable, role compression is recorded
as **not establishable on available evidence** — never as absent, and never
inferred from the number of names encountered (A20).

**Concentration is a condition, not a model.** Where authority is found
concentrated — in one person, one office, or one body — that is recorded as an
**observed condition of this organization at this time**, with its evidence and
its date, and where the organization says so, with the reason it gives. It is
never treated as the organization's permanent structure, as a property implied
by its size or founding, or as the diagnostic's default. Concentration,
distribution, delegation, contest, and unresolved authority are recorded with
the same fields and the same weight; none of them is the baseline against which
the others are exceptions. Where the organization states an intention to
distribute or delegate authority later, that is recorded as **anticipated but
not established**, with its stated trigger, and it changes nothing about the
present condition.

**Distinction maintained throughout.** Authority (the right to decide) ≠
responsibility (the duty to see something done) ≠ representation (keeping a
summary current) ≠ execution (doing the work) ≠ custody (holding the systems
or records) ≠ **sensemaking** (where a matter is worked through) ≠
**clarification** (where it is made definite enough to act on) ≠ **routing**
(where it is directed onward from). Each is recorded separately, even where one
person, one office, or one environment holds all eight — and the last three
confer no authority whatever, however consistently or exclusively they are
used (A9).

**Output.** Authority / Decision-Rights Map (Instrument 2).

**Non-prescription.** Do not create authority for the organization. Do not
treat formal organization charts as proof of actual authority. Do not treat
one person's presence in every record as proof that authority is theirs.

### A7. Functions and responsibilities

**Questions.** What organizational functions actually exist? What
responsibilities are formally assigned? What work exists only through habit or
individual ownership? Where are responsibilities duplicated? Where are
responsibilities unowned? Where is responsibility confused with authority?

**Evidence to request or observe.** Formal assignments (Client-Stated); what
each function is observed to produce; work that stops when a particular
person is absent; work two functions both believe they own; work no one will
claim.

**Derivation rule** (evidence-derived). Functions are recorded from what the
evidence shows exists and does work — never enumerated from an org chart
alone. Each function records **what it is evidenced by**: work · document
only · name only · **named by an account only** · not evidenced. A function
that appears in no evidence is recorded as "not evidenced," not as absent. An
**inventory of functions supplied by a participant** is recorded as
*Client-Stated*, against the question it answered, and compared with the
evidenced set; neither replaces the other, and a participant's list no more
establishes the organization's formal roster than a chart does.

**Three kinds of quiet** (evidence-derived). Distinguish: a function with no
evidence at all; a function with evidence but nothing consequential now; and a
function whose activity is consequential but invisible at any coordinating
level.

**Habit is established by observation.** Work held only by habit is identified
by observing work as performed (A10) and comparing it with what any assignment
covers — not by asking whether it exists. Where only accounts are available,
the finding is recorded as **"habit-held responsibility: capability not
exercised — observation unavailable,"** never as "no habit-held work found."
Where the evidence resolves every assignment to a single actor, the distinction
between habit and role may be unobtainable in principle; that is recorded as
the reason, and the gap is not closed by inference (A20).

**Output.** Function / Responsibility Map (Instrument 3).

### A8. Recurring decisions

**Questions.** What decisions recur? What triggers them? What evidence do
they require? Who makes them? What dependencies affect them? Where does
decision latency arise?

**Evidence to request or observe.** A sample of recent decisions of each
recurring kind, from trigger to disposition; the queue of decisions awaiting
someone; **the cadence of decision-making** (when, how often, and on what
occasion decisions are actually taken); how long each waited and why, **where
records or accounts permit**.

**Measurement guidance** (evidence-derived). Count rather than characterize
where the evidence allows: how many decisions awaited an accountable
authority at a point in time; **across how many places they were recorded**
(Instrument 7); how many were rediscovered rather than tracked. Where latency
cannot be established, record "latency not establishable from available
evidence" and record the distribution instead.

### A9. Information and source-of-truth discovery

**Questions.** What information materially supports operation and
decision-making? Where does authoritative truth reside? Which systems are
representations rather than authorities? Where are duplicate or contradictory
records? What critical information exists only in conversation or individual
memory?

**Procedure.** For each information domain that supports a recurring decision
or workflow, record: the authoritative source (the one place a fact is
established); its owner; **whether the organization can itself retrieve it**;
every representational system that also shows the fact; duplicate or
conflicting sources; how current each representation is and who keeps it so;
access limitations; and the treatment any new layer would need to apply —
*hold directly* (only what the new layer would legitimately own),
*reference*, *summarize*, *do nothing*, or *never authoritative by existence*
(conversation, working notes, speculation).

**Rules** (evidence-derived). A representation is not an authority because it
is convenient, current, or widely used. Conversation and working context are
not authoritative merely because they exist. A summarized fact must remain
distinguishable from its authoritative record. Where the owner of a fact
cannot be identified, record "owner unresolved" — do not appoint one.

**Three facts never collapsed.** For every candidate authoritative source,
record separately, each with its own evidence class: (a) **the source
exists** and what it contains; (b) **a stakeholder is confident in it** — who,
and how confident; (c) **the organization treats it as authoritative** — by
what act, instrument, or practice. Confidence is not authority; existence is
neither.

**Source roles, recorded separately.** For each information domain the
following may be one place or several different places, and each is recorded
with its own evidence class, with **"none evidenced"** permitted for any of
them:

- **where a matter is first worked through** — the environment in which it
  begins and is thought about;
- **where it is clarified** — where it is made definite enough to act on;
- **where it is routed from** — where it is directed to whoever or whatever
  takes it next;
- **who decides it** — the authority (A6);
- **where the fact is established** — the authoritative source;
- **where the durable record of the decision resides** — what the organization
  can retrieve afterwards, and by what means.

**Rule.** None of the first three roles makes a source authoritative. An
environment used consistently — even exclusively — for sensemaking,
clarification, or routing is recorded in those roles and is **not** an
authoritative source unless the organization has established it as one by act,
instrument, or practice (fact (c) above). Where the roles are held by different
places, that is a finding about how the organization works and not a defect;
where one place holds them all, that is recorded too, with what depends on it.
The roles are recorded as the organization describes them; the method supplies
no mechanism for any of them. **The first three roles describe where people
work, and are ordinarily obtainable only from participants.** Where no account
is available they are recorded **"not obtainable from records"** — which is not
the same entry as *none evidenced*: the first says the method could not reach
the answer, the second says the organization has no such place. A records-only
diagnosis will find this column largely empty, and that emptiness is a fact
about the diagnosis rather than about the organization.

**Source of resort.** For each domain, record **where a participant actually
goes when they need to resolve uncertainty or obtain a working answer** — per
participant, in their own words, **Client-Stated**. It is recorded beside the
authoritative source and never as it. Where the two differ, the divergence is
recorded under conflicts and is not corrected by the diagnostic; the difference
between them is frequently where informal authority becomes visible.

**One matter, many representations.** Where one organizational matter appears
in several systems, the matter's identity is stated first and its
representations are then listed against it. It is recorded as **one matter
with N representations**, never as N matters. Where the identity of a matter
is itself disputed, the dispute is recorded as a contradiction.

**Output.** Source-of-Truth Inventory (Instrument 4).

### A10. Workflow, handoff, dependency and obligation discovery

**Questions.** What work recurs? Where does work cross boundaries? Who is
expected to act next? What relies upon what? Which dependencies actually
prevent meaningful progress? Which obligations or time boundaries materially
affect operation?

**Trail-first procedure.** Work is discovered from evidence of **the last
actual instance** before any described or documented process is accepted as
the representation of how the work occurs. The trail is sought across four
**source categories** — the artifacts produced; the records and timestamps
they left; the correspondence around the work; and a stakeholder walking the
team through the last actual instance, step by step, in their own words.
**These are categories of trail evidence, not four limbs that must all exist
and all be obtained before the procedure counts as performed.**

**Pursued, not picked.** Every category **materially relevant** to the
workflow is pursued where it appears to exist and is reasonably accessible
within the authorized evidence boundary, and four things are recorded for
each: whether it **appears to exist**; whether it is **reasonably
accessible**; whether it was **actually obtained**; and **why it was not**,
where it was not. A materially relevant category that appears to exist is
never dropped because another category already produced a plausible trail.
**Prior familiarity with the workflow is not trail evidence** (A17 rule 6)
and satisfies none of this.

**The procedure is performed when** the materially relevant trail evidence
reasonably available within the authorized boundary has been obtained and
examined; every unobtained, unavailable, nonexistent or inaccessible category
is explicitly recorded; the actual-work trail is recorded **before** any
described or documented process is accepted as the workflow representation;
the described process is then recorded **as a separate row**; and the two are
compared. **It may be performed with evidentiary gaps remaining.** The team
does not begin from a process it already knows.

**A missing category does not void the procedure.** It bears on **evidentiary
sufficiency** — what findings may legitimately be made, the confidence and
context recorded through the method's existing non-scored vocabulary,
Instrument 5's sufficiency marker, and, through A18, possibly advancement.
The missing category and its reason stay visible. **Silence is never read as
agreement:** an unobtained source is never treated as though it would have
corroborated what was obtained.

**Performed is not sufficient.** That the procedure was performed establishes
nothing about whether the workflow is sufficiently understood. **A18 decides
that, independently** — *A10 performed, gaps remaining, A18 not satisfied* is
a permitted and expected state.

**The walkthrough is a category, not a gate.** A10 does not make a stakeholder
walkthrough a universal per-workflow completion requirement. Where one is
materially available and relevant to a **load-bearing** workflow it is
obtained, or the reason it was not is recorded. Where stakeholder evidence is
required by another provision of this method, that provision is unaffected by
anything here.

**Analytical distinctions** (used as aids, never imposed as vocabulary):

- *relies on* — one matter depends on another; work may still proceed;
- *cannot presently proceed because of* — the dependency currently prevents
  meaningful advancement; a time-bounded condition, not a property of the
  dependency;
- *expected next action transferred to* — who is now expected to act, on
  what, with what consequence; transfers expected action, never authority;
  acknowledgment is not completion;
- *obligation with a consequential time boundary* — a date whose passing has
  consequence, as distinct from a target date, a continuation requirement,
  or an expected follow-up.

**Questions for every handoff** (evidence-derived): what was handed off; to
whom; why; whether action is expected; current disposition; whether another
party is waiting on the result; whether escalation is required; **whether the
act has consequences the actor may not see** (A13).

**Next action, always.** For every matter, the **owner of the next expected
action** is recorded — or "unrecorded" — whether or not a handoff occurred.

**Neutrality rule.** Do not require Huerta Group's exact operational
vocabulary where the organization has different valid semantics. Use the
distinctions as analytical aids, not imposed terminology.

**Observation preference** (evidence-derived). Where a workflow is
load-bearing, observe it as performed rather than accept its description;
record the difference. It is a preference, and **not a fifth trail-source
category**.

**Output.** Workflow / Handoff / Dependency Inventory (Instrument 5).

### A11. Governance gap and governance-excess discovery

**Questions.** What matters require durable institutional authority? Where is
necessary authority missing or ambiguous? Where is governance duplicated or
contradictory? Where does excessive governance impede legitimate execution?
Which policies exist only as informal expectations?

**Excess test** (evidence-derived). Governance depth is measured against
operational load: governance that governs nothing operational, prescribes
mechanisms for work that does not occur, or is bypassed in practice by faster
direct authorization is recorded as excess or as drift — with the consequence
stated, not assumed to be harmful.

**Output.** Governance Gap / Excess Assessment (Instrument 8).

**Non-prescription.** Do not treat every governance gap as requiring formal
policy. Do not treat every friction point as a governance failure.

### A12. Institutional-memory risk discovery

**Principle** (mechanism-neutral). Institutionally significant knowledge should
become durable, organization-controlled, independently retrievable
institutional memory. Individual memory, conversation, contributor memory, and
transient working environments do not become authoritative merely because they
exist. A governed repository is one implementation of this principle, not the
universal one; the diagnostic assesses the principle, never a mechanism.

**Questions.** What critical knowledge exists only in individuals? What
institutional knowledge is trapped in transient systems? What knowledge would
be lost if a contributor left? Can the organization independently retrieve
significant decisions and operational history? Who controls durable
institutional memory? **What knowledge about the organization exists only
with its implementers, advisors, or vendors — including the diagnostic team
itself?** *Outside* is determined against the boundary recorded at A3 step 11;
where that boundary is unresolved, the question is recorded as unanswerable on
present evidence rather than answered against the team's own assumption.

**Evidence-derived supplementary questions.** Has the organization lost
significant records before, and how is that loss recorded? Is the chain by
which decisions travel from an accountable authority to the people who act
reliable, and is it recorded? Do corrections overwrite prior state or preserve
it?

**Output.** Institutional-Memory Risk Inventory (Instrument 6).

### A13. Systems and tooling discovery

**Questions.** Which systems support real operation? Which systems contain
authoritative information? Which systems duplicate or contradict one another?
Which tools impose obsolete workflows? Where is manual work appropriate? Where
is tooling absent but justified? **Which routine acts carry material,
irreversible, externally visible, governance, legal, financial, technical, or
institutional consequences — where the evidence warrants the question — and
does the organization's authorization practice carry that fact?**

**Rules** (evidence-derived). Current software is not proof of actual
workflow: observe the workflow, then the tool. A tool whose vocabulary cannot
express the organization's true condition is recorded as such (it is a finding
about the tool, not about the organization). Where a tool is used outside its
sanctioned path, record the path actually used. Which acts warrant the
consequence question is a judgement recorded with its basis; no list of
"risky acts" is imposed.

### A14. Operational friction and bottleneck discovery

**Questions.** Where is information repeatedly reconstructed? Where does
context get lost? Where do handoffs fail? Where does stale state persist?
Where are deadlines rediscovered instead of managed? Where are decisions
delayed? Where does work routinely bypass intended controls? **In how many
places is the same open matter kept?**

**Prompts** (evidence-derived, from six observed fragmentation problems):
state reconstruction (no single surface shows the current state; someone
assembles it); attention reconstruction (a fact exists somewhere without
becoming "action is required"); dependency invisibility (one party waits on
another without the wait being visible); temporal fragmentation (obligations
are documented correctly but invisible until someone revisits the record);
change detection (no concise answer to "what changed since I last looked");
context without authority distinction (working context is mistaken for
established fact).

**One-matter rule.** The A9 rule applies here: friction arising from one
matter kept in several places is recorded once, against the matter, with its
places counted and listed — never as several frictions. **Zero places is a
permitted and expected count**: a matter recorded nowhere at all is a row, not
an omission. Where that matter is a decision, the row cross-references
Instrument 2 rather than duplicating the authority map.

**Output.** Operational-Friction Inventory (Instrument 7).

### A15. Technology-worthiness

**Questions.** Is the underlying organizational problem sufficiently
understood? Is the workflow recurring enough to justify technical support?
Would technology preserve or clarify institutional meaning? Would technology
merely automate an unclear or dysfunctional process? Does an authoritative
system already exist? Is a new representation layer justified? Can manual
operation provide better learning first?

**Rule.** The outcome may legitimately be "no software intervention
warranted."

**Preceding question.** Before the test is applied to a problem: *does a
capability already exist — built, standby, or dormant — awaiting a decision
rather than a build?* A "yes" routes to the outcome *existing system should be improved rather than
replaced* or to Instrument 10, and the test proceeds only if the organization's
authority has decided otherwise. **Where no such authority is available — as in
any records-only diagnosis — the preceding question terminates the test for
that problem.** The seven questions below may still be answered **for the
record**, and answering them **cannot change the outcome**. That is a property
of a diagnosis conducted without the organization's authority, not a finding
about the problem.

**Evidence-derived test structure.** (1) Is the need *recurring*, on
evidence rather than anecdote? (2) Has it *outgrown* the organization's
existing documents and systems as the sole mechanism — a judgement by the
accountable authority, recorded as such? (3) Is the problem *understood* well
enough that automating it would not freeze premature assumptions? (4) Is the
*meaning* of what technology would encode established by the organization's
authority? (5) Does an *authoritative system already exist* that should be
improved rather than duplicated? (6) What would remain *deliberately manual*,
and why? (7) What would the technology be *structurally unable to do*? A
"need established" outcome authorizes nothing; authorization is the
organization's separate act.

**Output.** Technology-Worthiness Test (Instrument 9).

### A16. Questions reserved to client authority

**Purpose.** Explicitly separate diagnostic findings from decisions Huerta
Group does not have authority to make on behalf of the organization.

**Examples.** Organizational purpose; strategy; capital allocation; legal
structure; reserved decision rights; risk acceptance; employment authority;
policy adoption; major operating-model choices.

**Rule.** A question reserved to the organization's authority is recorded with
why the diagnostic team cannot decide it, which authority inside the
organization holds it, what depends on it, and the consequence if it remains
unresolved — and is not answered by the diagnostic. The instrument keeps its
established name; the thing it names is the organization's authority (A0).

**Output.** Questions Reserved to Client Authority (Instrument 10).

### A17. Evidence and provenance rules

1. Every material claim traces to its evidence and carries its class (A2).
2. Unavailable evidence is recorded as unavailable and never reconstructed from
   summaries, secondary description, memory, or conversation. A missing item
   is a register row with source **"not supplied," "not accessible," "not known
   to exist,"** or **"outside the authorized evidence boundary"** — the last
   for evidence the diagnostic was instructed not to obtain, which is a
   property of the engagement and not of the organization — and every finding
   that would have used it names the gap.
3. Contradictory evidence is preserved as contradictory, including
   disagreement between measurements; the diagnostic does not choose the
   flattering figure. **This includes contradiction within a single account.**
   Where one participant's statements conflict with each other, both are kept
   verbatim, cross-referenced, and recorded as a finding against the account —
   never merged, never reconciled by asking the participant to choose, and
   never resolved by the team preferring the statement that fits its other
   evidence. Where the two can be read as being about different objects, that
   reading is recorded as *Inferred* and identified as the team's, with what
   would settle it.
4. Absence of a record is not proof of non-occurrence without a stated basis
   for that inference.
5. **Confidential material.** The binding minimum, which governs whether or
   not any parent doctrine is available: **do not reproduce, transfer, abstract
   into another institutional corpus, or disclose client-specific or
   confidential material beyond what is necessary to perform and evidence the
   authorized diagnostic.** Where a claim can be supported without reproducing
   confidential substance, preserve **that the evidence exists, its provenance,
   the conclusion it supports within the authorized boundary, and any necessary
   limitation** — without copying the substance itself. Where a governing
   confidentiality doctrine applies to the engagement, it is cited and it
   governs; **the diagnosis records whether that doctrine was actually held by
   the team**, and where it was not held or cannot govern the engagement, the
   minimum above is the conservative default and the diagnosis says which it
   applied. Handling beyond the conservative default requires confidentiality
   terms or the organization's authority established **before** proceeding.
   Client-specific material is never converted into general institutional
   methodology without review, abstraction, confidentiality protection, and
   approval.
6. **Admissibility.** Every evidence item records how the team came to hold
   it — *method step*, *prior knowledge*, or *volunteered*. An item held by
   prior knowledge supports **no finding** until it has been obtained through
   a method step (requested, observed, or supplied); until then it is
   recorded as prior knowledge so that the method — not the team's
   familiarity — is what is evaluated. A volunteered item is recorded with its
   class and traced to the step that would have requested it; it does not
   excuse the step. **Where a prior-knowledge item conflicts with what the
   method step returns, both are recorded and so is the divergence.** The prior
   item still supports no finding; discarding it silently would leave no trace
   that the team's familiarity was wrong, while retaining it silently would let
   a right guess look like a derived answer. The rule polices what is
   admissible; recording the conflict is what makes the team's own calibration
   visible.
7. **Custody.** The diagnostic team's own working material about the
   organization — notes, extracts, registers, drafts, transient workspaces —
   is itself inventoried in Instrument 6 under *implementer-side / advisor-side
   custody*, and its custody disposition (returned, retained under agreement,
   destroyed) is agreed with the organization before the diagnosis closes.
   Significant organizational evidence does not become dependent on the team.
   **Where that agreement cannot be obtained, the condition is unsatisfied and
   the diagnosis may not be represented as an unqualified completed diagnosis**
   — it carries the unresolved-closure statement A18 requires, and its work,
   its outcome, and its preservation are unaffected.

### A18. Minimum diagnostic outputs and advancement logic

**Minimum outputs.** Evidence & Limitations Register; access statement;
**identity-of-record statement (or its explicit absence), produced by A4**;
Authority / Decision-Rights Map; Function / Responsibility Map; Workflow /
Handoff / Dependency Inventory; Source-of-Truth Inventory; Operational-Friction
Inventory; Institutional-Memory Risk Inventory; Governance Gap / Excess
Assessment; **explicit exclusions** (what must not enter any system; what
should remain manual; non-goals); Technology-Worthiness Test outcome; Questions
Reserved to Client Authority (Instrument 10).

**Where the exclusions come from.** Each limb is produced **where the
diagnostic domain that discovers it sits** — what must not enter a system
ordinarily from A13 and A15; what should remain manual from A13, A14 and A15;
non-goals from A4, A11 and A16 — and no limb is assigned to a single universal
stage. A18 **aggregates** them as a required final output; it does not
originate them.

**Rule.** Diagnosis does not advance to design merely because data collection
is complete.

**Minimum evidence before design.** Material evidence limitations are
explicit; organizational purpose and outputs are sufficiently understood;
authority/decision-rights ambiguity relevant to the problem is visible;
**source-of-truth boundaries are sufficiently identified for diagnosis —
including unresolved or contested authority where that is the truthful
finding**, which is what the test asks for and is not a requirement that every
source authority be institutionally established before diagnosis may proceed;
critical workflows and dependencies are sufficiently understood; major
diagnostic contradictions have been preserved rather than hidden; questions
requiring the organization's authority are separated from analytical findings;
prescription would not depend primarily upon unverified inference.

**How the judgement is recorded.** Each element of the minimum-evidence test
is answered with the same evidence classes as a finding (Observed /
Client-Stated / Inferred) and with its evidence. The outcome carries the
**re-derived two-pass access statement** (A3 step 10) and, where no stakeholder
account was obtained, the label **"records-only."**

**Disclosure of the diagnostic team's position.** Stated **before any
finding**, as one block:

1. **Who the diagnostic team is** — the institutional actor and, where they
   differ, the individual or agent actor materially performing the diagnostic.
   Both are named; neither substitutes for the other.
2. **Author-of-records**, where the team authored records it is judging.
3. **Account and record share a source** — marked on each such comparison,
   with the outcome stating how many corroborations carry the mark: agreement
   between one source and itself is consistency, not independent corroboration
   (A2 rule 6).
4. **Material prior involvement.** Where the diagnosing actor previously
   participated in work that could reasonably shape its interpretation of the
   organization — designing, constructing, governing, implementing, or
   materially evaluating its systems; authoring substantial portions of the
   evidence now diagnosed; or repeatedly advising on the same operational
   structures now under diagnosis — the outcome states **who or what
   participated, in what capacity, over what portion of the organization,
   whether the diagnosis therefore lacks independence in that area, and what
   limitation that creates**. Mere incidental familiarity, one-off exposure, or
   ordinary awareness does not by itself make a diagnosis non-independent, and
   is not inflated into a disclosure that says it does.
   **Institutional involvement is the default reading**, because replacing an
   individual or agent does not remove prior involvement by the institution
   that designed, constructed, or governed the organization; **individual or
   agent authorship is disclosed as well** where relevant, since it can create
   an additional layer of dependence. This governs disclosure **within this
   diagnostic** and establishes no general independence doctrine for other
   work.

**Which evidence was absent.** The outcome **names the categories of evidence
that were not obtained** — at least: participant accounts; observation of work
as performed; the organization's operating systems; and any category the
engagement placed outside the authorized evidence boundary — each with **the
constraint it triggers** on what may be concluded. The categories are named
rather than summarized into a second label, because "records-only" says which
half was present and nothing about which half was missing.

**Advancement state, declared separately from completeness.** The outcome
states the advancement state **in the method's own words** — one of the five
outcomes below — and states it **separately from how complete the instruments
look**. A fully populated instrument set is not evidence of sufficiency; it is
evidence that a corpus was large. The declaration and the per-instrument
sufficiency marker (Part B) are the two places this is stated, and neither is
expressed as a number, a percentage, a score, a level, or an index.

**Possible outcomes.** Proceed to Establish Meaning & Authority. Request
additional evidence. **Return unresolved questions to the organization's
authority.** Narrow the engagement problem. Conclude that no material
intervention is presently warranted.

**The outcome is not the instrument.** *Return unresolved questions to the
organization's authority* is an **advancement action** — one of five states a
diagnosis can end in. **Instrument 10 is a required output of every
diagnosis**, maintained throughout, whatever the outcome. A diagnosis that
proceeds still produces Instrument 10; a diagnosis that returns questions
still had to produce it first. They are not the same object and are never
named as one.

**Unresolved closure, where a mandatory closure condition fails.** A diagnosis
whose mandatory closure condition is unsatisfied (A17 rule 7) **may close as an
application record** — the application may stop, its work is preserved, its
outcome is returned — but **may not be represented as an unqualified completed
diagnosis**. It carries a conspicuous **unresolved-closure statement** naming
**the unsatisfied condition, why it remains unsatisfied, who or what must
satisfy it if known, and what consequence the unresolved condition has for use
of the diagnosis**. The statement sits **beside** the outcome and is **not a
sixth outcome**: it is a condition on the status and interpretation of the
record.

**Four states, kept apart.** *Application or stage work completed* · *subject
diagnosis complete* · *advancement permitted* · *closure condition
unresolved*. They are independent, and the first never implies the second.
**Language rule:** do not use "closed," "complete," or equivalent unqualified
language of the subject diagnosis while a mandatory closure condition remains
unsatisfied. This is closure semantics for this diagnostic only; it defines no
general project lifecycle.

**Constraints on outcomes.** A **records-only** diagnosis may produce only
*request additional evidence*, *return unresolved questions to the
organization's authority*, or *narrow the engagement problem*. A diagnosis
whose access statement records "none" for a corpus, system, or person that a
material finding depends on cannot *proceed* on that finding.

### A19. Non-prescription rules

Do not prescribe software during initial discovery merely because software
could be built. Do not impose Huerta Group governance structures. Do not create authority for the organization. Do not resolve the
organization's strategic choices on its behalf. Do not infer missing organizational facts merely to make the
diagnostic complete. Do not treat formal org charts as proof of actual
authority. Do not treat current software as proof of actual workflow. Do not
treat stakeholder consensus as proof of factual accuracy. Do not treat a
missing documented process as proof that no real process exists. Do not treat
every organizational friction point as a governance failure. Do not treat every
governance gap as requiring formal policy. Do not treat every manual activity
as an automation opportunity. Do not conceal contradictory evidence.

### A20. Known limitations

Self-application does not establish external validity. Huerta Group and HGP
are related cases, not independent external organizations. Founder-led
governance may not generalize to distributed authority structures. Repository
maturity in Huerta Group may create evidence access not available elsewhere.
Internal participants may share context unavailable in a real engagement.
Hostile, low-trust, political, or uncooperative stakeholder environments are
not fully testable internally. Large organizational scale is not validated.
Regulated-industry applicability is not validated merely through Huerta Group
use. Cross-cultural and multinational applicability remains untested.

Evidence-derived additions: at the freeze of this version the people-facing
procedures of A1, A3, A6–A8 and A10 have been exercised **once** — in one
written, asynchronous session with one participant, followed by one
clarification round. **A10's observation of work as performed has never been
exercised at all**, and every finding that would depend on it is therefore
untested. No instrument has been used more than twice. The evidence base is
weeks old and single-operator, and in that application the participant was
also the author or approver of most records compared against — a condition no
internal application can remove. **Habit-held responsibility was asked for and
not obtained**; in a single-actor organization the distinction between habit
and role may be unobtainable without observation (A7). The A5 consumption
classification has been informative for one founding-stage organization and
may not stay informative elsewhere. The conditional neutral terms of A1 may
lose precision; only a second organization can test that. Whether
single-actor organizations warrant a distinct diagnostic case is undecided.
Nothing in this version has been exercised against multiple participants,
distributed or contested authority, refusal, delay, political disagreement,
organizational scale, a legacy estate, or the organization's expectations.

**The observation-dependent tests, listed together.** A7's habit test and
**A6's role-compression test are equally observation-dependent** — establishing
that every decision class resolves to one actor needs observed authority for
every class, exactly as establishing habit needs observed work. An application
reading an earlier limitations list would have thought A6 the safer of the two;
it is not, and two authors read that test differently in part because this list
did not say so. **A9's source roles carry the same dependence**: the Stage 4
widening of *source of resort* into six source roles made Instrument 4
substantially more account-dependent than the field it replaced, and a
records-only application will find most of that column unobtainable. A
limitations list that is selectively complete misleads exactly the careful
reader who trusts it.

**An ambiguity carried through Draft v4, and since determined.** A10's
trail-first procedure lists four forms of evidence — artifacts, records and
timestamps, correspondence, and a stakeholder walking the team through the
last actual instance — and, **through Draft v4, did not say whether they were
conjunctive**. Two authors read it differently: one treated a records-only
trail as satisfying the procedure, the other treated the walkthrough as
required and recorded the step as unperformed. The Founder Office determined
the question at `fd-0028`: the four are **source categories**, not a
conjunctive gate, and A10 above is reconciled accordingly. **The divergence is
preserved as methodology-development evidence that the wording was genuinely
ambiguous before determination — neither author applied the method
incorrectly**, and the determination does not make one of them retrospectively
right. **What the determination did not do is test anything:** the reconciled
A10 carries Founder Office semantic authority and no application evidence, and
A10's observation limb remains unexercised.

**Limitations this version introduced and has not tested.** Every change in
this version is untested as applied. The evidence-model additions (source kind;
governed-vocabulary adoption; the evidence-boundary value; the presupposition
value), A18's disclosure, absent-category naming, advancement-state
declaration and unresolved-closure statement, the per-instrument sufficiency
marker, and A4's identity-of-record output have **never been used in an
application**. Each was proposed on evidence that something was missing; none
was proposed on evidence that this is the right thing to put there.

## Part B — Provisional Working Instruments

**Status: PROVISIONAL WORKING FORMS — NOT STANDARDIZED.** Global rules: no
numerical scoring; no maturity levels; no weighted formulas; no automated
conclusion generation; no standardized consulting questionnaire; no software
implementation; no implication that these forms are final standards. Every form is a table or list kept by hand; every entry cites evidence and
carries an evidence class; every form's header carries the access statement
(A3 step 10) and, where applicable, the label "records-only."

**At the head of every instrument, before its first row**, two further things
appear — not only in a report header, because a label three lines above several
thousand words of filled table is not where a reader meets the evidence:

1. **The advancement-state declaration** (A18), stated in the method's own
   words and separate from how complete the form looks.
2. **An evidence-sufficiency marker for that instrument**, exactly one of
   three values already in the method's vocabulary — **completed** ·
   **completed with named gaps** · **blocked by insufficient evidence** — with
   **the reason** in prose. The marker is per instrument and is **never
   aggregated, averaged, or combined across instruments**, and never expressed
   as a percentage, score, level, index, or traffic light. Three words and a
   reason.

A populated form is evidence that a corpus was large. It is not evidence that
the evidence was sufficient, and the marker is where the difference is stated.

### Instrument 1 — Evidence & Limitations Register

- **Stage used:** A3 (opened first; maintained throughout).
- **Purpose:** the single place where every material evidence item, its
  source, its class, its limits, and the way the team came to hold it are
  recorded, so that every finding can be traced back and every gap is
  visible.
- **Minimum fields:** evidence identifier; source; source owner; evidence
  class (observed / client-stated / inferred); **source kind** — what kind of
  thing supplied the claim (person or stakeholder account / organizational or
  institutional record / supplied institutional source / systematic
  verification or validation record / another kind the evidence requires),
  descriptive and non-ordinal (A2 rule 5); **obtained via (method step /
  prior knowledge / volunteered / follow-up)**; subject; what the evidence
  supports; **stated force, in the speaker's own words** (the qualifier the
  speaker used, quoted, or "none"); **opposing account (sought / not sought /
  sought and unavailable, with the reason)**; **shares a source with** (the
  record the same party authored or approved, where this row corroborates
  one); limitations; conflicts; confidence/context; provenance.
- **Recording rules:** one row per evidence item; "what the evidence
  supports" states the claim, not the conclusion; conflicts cross-reference
  the conflicting row; provenance records how and when the item was obtained
  and by whom; items requested but not supplied are rows with source "not
  supplied," "not accessible," or "not known to exist"; **"cited, not
  held"** is a row type for authorities a record cites that the organization
  does not hold; **the organization's own terms** (A1) are rows, with who
  used the term and where; a supplied document yields **two rows** where both
  its existence and its asserted condition matter (existence and content:
  observed; asserted condition: client-stated); a prior-knowledge row
  supports no finding until a method-step row exists for the same item;
  confidence/context is prose, never a score; **the source kind never carries
  quality** — it is not a rank, not a strength, and not a tie-breaker, and
  evidence quality is expressed only through limitations, conflicts,
  provenance, freshness, coverage, and confidence/context; **each question limb
  is its own row**, recorded *answered* or *not supplied*; **"response does not address
  the question asked"** is a row type, recording what was asked, what was
  answered, and whether a follow-up was taken; a clarification obtained by
  follow-up is a **new row with its own date** that adds to the original row
  and never replaces, annotates, or supersedes it — and the fact that the
  original was under-determined is itself preserved; "stated force" quotes the speaker's own qualifier **and, for a document,
  the document's own qualifier** — "draft," "provisional," "as of," "believed
  to be," quoted verbatim under the same rule, since in a records-only
  diagnosis the document qualifier is the only form the field can take — and
  the team assigns no category of its own to a speaker's state of mind or to a
  document's; "opposing account" is filled **per row by default** for every row
  a finding relies on, so that unopposed evidence is never read as corroborated
  (A2 rule 6) — a **register-wide statement** of the value is permitted **only**
  where the value is genuinely uniform across the register, states the value
  and its reason once, and records **every exception inline**, because sixty
  identical fields bury the rows that differ.

### Instrument 2 — Authority / Decision-Rights Map

- **Stage used:** A6 (also informs A8, A11, A16).
- **Purpose:** make visible who may decide what, who actually does, where
  the two diverge or no one can say, and where several decision rights rest
  on one actor.
- **Minimum fields:** decision/domain; formal authority; observed authority;
  **delegated authority and the instrument delegating it**; **informal
  authority** — exercised without a formal grant, with what evidences the
  exercise; **contested (each claim and its basis)**; **anticipated but not established (with the trigger
  the organization names)**; **held by the same actor as…**; responsible
  function/role; required consultation/input; escalation path; evidence;
  ambiguity/conflict.
- **Recording rules:** "formal authority" is Client-Stated unless a governing
  instrument was observed; "observed authority" cites decisions actually
  made; "unresolved" is a permitted and expected value for either;
  consultation and escalation are recorded as "none evidenced (basis: …)" or
  "none formally required (instrument: …)" — **never "not applicable"**;
  where every row resolves to one actor, a **role-compression finding** is
  recorded against the map with its continuity consequence and the evidence
  that established it; the map never assigns authority — it records or
  records the absence of it; responsibility is recorded separately from
  authority even where the same person holds both; **authority concentration,
  wherever found, is recorded as an observed condition of this organization at
  this time**, with its date and evidence — never as its permanent structure
  and never as the method's default; concentration, distribution, delegation,
  contest, and unresolved authority carry the same fields and the same weight;
  an intended future delegation is recorded as *anticipated but not
  established* and changes nothing about the present rows.

### Instrument 3 — Function / Responsibility Map

- **Stage used:** A7.
- **Purpose:** record the functions that demonstrably exist, what they are
  assigned and observed to do, what evidences each, and where responsibility
  is duplicated, unowned, or held only by habit.
- **Minimum fields:** function; purpose; **evidenced by (work / document
  only / name only / named by an account only / not evidenced)**; formal
  responsibilities; observed responsibilities; key outputs; dependencies;
  overlap; unowned work; evidence.
- **Recording rules:** functions are derived from evidence of work, never
  enumerated from a chart; "unowned work" is recorded against the map as a
  whole where no function claims it; "observed responsibilities" that no
  formal assignment covers are flagged as habit- or individual-held; a function evidenced by name only is not a finding that it does nothing; a
  **participant-supplied inventory** of functions is recorded as Client-Stated
  against the question it answered and compared with the evidenced set, and
  neither replaces the other; **habit-held work is established by observing
  work as performed, not by asking** — where observation was unavailable the
  map records "capability not exercised — observation unavailable," never "no
  habit-held work found."

### Instrument 4 — Source-of-Truth Inventory

- **Stage used:** A9 (informs A13, A15).
- **Purpose:** for each information domain that matters operationally,
  establish the one authoritative source, its owner, whether the organization
  can retrieve it, and every representation of it — so that duplication,
  contradiction, staleness, and "authority by convenience" become visible.
- **Minimum fields:** information domain; authoritative source; source owner;
  **retrievable by the organization (yes / no / partial, with basis)**;
  **source roles** — where the matter is first worked through · where it is
  clarified · where it is routed from · who decides it · where the fact is
  established · where the durable record resides, each with its class and
  "none evidenced" permitted; **source of resort, per participant**
  (Client-Stated: where they actually go for a working answer) — the first
  three roles are ordinarily obtainable only from participants and are recorded
  **"not obtainable from records"** where no account exists, which is a
  different entry from *none evidenced*;
  representational systems; duplicate/conflicting sources; freshness
  concerns; access limitations; evidence.
- **Recording rules:** "authoritative source" may be "unresolved" — it is
  never appointed by the diagnostic; the three facts of A9 (exists /
  confidence / treated as authoritative) are recorded separately with their
  own classes; conversation, working notes, and speculation are recorded as
  "never authoritative by existence"; a representational system that is
  treated as authoritative in practice is recorded as such under "conflicts";
  one matter in several systems is one row with N representations (A9);
  "retrievable" is the pointer — the loss-risk analysis lives in Instrument
  6; freshness concerns are described qualitatively, never scored; **no source
  role makes a source authoritative** — an environment used for sensemaking,
  clarification, routing, or resort is recorded in that role and becomes
  authoritative only by the organization's own act, instrument, or practice;
  a **source of resort that diverges from the authoritative source** is
  recorded under conflicts, with both, and the divergence is a finding rather
  than an error to correct.

### Instrument 5 — Workflow / Handoff / Dependency Inventory

- **Stage used:** A10 (informs A8, A14).
- **Purpose:** record recurring work as it actually flows — who acts, what is
  handed to whom, who is expected to act next, what relies on what, what
  presently prevents progress, and which time boundaries carry consequence.
- **Minimum fields:** workflow/matter; trigger; participants; expected
  outputs; handoffs; **owner of the next expected action (or unrecorded)**;
  dependencies; blocking conditions; obligations/time boundaries; failure
  points; evidence.
- **Recording rules:** each workflow has a **trail row** (as discovered from
  artifacts and the last actual instance) before any **described row**;
  each handoff answers the eight handoff questions (A10); "dependencies" and
  "blocking conditions" are separate columns — a dependency is recorded as
  blocking only where evidence shows work cannot presently proceed;
  obligations record the consequence of the boundary passing; observed flow
  and described flow are both recorded where they differ; **the trail row
  records, for each materially relevant trail-source category, whether it
  appears to exist, whether it is reasonably accessible, whether it was
  obtained, and why not where it was not** (A10); and the instrument's
  evidence-sufficiency marker (Part B) is set truthfully rather than by habit
  — **completed** where the materially relevant trail categories were obtained
  and no material evidentiary gap remains; **completed with named gaps** where
  the procedure was performed, one or more materially relevant categories were
  unavailable or unobtained, and the remaining trail still supports a
  meaningful workflow representation; **blocked by insufficient evidence**
  where the available trail is too incomplete to support one. **An
  artifact-derived or partial trail is not marked *completed* automatically**,
  and the marker never reports the procedure's completion in place of the
  evidence's sufficiency.

### Instrument 6 — Institutional-Memory Risk Inventory

- **Stage used:** A12.
- **Purpose:** locate knowledge the organization would lose, cannot retrieve,
  or does not control — including knowledge held only by its implementers,
  advisors, vendors, or the diagnostic team — without prescribing how it
  should be kept.
- **Minimum fields:** knowledge/domain; current holder/location;
  organizational control; independent retrievability; authority status; loss
  risk; continuity consequence; evidence.
- **Recording rules:** "current holder/location" may be a person, a
  conversation, a transient system, a personal device, a durable
  organization-controlled record, or **implementer-side / advisor-side
  custody** (a row type: knowledge held by a consultant, developer, vendor,
  transient workspace, or implementer-controlled system — the diagnostic
  team's own material included, per A17 rule 7) — recorded as found;
  "organizational control" and "independent retrievability" are answered as
  yes / no / partial with the basis stated; "authority status" records
  whether the organization treats the item as authoritative and on what
  basis; loss risk and continuity consequence are prose; **"outside the organization"
  is read against the boundary recorded at A3 step 11**, and where that
  boundary is unresolved the row records the holding and the unresolved
  boundary rather than a judgement about inside or outside.

### Instrument 7 — Operational-Friction Inventory

- **Stage used:** A14 (informs A15).
- **Purpose:** record where work is reconstructed, context lost, state stale,
  obligations rediscovered, controls bypassed, or one matter scattered — with
  frequency and consequence — as the evidence base for any later
  intervention.
- **Minimum fields:** friction; where observed; **places recorded (count and
  list)**; frequency; consequence; affected roles/functions; underlying cause
  known/unknown; current workaround; evidence.
- **Recording rules:** "underlying cause" is recorded as unknown where it is
  unknown; "current workaround" records what people actually do, including
  bypassing intended controls; frequency is counted where possible and characterized otherwise, and the
  difference is stated; one matter kept in several places is one row (A14);
  **"places recorded" may be zero**, and a matter recorded nowhere at all is a
  row rather than an omission — where that matter is a decision, the row
  cross-references Instrument 2 instead of duplicating the authority map.

### Instrument 8 — Governance Gap / Excess Assessment

- **Stage used:** A11.
- **Purpose:** identify where durable authority is missing, ambiguous,
  duplicated, contradictory, or in excess of operational load — as findings,
  never as policy.
- **Minimum fields:** subject; current authority/governance; gap / excess /
  ambiguity / conflict; operational consequence; evidence; client authority
  required; diagnostic recommendation only.
- **Recording rules:** every row ends in "diagnostic recommendation only" —
  a description of what the organization might decide, never a decision;
  "excess" is asserted only against evidence of operational load or bypass;
  "client authority required" names the authority, or "unresolved."

### Instrument 9 — Technology-Worthiness Test

- **Stage used:** A15 (after Instruments 4, 5, 7 for the problem in question).
- **Purpose:** decide, for one diagnosed recurring problem at a time, whether
  a technical intervention is warranted, premature, or unnecessary.
- **Preceding question:** does a capability already exist — built, standby,
  or dormant — awaiting a decision rather than a build? If yes, record the
  outcome *existing system should be improved rather than replaced* or refer
  the decision to Instrument 10, and continue only if the organization's
  authority has decided otherwise. **Where no such authority is available, the
  preceding question terminates the test**: the questions below may be answered
  for the record and cannot change the outcome, which is a property of a
  diagnosis conducted without the organization's authority and not a finding
  about the problem.
- **Minimum questions:** Is the problem recurring? Is the problem
  sufficiently understood? Is authoritative ownership understood? Would
  technology reduce material friction? Would technology duplicate an existing
  authoritative system? Would technology freeze premature assumptions? Can
  manual operation generate needed learning first? Is technical
  representation likely to preserve institutional meaning?
- **Permitted outcomes:** Technical intervention appears warranted. Manual /
  process intervention should precede technology. Existing system should be
  improved rather than replaced. Insufficient evidence. No technical
  intervention presently warranted.
- **Recording rules:** one test per problem; each question answered in prose
  with evidence, never as a score; the outcome is one of the five permitted
  outcomes and nothing else; "warranted" is a finding about need, never an
  authorization to build.

### Instrument 10 — Questions Reserved to Client Authority

- **Stage used:** A16 (maintained throughout).
- **Purpose:** keep the decisions that belong to the organization out of the
  diagnostic's findings, while making their dependencies and consequences
  visible.
- **Minimum fields:** question; why diagnostic team cannot decide it;
  responsible client authority; evidence/context; decision dependency;
  consequence if unresolved.
- **Recording rules:** the question is stated neutrally, without a
  recommended answer; "responsible client authority" may be "unresolved"
  and, if so, that is itself a finding for Instrument 2; "decision
  dependency" names the diagnostic outputs or later stages that wait on it.

## Part C — What this draft is, and is not

- It is a **provisional draft** (Methodology Foundation v1 §5.1: *draft*) of a
  subordinate methodology asset under the Methodology Foundation v1's
  organizational understanding, assessment, and analysis domains, situated at
  steps §3.1–§3.3 of the approved progression (mr-0004).
- It is **not** an approved internal method, a Method Library record, a
  standard, an instrument set of any established status, a consulting
  lifecycle, a phase model, a questionnaire, a scoring model, a maturity model,
  or software.
- It is **not designated "validated through use."** The Founder Office withheld
  that designation expressly (`fd-0022` §2.1) after the first stakeholder
  application, and this version does not claim it, imply it, or approach it by
  accumulation.
- **It is untested as a version.** Draft v1 was applied once, in a
  self-application dry run. Draft v2 was applied once, in a structured
  stakeholder-evidence application on the same organization followed by one
  clarification round, producing seventy-three classified comparisons, eight
  preserved contradictions, and a refusal to advance. Draft v3 was applied
  twice — once to a subordinate unit, and once again to that same unit by a
  second author working from the method text alone. **Draft v4 was applied to
  nothing, and neither has Draft v5 been.**
- **What this version inherits, and what it does not.** Where it leaves an
  element materially unchanged, that element carries the evidence its
  predecessors earned. Where it changes an element, the element carries **the
  rationale for the change and none of the validation** — the evidence that
  something was missing is not evidence that this is the right thing to put
  there. Which elements are which is recorded item by item in Part D, and the
  distinction is not blurred by describing the version as a whole.
- **None of it establishes external validity**, general applicability, or
  validated-through-use status. Every application to date is on one
  organization or a unit of it, by a diagnostic team with material prior
  involvement in the method, the institution, or the subject — a condition no
  internal application can remove, and one A18 now requires disclosed wherever
  it recurs.
- The maximum claim any record may make about it is: *provisional
  organizational diagnostic, consolidated from a tested predecessor,
  reconciled to one Founder Office methodology determination, and itself
  untested as a version — self-application evidence, one internal
  stakeholder-evidence application with one clarification round, one internal
  application to a subordinate unit, one second-author replication of that
  application, documented deficiencies and dispositions, and a total remaining
  external-validation gap.*
- Where it and any governing source could be read differently, the governing
  source wins.
