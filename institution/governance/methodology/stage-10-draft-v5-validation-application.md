I used *Client-Stated* unchanged, because CS-16 is expressly a terminology pass with no doctrine changes and renaming an evidence class would be a doctrine change. **The consequence is small but real: CS-16's settlement is not exhaustive, and it reads as though it is.** A secondary effect surfaced in application: A2 defines *Client-Stated* as *"asserted by an organizational participant or supplied institutional source"* — in a records-only diagnosis the first disjunct is empty by construction, so the class name evokes a participant who is never there.

### MF-2 — A2 has no recurrence axis, and the subject's own scheme has one
1. **Evidence.** Recording E-09 to E-12 in the register. The Founder Validation Program's Evidence Strength scale (E-08 §5) runs *Single Occurrence → Repeated by Founder → Repeated Across Multiple Books → Repeated Across Multiple Authors → Independently Corroborated*. I needed to express that FVO-001-001 is **one occurrence, observed by the implementer of the thing observed, and the entire operating history of the program**. A2's three classes cannot say this: the observation is *Observed* as to the defect and *Client-Stated* as to the condition, and both classes are silent on recurrence and on observer-independence.
2. **Affected section.** A2, the three classes and rule 6; read against A1's block *Where the organization's apparatus reaches evidence itself*.
3. **Kind of problem.** **Failure to produce a required representation** — A2 cannot represent a distinction the diagnosis materially needed.
4. **Consequence.** I expressed it through limitations and confidence/context prose, as A2 rule 5 directs, and it works — but it works by narration rather than by structure, so it is invisible to anyone scanning classes. **A1's instruction was followed exactly: the observation is preserved as evidence for later methodology evolution and was not substituted in during the engagement.** This finding is that preservation. Note what makes it strong evidence rather than a preference: A2 rule 6's own final sentence reaches for exactly this axis (*"account and record share a source"* is a statement about observer-independence) without having a place to record it. **The subject's scheme has the axis the method's own rule 6 needs.**

### MF-3 — A18 item 3's mark cannot attach in a records-only diagnosis, where its risk is highest
1. **Evidence.** Writing §1 item 3. A18 requires *"the outcome stating how many corroborations carry the mark."* With no accounts, the count is zero — while every record in the corpus shares a source with every other record, which is the maximum of the condition the mark exists to expose.
2. **Affected section.** A18, *Disclosure of the diagnostic team's position*, item 3; A2 rule 6's final sentence.
3. **Kind of problem.** **Failure to produce a required representation**, conditional on the diagnosis type.
4. **Consequence.** The disclosure block reports **zero** at exactly the moment the underlying risk is total. A reader who takes the count at face value — and the count is presented as a count, with no accompanying condition — reads the safest possible number off the least independent possible diagnosis. **I wrote the explanation in §1 because the number alone would mislead, but the method does not require that explanation and a less wary applier would simply write "zero."** The mark is framed for account-versus-record and has no form for record-versus-record.

### MF-4 — *opposing account* is the wrong field name for the condition it must record in a records-only diagnosis
1. **Evidence.** Filling Instrument 1's *opposing account* field. In records-only there are no accounts, so the value is uniformly *not sought* — but the register nonetheless contains **four preserved contradictions between records** (C-1 to C-4). The field whose purpose is to stop unopposed evidence being read as corroborated is uniformly empty at the same moment the register holds four genuine oppositions, because they are the wrong kind of object for the field.
2. **Affected section.** Instrument 1, minimum fields and the *opposing account* recording rule; A2 rule 6.
3. **Kind of problem.** **Ambiguity**, with a presentation consequence.
4. **Consequence.** A2 rule 6's principle is about a second **source**, and it applies to records as much as to people — the rule's own wording says *"only one participant, source, or side."* The field name says *account*. **The register-wide statement mechanism made this worse rather than better:** it is permitted only where the value is genuinely uniform with every exception inline, and the value *is* uniform, so I stated it once and recorded no exceptions — leaving the four record-versus-record contradictions to live only in a separate contradictions list rather than against the rows they oppose. That is defensible under the rule as written, and it is exactly the burial the rule was added to prevent. **I want to be careful here: this is a genuine ambiguity and not a failure. The rule prevented a different burial (sixty identical fields) and I do not think it should be reversed.**

### MF-5 — Part B's per-instrument header requirement is unworkable at literal compliance, and I deviated
1. **Evidence.** Writing the headers for Instruments 1 through 10. Part B requires **at the head of every instrument, before its first row**: the access statement (A3 step 10), the advancement-state declaration, and the sufficiency marker with its reason. A3 step 10's access statement in this application is a **two-pass, four-condition table covering eleven identified items and thirteen access determinations** — roughly fifty lines. **Ten instruments × a fifty-line statement is five hundred lines of verbatim repetition, in a diagnosis where the statement is identical across all ten because it was re-derived zero times.**
2. **Affected section.** Part B, global rules, the block beginning *"At the head of every instrument, before its first row"*; A3 step 10's *"carried into the header of every instrument."*
3. **Kind of problem.** **Usability/presentation defect**, with an unworkable-requirement edge.
4. **Consequence, stated as what I actually did rather than as a hypothetical.** **I did not comply literally.** I carried, at each instrument head, an explicit reference to A3-O2 as the statement in force plus the access determinations material to that instrument, together with the full advancement-state declaration and the full marker and reason. **That is a deviation from the method text and I am recording it as one rather than presenting it as compliance.** My reason: full repetition would have buried the per-instrument marker — the one element that genuinely differs between instruments and the element Part B's own rationale says the reader must meet at the evidence. **This is the same failure mode Instrument 1's opposing-account rule identifies in so many words — *"because sixty identical fields bury the rows that differ"* — and Part B mandates it.** The method contains the diagnosis of the problem in one place and the cause of it in another.
5. **Honest counter-consideration.** Part B's rationale is correct: *"a label three lines above several thousand words of filled table is not where a reader meets the evidence."* The defect is not the requirement's purpose but its failure to distinguish the element that varies per instrument (the marker) from the element that is constant across a whole diagnosis (the access statement, when re-derived zero times).

### MF-6 — A10's four categories are unequal in obtainability, and *correspondence* has no defined location
1. **Evidence.** Filling the four A10 tables at §6. Three of the four categories have obvious loci in a software venture: artifacts are files, records and timestamps are version control, a walkthrough is a person. **Correspondence has no locus the method names, and no locus the subject's records name.** I recorded *existence unknown* on W1 and W3 — which is correct — but I reached that entry by reasoning about where correspondence *might* live (GitHub, email, chat) rather than by checking anywhere the method or the subject identified.
2. **Affected section.** A10, *Trail-first procedure*, the third source category; A10, *Pursued, not picked* (the *appears to exist* test).
3. **Kind of problem.** **Ambiguity** in an evidence category, with a downstream effect on a recording state.
4. **Consequence.** *"Whether it appears to exist"* is the first of the four per-category facts, and for correspondence in this subject I could not determine it without a participant — so **the first fact of a category is itself participant-dependent**, which A10 does not anticipate. This pushes correspondence toward *existence unknown* by default in any records-only application, and *existence unknown* is the weakest of the five states: it constrains nothing and supports nothing. **A20's non-selective limitations list does not mention this**, and it belongs there alongside the A6/A7/A9 group, because it is the same kind of hidden participant-dependence in a limb that looks records-reachable.

### MF-7 — A15's preceding question fires on a *documented* dormant capability, and the method does not say whether documentation is enough
1. **Evidence.** Applying Instrument 9's preceding question to FR-1. My "yes" rests on E-13's statement that `supabase_migrations.schema_migrations` is EMPTY and on E-14's naming of a *"Preferred application path."* **I have not observed the table, because the database is outside the boundary.** So the preceding question — which terminates the whole test — fired on **Client-Stated** evidence about a capability's existence.
2. **Affected section.** A15, *Preceding question*; Instrument 9, *Preceding question*.
3. **Kind of problem.** **Ambiguity** — the evidentiary standard for the preceding question is unstated.
4. **Consequence.** The preceding question is the most consequential single question in Instrument 9: a "yes" terminates the test in a records-only diagnosis and fixes the outcome. **A18's constraint that a diagnosis recording "none" for a system cannot *proceed* on a finding that depends on it does not obviously reach here**, because the terminated test does not *proceed* — it stops, which is the conservative direction. **I judged that stopping on Client-Stated evidence was safe precisely because it is conservative, and I want that judgement visible rather than buried**, since the method does not authorize it and a differently-inclined applier could reason the other way and continue the test. There is a second-order effect worth naming: because a "yes" is conservative and a "no" is not, an applier under time pressure has a standing incentive to answer "yes" on thin evidence and stop.
