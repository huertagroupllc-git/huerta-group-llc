## 1. Why the reconciliation was prepared and not applied

`fd-0028` §2.9 carries the instrument's own stop rule:

> Use the repository's existing methodology/versioning rules to determine
> whether this bounded Founder Office reconciliation remains Draft v4 at a new
> recorded digest/revision state or requires another repository-native
> representation. **Do not create Draft v5. If existing governance cannot
> represent an authorized same-version semantic reconciliation truthfully
> without creating Draft v5, stop before choosing and return that
> repository-governance issue.**

The governing rule is **Methodology Foundation v1 §6.4**, quoted in full:

> A **non-substantive correction** may update: Spelling. Formatting. Broken
> references. Metadata errors. Clerical inconsistencies.
>
> A **substantive refinement** changes: Meaning. Scope. Requirements.
> Methodological logic. Decision criteria. Required evidence. Approved
> application. Limitations. Disclosure boundaries.
>
> **Substantive refinements require a new version and approval appropriate to
> the change.**

**The A10 reconciliation is substantive on four of the nine listed dimensions**
and on none of the five non-substantive ones:

| §6.4 dimension | How the reconciliation changes it |
| --- | --- |
| **Meaning** | The four-item list changes from a conjunctive gate (on one faithful reading) to source categories |
| **Requirements** | Four facts must now be determined and recorded per materially relevant category; a category that appears to exist may not be silently omitted |
| **Required evidence** | A stakeholder walkthrough ceases to be a precondition of the procedure; the materially-relevant-and-reasonably-accessible standard replaces it |
| **Limitations** | A20's sole unresolved limitation is removed and rewritten as a determined-and-reconciled note |

**Two further facts bear on the choice, and neither settles it.**

1. **The program has applied §6.4 twice, both times as a new version.** Draft
   v3's header records it was "produced under §6.4 as a new version, never as an
   edit of a tested baseline"; Draft v4's header records the same. That is the
   repository's own applied reading of the rule, twice, on authorized changes.
2. **Draft v4's own text forbids what the reconciliation requires.** Its header
   states: *"this text is not altered in place"* and *"**Amendment:** by a later
   version under Methodology Foundation v1 §6.4, never by silent edit."*
   Applying the reconciliation in place requires **editing the sentence that
   forbids editing it in place** — a self-authorizing move Development will not
   make on its own judgment.

The instrument forbids Draft v5. §6.4 requires a new version for a substantive
refinement. **Those two directives cannot both be satisfied under the rule as
written**, which is the stop condition exactly. Development stopped before
choosing.

## 2. The candidate representations, and their governance consequences

**Development does not choose among these and expresses no preference.** They
are set out so the determination can be made in one step.

### Option 1 — In-place amendment of Draft v4 at a new recorded digest

Draft v4 is edited at its existing path; its digest changes; the Stage 9 state
remains byte-retrievable at commit `240ccf9` and is recorded historically in the
integrity record beside the new digest.

- **Supports it:** every corpus document declares `versioning: git-history`, so
  the prior state is preserved and addressable, satisfying §6.5's rule that
  superseded material is not deleted from institutional history. Draft v4 is a
  §5.1 *draft*, carries no institutional authority, and — unlike v1, v2 and v3 —
  **has no application whose evidence depends on its bytes**. Nothing that was
  ever done with this method would be misdescribed.
- **Against it:** §6.4 says *new version*, and this is not one. It departs from
  the repository's twice-applied reading. It requires editing Draft v4's own
  freeze and amendment statements to make the edit permissible. And every Stage
  9 record citing digest `3137da8c…` would describe a state no longer at that
  path, discoverable only by commit.

### Option 2 — A new version (Draft v5)

- **Supports it:** it is what §6.4 says, and what the program did twice before.
- **Against it:** **expressly forbidden by the authorizing instrument.** It
  would also represent a one-paragraph determination-encoding as a version
  succession equal in weight to the seventeen-item Stage 7 consolidation, which
  would misdescribe the change's size in the version chain.

### Option 3 — A distinct revision identity short of a new version

A separate governed path (for example a Draft v4 revision record) carrying the
reconciled text, with Draft v4's Stage 9 text left byte-identical.

- **Supports it:** it is not Draft v5, and it leaves every Stage 9 digest
  citation true.
- **Against it:** **the repository has no point-revision convention for
  methodology drafts.** Drafts v1 through v4 are whole versions, one commit
  each. Establishing a revision tier is **new versioning governance**, which
  this instrument does not authorize, and it is arguable whether a revision
  identity satisfies §6.4's "new version" at all.

### Option 4 — A Methodology-room interpretation of §6.4's scope

A determination that §6.4's new-version requirement governs assets with
standing — approved methods, or texts whose prior state has been relied upon —
and does not bind an unapproved, unapplied draft, which would make Option 1
available without strain.

- **Supports it:** §6.4 sits under *Authority and Approval*; §5.1 classes a
  draft as non-authoritative; and Draft v4 has neither approval nor application
  behind it.
- **Against it:** §6.4 states no such limit, and reading one in is an amendment
  of a Company-wide approved foundation document. **That is a Methodology-room
  question, not a drafting one**, and not Development's to answer — per
  `AGENTS.md`, a task instruction "may not amend constitutional text or
  Company-wide strategic authority."

## 3. The prepared wording, clause by clause against the determination

What follows is the **complete** reconciliation as prepared. It is recorded here
so that the determination and its encoding can be compared by a reader who wrote
neither (`fd-0028` §4 Deficiency 2). **None of it has been applied to Draft v4.**

### 3.1 A10 — the trail-first procedure

**Draft v4 as it stands** (unchanged, and identical to Draft v3):

> **Trail-first procedure.** Work is discovered from its trail before it is
> discovered from its description: the artifacts produced, the records and
> timestamps they left, the correspondence around them, and a stakeholder
> walking the team through **the last actual instance** of the work, step by
> step, in their own words. Only then is any described or documented process
> recorded — as a separate row — and the two are compared. The team does not
> begin from a process it already knows.

**Prepared replacement:**

> **Trail-first procedure.** Work is discovered from evidence of **the last
> actual instance** before any described or documented process is accepted as
> the representation of how the work occurs. The trail is sought across four
> **source categories** — the artifacts produced; the records and timestamps
> they left; the correspondence around the work; and a stakeholder walking the
> team through the last actual instance, step by step, in their own words.
> **These are categories of trail evidence, not four limbs that must all exist
> and all be obtained before the procedure counts as performed.**
>
> **Pursued, not picked.** Every category **materially relevant** to the
> workflow is pursued where it appears to exist and is reasonably accessible
> within the authorized evidence boundary, and four things are recorded for
> each: whether it **appears to exist**; whether it is **reasonably
> accessible**; whether it was **actually obtained**; and **why it was not**,
> where it was not. A materially relevant category that appears to exist is
> never dropped because another category already produced a plausible trail.
> **Prior familiarity with the workflow is not trail evidence** (A17 rule 6)
> and satisfies none of this.
>
> **The procedure is performed when** the materially relevant trail evidence
> reasonably available within the authorized boundary has been obtained and
> examined; every unobtained, unavailable, nonexistent or inaccessible category
> is explicitly recorded; the actual-work trail is recorded **before** any
> described or documented process is accepted as the workflow representation;
> the described process is then recorded **as a separate row**; and the two are
> compared. **It may be performed with evidentiary gaps remaining.** The team
> does not begin from a process it already knows.
>
> **A missing category does not void the procedure.** It bears on **evidentiary
> sufficiency** — what findings may legitimately be made, the confidence and
> context recorded through the method's existing non-scored vocabulary,
> Instrument 5's sufficiency marker, and, through A18, possibly advancement.
> The missing category and its reason stay visible. **Silence is never read as
> agreement:** an unobtained source is never treated as though it would have
> corroborated what was obtained.
>
> **Performed is not sufficient.** That the procedure was performed establishes
> nothing about whether the workflow is sufficiently understood. **A18 decides
> that, independently** — *A10 performed, gaps remaining, A18 not satisfied* is
> a permitted and expected state.
>
> **The walkthrough is a category, not a gate.** A10 does not make a stakeholder
> walkthrough a universal per-workflow completion requirement. Where one is
> materially available and relevant to a **load-bearing** workflow it is
> obtained, or the reason it was not is recorded. Where stakeholder evidence is
> required by another provision of this method, that provision is unaffected by
> anything here.

**And one sentence added to the existing *Observation preference* paragraph**,
which is otherwise unchanged:

> It is a preference, and **not a fifth trail-source category**.

### 3.2 Instrument 5 — the sufficiency marker

**Prepared addition to Instrument 5's recording rules**, appended to the
existing rules and displacing none:

> the instrument's evidence-sufficiency marker (Part B) is set truthfully
> rather than by habit: **completed** where the materially relevant trail
> categories were obtained and no material evidentiary gap remains; **completed
> with named gaps** where the procedure was performed, one or more materially
> relevant categories were unavailable or unobtained, and the remaining trail
> still supports a meaningful workflow representation; **blocked by insufficient
> evidence** where the available trail is too incomplete to support one. **An
> artifact-derived or partial trail is not marked *completed* automatically**,
> and the marker never reports the procedure's completion in place of the
> evidence's sufficiency.

### 3.3 A20 — the limitation, rewritten as determined rather than deleted

**Prepared replacement** for the paragraph headed *An ambiguity carried forward
unresolved*:

> **An ambiguity carried forward at this version's creation, and since
> determined.** A10's trail-first procedure lists four forms of evidence and, as
> this version was constructed, **did not say whether they were conjunctive**.
> Two authors read it differently: one treated a records-only trail as
> satisfying the procedure, the other treated the walkthrough as required and
> recorded the step as unperformed. The Founder Office determined the question
> at `fd-0028`: the four are **source categories**, not a conjunctive gate, and
> A10 above is reconciled accordingly. **The divergence is preserved as
> methodology-development evidence that the wording was genuinely ambiguous
> before determination — neither author applied the method incorrectly**, and
> the determination does not make one of them retrospectively right.
