    <determination id="CS-01">
      <title>Source kind versus evidence quality</title>

      <decision>
        APPROVE a descriptive source-kind attribute.

        The source-kind attribute answers only:

        "What kind of thing supplied this claim?"

        It does not answer:

        "How good, reliable, strong, credible, or persuasive is this evidence?"
      </decision>

      <permitted_structure>
        Source-kind values must remain:
        <item>categorical;</item>
        <item>descriptive;</item>
        <item>organization-neutral;</item>
        <item>non-ordinal.</item>
      </permitted_structure>

      <minimum_capability>
        Draft v4 must be capable of distinguishing at minimum:
        <item>person / stakeholder account;</item>
        <item>organizational or institutional record;</item>
        <item>supplied institutional source;</item>
        <item>systematic verification / validation record;</item>
        <item>other source kinds genuinely required by application evidence.</item>
      </minimum_capability>

      <normalization_authority>
        Development may normalize exact labels during future Draft v4 construction provided the categories remain descriptive and non-hierarchical.
      </normalization_authority>

      <critical_boundary>
        A systematic verification record may be distinguished from a stale narrative record because they are different evidence-artifact kinds, not because one is methodologically ranked above the other.
      </critical_boundary>

      <evidence_quality>
        Evidence quality remains separate and may be expressed only through such factors as:
        <item>limitations;</item>
        <item>contradictions;</item>
        <item>provenance;</item>
        <item>freshness;</item>
        <item>coverage;</item>
        <item>contextual explanation.</item>

        Do not encode evidence quality through the source-kind field.
      </evidence_quality>

      <a2_rule>
        A2's existing non-ordering rule remains controlling.
      </a2_rule>

      <title>A1 terminology versus A2 evidence classification</title>

      <decision>
        A2 governs the diagnostic's own evidence classification.

        A1 governs the subject's terminology.
      </decision>

      <subject_taxonomy>
        Where the subject has its own:
        <item>evidence taxonomy;</item>
        <item>validation vocabulary;</item>
        <item>evidence-strength scheme;</item>
        <item>or similar apparatus;</item>

      <diagnostic_rule>

        Do not allow the subject's scheme to replace, redefine, or silently modify A2 for purposes of the diagnostic's cross-application evidence model.

      <reason>
        The diagnostic requires a stable cross-subject evidence-classification framework while still representing the subject's own evidence language accurately.

      <future_methodology_evolution>
        If a subject's evidence framework appears materially stronger, more precise, or more useful than A2, preserve that observation as evidence for later methodology evolution.

        Do not substitute that scheme during the current engagement merely because it appears superior.

      <governing_distinction>
        A1 preserves the subject's language.

        A2 preserves the diagnostic's evidence semantics.

        Neither erases the other.
      </governing_distinction>
    </determination>

    <determination id="CS-03">
      <title>Prior involvement and meaning of diagnostic team</title>

      <decision>
        "Diagnostic team" means the actual institutional or individual actor materially performing the diagnostic.
      </decision>

      <disclosure_rule>
        The method must disclose both:
        <item>the institutional relationship;</item>
        <item>the relevant human/agent relationship where they differ.</item>
      </disclosure_rule>

      <material_prior_involvement>
        Prior involvement is material when the diagnosing actor previously participated in work that could reasonably shape interpretation of the subject, including:
        <item>designing the subject's systems;</item>
        <item>constructing them;</item>
        <item>governing them;</item>
        <item>implementing them;</item>
        <item>materially evaluating or validating them;</item>
        <item>authoring substantial portions of evidence later diagnosed;</item>
        <item>repeatedly advising on the same operational structures now under diagnosis.</item>
      </material_prior_involvement>

      <non_material_exposure>
        Mere incidental familiarity, one-off exposure, or ordinary awareness does not automatically make a diagnosis non-independent.
      </non_material_exposure>

      <required_disclosure>
        Where prior involvement is material, the diagnostic must state:
        <item>who or what previously participated;</item>
        <item>in what capacity;</item>
        <item>what portion of the subject was involved;</item>
        <item>whether the current diagnosis therefore lacks independence in that area;</item>
        <item>what limitation that creates.</item>
      </required_disclosure>

      <institutional_default>
        Institutional prior involvement is the default structural reading because replacing an individual or agent does not eliminate prior involvement by the institution that designed, constructed, or governed the subject.
      </institutional_default>

      <individual_layer>
        Individual/agent authorship must also be disclosed where relevant because it may create an additional dependence layer.
      </individual_layer>

      <scope_boundary>
        This does not establish a universal independence doctrine for all Huerta Group consulting work.

        It governs disclosure within the Organizational Diagnostic.
      </scope_boundary>
    </determination>

      <title>Confidentiality doctrine dependency</title>

      <decision>
        Methodology Foundation v1 §7.5 does not travel by implication merely because the Organizational Diagnostic cites it.

      <client_facing_requirement>

        <item>state the minimum binding handling principle necessary for safe diagnostic operation;</item>
        <item>cite the governing Huerta Group confidentiality doctrine where available;</item>

        <item>require a conservative default where the governing doctrine is not held or cannot govern the engagement.</item>

        <item>turning Methodology Foundation v1 §7.5 into an invisible dependency;</item>
        <item>duplicating the full parent confidentiality doctrine.</item>
