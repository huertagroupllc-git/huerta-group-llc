# Deliverable B — Governance Mechanism Audit

**Huerta Group — Repository Foundation Project, Phase 1 · Audited at commit `4dc354e` (= origin/main) · READ ONLY — no repository modification occurred · Builds on accepted Deliverable A**

## 1. Executive Summary

The repository's governance operates as a **five-layer mechanism stack**: conversational Founder-Office task instructions → AI instruction files (CLAUDE.md/AGENTS.md) → procedural standards (Development Standards, Bounded Autonomous Completion Standard, ADR practice) → declared institutional doctrine (index hierarchy, foundations, source-supremacy rules) → technical enforcement (four strict schemas, five deterministic validators, lint/tsc/build). The text of this stack is coherent, mutually referential, and unusually disciplined; **its enforcement is inverted and trigger-dependent**. The strongest enforcement (164 distinct mechanical failure rules) protects the *lowest* tiers (metadata integrity), while the highest tiers (constitutional authority, approval events, task gating, canonical-source transmission) are enforced only by convention and conversation. **Nothing in the repository runs any check automatically** — there is no CI, no hook, no workflow; every technical mechanism fires only when a human or AI chooses to invoke it.

Material findings: 26 mechanisms inventoried (§3); one operative conflict (corpus-purity validation vs the Founder determination retaining OSPA material untracked inside `docs/`); one committed standard that hard-codes a builder model ("Claude Opus 5") and a personally named escalation authority ("Esteban") at odds with practiced Founder task configurations and the configurable-authority doctrine; three-way naming variance for the same human authority; duplicated-but-divergent completion-report and validation-gate rules; and a set of load-bearing behaviors (verbatim source transfer, approval provenance, Founder determinations themselves) that exist nowhere in the repository. Three formal escalations are raised (§14). No mechanism requires retirement; most require **Preserve** or **Clarify**.

## 2. Governance Hierarchy in Practice

**Declared hierarchy** (docs/README.md, restated in AGENTS.md): 1 Constitution → 2 Vision Foundation → 3 approved Company-wide documents → 4 Development Standards + ADRs → 5 individual task instructions, with the escalation formula "the higher authority prevails and the conflict must be escalated, not resolved silently," and the exception rule that a task may authorize exceptions to lower-level standards but never amend higher authority.

**Hierarchy in operation** (verified from mechanism behavior): execution is *initiated and shaped* at tier 5 (Founder-authored XML tasks), *routed* through the AI instruction files, *disciplined* by the tier-4 standards, and *checked* only by tier-4-adjacent validators. Tier 1 has no preserved text; tier 2's approval evidence is circular; tiers 1–3 have **zero mechanical presence** — no validator enforces anything the Constitution or Vision says. The practical apex of day-to-day governance is the conversational layer plus the AI instruction files, both of which *declare* subordination to the documented hierarchy. The declared and practiced orders are consistent in direction but inverted in enforcement strength.

## 4. Authority Analysis

Every mechanism that *claims* authority does so derivatively and says so — the standards subordinate themselves to Constitution/Vision; ADRs disclaim institutional authority; metadata disclaims authority over sources; validators enforce shape, not meaning. The result is a clean **downward-only authority flow in text**. Three verified authority observations: (1) the top of the flow is textually thin — the Constitution record contains no ratified text and the Vision's approval reference is circular, so every subordination clause points at authority whose repository evidence is a title and a listing; (2) the *operative* granting instrument for all repository work is the tier-5 Founder task plus its determinations, none of which are repository-recorded — including the six determinations recited in this task; (3) the human authority is named inconsistently — "Esteban" (bounded standard), "the owner" (ADR practice, ADRs, docs/README), "Founder Office" (current project instruments) — with no mapping artifact.

## 5. AI Execution Governance

Verified control chain for an AI contributor: tooling injects **CLAUDE.md** → imports **AGENTS.md** (hierarchy, Vision-first consultation duty, no-authority-inference, conflict-pause, keep current/planned/vision distinct) → consequential work bound by the **Bounded Standard** (loop, builder–inspector with six classifications, three-attempt stop rule, 14 protected actions, evidence rules — "linting or compilation alone does not establish completion," "never report COMPLETE while a required criterion remains failed or unverified") → **Development Standards AI section** (AI is a tool not an authority; human responsibility identifiable; no fabrication; AI output reviewed like human work) → per-task XML instruments (14 required prompt sections, observable acceptance criteria). Two ungoverned inputs also shape AI behavior: session memory (M26) and the conversational source-transfer protocol (M16). Notable: ADR-0006 already reserves a "machine/integration identity" class and bans shared human credentials for automation — the only forward-looking machine-governance text in the corpus. `machineConsumptionEligible` remains `"unresolved"` for all 46 documents, so no repository document is currently cleared for AI retrieval use — a deliberate, recorded gate.

## 6. Repository Governance Mechanisms

The repository governs itself through: the corpus rule (`docs/**/*.md` = governed, root instruction files excluded by explicit ADR-0001 decision); same-change manifest duty (stated in triplicate: ADR-0001, ADR practice, index maintenance rules); canonical-location/no-duplication; placement defaults (documents inside, runtime outside — ADR-0002/0003); pinned record admission (a new governed record mechanically requires validator-set amendment, making admission a deliberate act); and the per-domain maintenance rules in the index. These interlock correctly. The single structural weakness (Deliverable A §17, now mechanism-confirmed): the corpus rule provides **no staging semantics** — any pre-approval Markdown inside `docs/` is instantly claimed by the exact-coverage check, which is precisely the OSPA condition.

## 7. Validation and Enforcement Mechanisms

Complete verified enforcement inventory: 5 validators (19/20/29/44/52 `fail(...)` sites — 164 distinct rules: schema conformance, exact coverage both directions, uniqueness, path existence, reference/dependency/relationship resolution, cycle detection, provisional-assignment rejection, disclosure guards, authority-configurability guards, public-page-as-authority rejection, instance/offering rejection, evidence-required legal states, manifest-inclusion and capability-presence cross-checks) + 4 strict schemas + lint/tsc/build + `.gitignore`. **Enforcement typing across all 26 mechanisms: Technical 8 · Procedural 10 · Advisory 5 · Implicit 3.** Every technical mechanism is invocation-gated (M14): the enforcement chain's first link is always a human or AI *choosing* to run it, and Vercel deploys on push regardless. The gate definition itself is fragmented — no single document lists the full required pre-push suite (three commands in the standards; five validators required domain-by-domain elsewhere).

## 8. Canonical Source Governance

Mechanisms: verbatim-preservation headers ("complete approved text, preserved exactly as transmitted… Markdown presentation normalized only"); source-supremacy clauses in every derived record; pending-insertion markers for untransmitted text (Constitution — the practiced convention for absent sources); provenance-never-fabricated rule; canonical-location rule. Verified gaps: the transfer protocol that produced every preserved source is conversational (M16); approval events are unrecorded (all dates null — deferred to Deliverable C by determination); and the practiced protocol's strongest safeguard — refusal to reconstruct — is documented nowhere a future contributor would find it. The untracked OSPA material demonstrates both halves: correct behavior (not committed, per determination) and missing mechanism (nowhere sanctioned to put it).

## 9. Lifecycle and Status Governance

Vocabularies are complete, schema-enforced, and dimension-separated (M19/M20): content/record lifecycle, governance status, maturity, readiness (9–10 independent dimensions per domain), disclosure, retirement, archival — with validator-enforced no-inference guards (completion≠competency, readiness≠issuance, approval≠delivery, technical-readiness≠program-approval, etc.). ADR statuses (`Proposed→Accepted|Rejected`, `Superseded by`) and manifest statuses (`current/superseded/historical`) are defined. **Verified limitation: the entire supersession/retirement machinery has never been exercised** — no superseded, retired, or historical artifact exists, so both-direction linking, retention behavior, and validator handling of non-current states are untested in practice.

## 10. Contributor Governance

For AI contributors: comprehensive (§5). For human contributors: **no dedicated mechanism exists** — no CONTRIBUTING.md, no CODEOWNERS, no PR/issue templates, no workflows; a human contributor's governance is whatever they read in AGENTS.md (addressed to agents), the standards, and the index. Git-level contributor controls are absent locally (branch protection is platform-side and not verifiable from repository evidence); committer identity has been misconfigured for 26 of 27 commits, and the bounded standard's required commit trailers are a conversational convention, not a repository-recorded rule.

## 14. Formal Escalations

**ESC-B1 — Unrecorded reclassification of the documentation index.**
*Issue:* The Founder determination classifying `docs/README.md` as a Repository Standard is not reflected anywhere in the repository; manifest metadata still records `Unspecified`/`unspecified`, and ADR-0001 flags the gap as awaiting owner decision. *Evidence:* `knowledge/manifest.json` documentation-index record; ADR-0001 authority notes; this task's determination text. *Governing sources involved:* ADR-0001, docs/README.md, knowledge manifest, Founder determination. *Why P&SD cannot resolve:* this execution is read-only; more fundamentally, reclassification is an institutional metadata act that ADR-0001 reserves to the governing hierarchy, and the determination did not authorize its implementation. *Options:* (a) authorize a bounded metadata change now; (b) fold into Deliverable D migration; (c) record the determination in a to-be-created determinations register first. *Recommended disposition:* Clarify — (b), with the determination text preserved verbatim when recorded. *Exact determination required:* "The docs/README.md manifest record shall be updated to classification 'Repository Standard' [with stated authority tier] during [now / migration], citing this determination as its approval reference."

**ESC-B2 — Committed standard pins a builder model and a personal escalation authority.**
*Issue:* The Bounded Autonomous Completion & Verification Standard mandates "Builder model: Claude Opus 5" and names "Esteban" as escalation authority; Founder task instruments routinely specify other models (this task: "Claude Opus 4.1 / Claude Sonnet 4"), and current instruments route authority through "Founder Office," while foundation doctrine requires authority representations to remain configurable and non-person-bound. *Evidence:* bounded standard §Required Model Configuration and §Clarification Policy (quoted in audit evidence); this task's `<model>` block and `<authority>` block; registry `configurable: true` constants. *Governing sources involved:* bounded standard, Development Standards, MFv1/IPFv1/EWDFv1 authority principles, Founder task instruments. *Why P&SD cannot resolve:* revising an approved company standard's intended policy (which model, whose authority, under what name) is an institutional preference P&SD cannot self-determine; the tier-5 exception rule covers per-task overrides but not the standing text. *Options:* (a) amend the standard to reference "the current Founder-approved model configuration" and "the Founder Office (currently exercised by the owner)"; (b) leave text, record a standing determination that task instruments supersede it; (c) defer wording to Deliverable C's governance-consolidation proposal. *Recommended disposition:* Clarify via (a) or (c). *Exact determination required:* "The bounded standard's model-configuration and escalation-authority clauses shall be [amended now / superseded by standing determination / revised in Deliverable C] to [approved wording]."

**ESC-B3 — Corpus purity vs untracked-retention determination.**
*Issue:* The determination that OSPA reconstruction material "remain untracked working material and shall not be committed" leaves it physically inside `docs/`, where ADR-0001's exact-coverage corpus rule makes `validate:knowledge` fail on any working tree containing it; the repository has no sanctioned staging location. *Evidence:* validator output "coverage: governed document missing from manifest: docs/operating systems & architecture foundation/OSPA-FND-V1-S10.md"; ADR-0001 corpus rule; Founder determination. *Governing sources involved:* ADR-0001, knowledge validator, Founder determinations. *Why P&SD cannot resolve:* relocating Founder working material was not authorized (read-only execution; the determination is silent on location), and defining a staging convention is a repository-architecture matter reserved to Deliverable C with Founder approval. *Options:* (a) determine an interim location outside `docs/` (e.g., an untracked, ignored top-level working directory) and authorize the move as a non-repository file operation; (b) accept standing working-tree validation failure until migration; (c) amend the corpus rule (Deliverable C scope). *Recommended disposition:* Founder Office Determination Required — (a) interim, (c) structural. *Exact determination required:* "OSPA working material shall reside at [location outside docs/] until migration; the Deliverable C architecture shall define the permanent staging convention."

## 15. Responses to Required Questions

**Q1 — Effective hierarchy in practice:** Founder-Office conversational instruments (initiate, configure, gate) → CLAUDE.md/AGENTS.md (frame every session) → bounded standard + Development Standards (discipline execution) → index hierarchy + foundation source-supremacy (interpret authority) → schemas/registries/validators (mechanically enforce structure). Text says top-down; enforcement runs bottom-up.

**Q2 — Divergence from the claimed hierarchy:** direction matches; substance diverges at the top (tier 1 has no preserved text, tier 2's approval is circular, tiers 1–3 have no mechanical presence) and at the entry point (the practical apex — task instruments, determinations, agent memory — is not repository-recorded at all).

**Q3 — What controls AI contributors:** CLAUDE.md → AGENTS.md → bounded standard (loop, inspector classifications, protected actions, stop conditions, evidence and honest-reporting rules) → Development Standards AI section → per-task XML instruments → plus two ungoverned inputs: session memory and the conversational source-transfer protocol.

**Q4 — Existing AI instructions vs current Founder direction:** yes, three: the model pin ("Claude Opus 5") contradicted by every recent Founder instrument; the personally named escalation authority vs Founder-Office routing; and the standard's default commit-and-push completion posture vs this project's read-only phase mode (reconciled per-task by the tier-5 exception rule, but the standing default invites error). Additionally, AGENTS/manifest will keep presenting the index's old `Unspecified` classification to every future session until D-1 lands.

**Q5 — Must survive migration unchanged:** source-supremacy and metadata≠authority doctrine; verbatim-preservation discipline; the hierarchy-with-escalation formula ("higher authority prevails… escalated, not resolved silently"); configurable-authority representation; separated-dimension/no-inference architecture; exact-coverage validation principle; canonical-location/no-duplication; historical-preservation/no-deletion; the six Accepted ADRs' operative criteria (placement, isolation threshold, permissions≠authority); the protected-actions and evidence/honest-reporting rules.

**Q6 — Require determination before migration:** D-1 through D-8 (§13), most acutely D-3 (staging), D-4 (enforcement automation), D-5 (source-transfer codification), and D-8 (corpus scope of the AI instruction files).

**Q7 — Redundant / ambiguous / weakly enforced / conversation-dependent:** redundant: manifest-duty triplication, completion-report duality, hierarchy restatement; ambiguous: gate timing, "consequential"/trigger terms, ADR tier hard-coding; weakly enforced: everything advisory in §3 plus all technical mechanisms by virtue of M14; conversation-dependent: source-transfer protocol, approval events, Founder determinations, task gating, agent memory, commit-trailer convention.

**Q8 — Gaps that would materially impair future repository-based AI work:** no automated enforcement (an AI that forgets to validate ships unvalidated); no repository record of determinations or approvals (future sessions cannot verify authority without conversation history — the exact failure mode OSPA now evidences); `machineConsumptionEligible` unresolved corpus-wide (blocks any sanctioned AI retrieval layer); no staging semantics (AI work-in-progress has nowhere lawful to live); no codified source-transfer protocol (a future agent could reconstruct where this lineage refused); memory-resident operative rules (non-portable across agents/machines); no human-contributor governance if a person ever joins.

## 16. Overall Audit Determination

The governance mechanism stack is **textually coherent, doctrinally consistent, and mechanically excellent where it is mechanical — but it is a voluntary system**: its strongest guarantees fire only when invoked, its highest authorities are its least evidenced, and its most critical behaviors live outside the repository in conversation and agent memory. No mechanism warrants retirement; the stack is fit to carry migration **after** the eight determinations in §13 — chiefly staging semantics, enforcement automation, source-transfer codification, and determination recording — are made by the Founder Office and handed to Deliverable C as requirements rather than discoveries.
